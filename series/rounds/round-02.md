# THE BLACKFLAMES — Round 2 Rating (Chief Assurance Rater)

**Scope.** Book I, *The Fragment Bearer*, Part One complete: front matter, Prologue and Chapters 1–11 (13 units, 70,212 words). The five new chapters (7–11) were read closely in their current files, and so were the three revisions (Ch.2, 5, 6). The rest were re-skimmed for the round's single-line fixes (Ch.3 l.73, Ch.4 l.243) and checked against FACTS.md, PLAN.md, the magic system, the mystery ledger and the ideas register as they now stand.

As in Round 1, this rating is independent of the Assurance verdicts. Where a repair fixed a flagged problem, the fix counts. Where a verdict's problem is still in the file, it counts. Where the canon sheet and the prose now disagree, the disagreement counts against continuity even if the prose itself is right.

**Standard.** 10 means a masterpiece ready to publish. 8 means professional, publishable work with visible seams. 9 means work a demanding critic would call exceptional.

### Overall (weighted judgement): **8.5 / 10** (Round 1: 7.9; +0.6)

**Why 8.5 and not higher.** The prose and the set pieces are now at the level of the best of the genre. Ch.11 is the first chapter I would call 9-grade work, and Ch.10's Gloss and Ch.9's two columns are original. Three things hold the series below 9:

1. **Process debt.** The canon sheet that was created to stop drift is drifting itself. One chapter (Ch.7) went through the round with problems that were flagged and never repaired.
2. **The style tics are logged but not cured.** The simile budget has been called "executed" twice and is still over in Ch.5 and Ch.8.
3. **The promises are still unpaid.** Part One ends on a superb cliff, but the book's large debts (Amara's price, Angeska, the strip's verb, Fragment 0) are all still open. That is correct at this point in the book, but it means the reader's trust is now carried by execution alone.

**Why not lower.** Part One works as a novel's first act. It has a clear underdog against competent, terrifying power. It has found family under strain (the kolo, *kubet*, the laugh). Its magic is rare, visual and procedural, and its climax pays off chapter 1's candle in a way a demanding reader will remember.

## 4. Top 10 weaknesses holding the rating below 10

1. **FACTS.md has become a source of drift.** Five specific problems:
   - §8 contradicts the repaired Ch.10 on four points: "Fourth day of bread" (text: third), "First light, Mon 3 Mar, in the rain" (no rain in the text), "upright and apart" (the text and the Prologue: leaning, branching), and "from the fourth sign on" (text: after the roof row; warm before the knotted cord).
   - There is **no Ch.8 or Ch.9 section**. The litany names, the informant bargain, the safe-conduct, the purse, the tally, the cage's final position and Matteo's particulars are canon only in prose.
   - There are two sections numbered §9, out of order.
   - The master chronology's "autumn 1325: the embassy leaves Lalibela" contradicts "five months on the road" (Ch.2 l.113) and Giyorgis's "before the feast of the Cross" (Ch.4).
   - The Ch.6 "Venetian letters" entry contradicts that chapter's own roster of searchers.

   Every repair this round was briefed against FACTS, so a stale FACTS row will produce the next break.
2. **Ch.7 went through the round unrepaired with known faults.**
   - The calendar compression contradicts FACTS and Ch.8's "thirteen days".
   - "Thirty years ago… lips split" (l.253) moves the 1325 Nubian crossing to c.1295.
   - "Eleven days" (×2) should be twelve or thirteen.
   - The narration explains Tamrat's belly at l.315 ("his belly goes toward the board…"), the one thing the brief forbids for Tamrat.
   - Bishoy knew Dimiana's door and did not take them there for eleven days.
   - Tamrat's "second Sunday of the fast" should be the third by Hudade reckoning.

   A PASS verdict did not mean the problems went away.
3. **The simile and figure budget is logged as done and is not done.** Ch.5 has about 9 'as a / the way a' constructions against about 3.4, and Ch.8 about 6 against about 4. Revisers convert similes into "with the care/attention a man gives X" or appositive metaphors from outside the POV's field (Ch.2 'bill of sale' and 'child's toy'; Ch.6 'plumb line'). The echo log is incomplete in every chapter section. Recurring units are wearing thin: *barleycorn* (scar, ice, the flame's blue), the archive-smell triad (Prologue, Ch.9, Ch.10), the open hand of Desta (Ch.9 close, Ch.11 l.333), and doors (Ch.6, three figurative doors around the protected one).
4. **Tamrat's sententiousness.** Ch.7 has five maxims in his head. "A man does not name what he has not let into his house" closes a scene on an aphorism. "He was a man who noticed a room" and "He was a man who remembered colours" are the same construction twice. "That was Rome: …" is a third. "What a man takes without a blessing, he has no right to keep" is excellent and should be the chapter's only one. Giovanni (Ch.8) and Humbert (Ch.6) drift the same way ("Justice is whatever the Church decrees", "a better accountant than any man").
5. **Angeska's tell ration is honoured in plan and broken at line level.**
   - Ch.8 alone adds the eyes going down "as a clerk's go to a sum", her own question about bread, her knowledge of the loaf count, a note folded in advance in Latin better than his, and "your Sicilian", whose source is unseen.
   - Ch.7's Avignon glance is blunted because Ch.5's guard already pointed the way.
   - The ledger rule ("one new tell per Angeska-adjacent chapter") needs a line-level definition: the one is named in the brief, and everything else must be characterisation that does not point toward Avignon.
6. **The magic's geometry is still left to writers.** The Ch.10 doorway was fixed in repair, but Ch.7's aborted Lean has no distance cue: two steps toward a seated widow under a bracket lamp, and a tip that "holds… listening" for three breaths, which reads as two stages of a full Lean. Readers assemble the hand's-breadth rule from exactly these scenes. A related ruling is still owed: does unpicking alone uncover Fragment 0, or only reading aloud (Ch.10 implies only reading)?
7. **The costs the Round 1 rating called "a debt" are ruled but not yet paid on the page.**
   - Amara's price is Talka's sister Elleni and the restarted Psalm 101:10. Nothing in Ch.1–11 shows it, and Ch.4 (Talka's POV) passed without it.
   - Zoser's first-memory loss ("who taught him to write") is now beautifully set up by the dust memory and the second አ, but it is not scheduled in any Part Two row I can find as a felt loss.
   - The excommunication takes (good), but its first social cost to Zoser is still only an ink bead.

   Part Two must start paying.
8. **Smell and the senses drop out in document scenes.** Ch.7 (the Needle, the cat and the river tongue, the diptych, the reading of the record), parts of Ch.8's collation and the Ch.9 table sections give the framed document and drop the room. The style guide's "smell in every scene" now loses most often exactly where the formal innovation is, and that is where it is most needed to keep the documents physical.
9. **Length creep and Part One pacing.** Ch.7 (6,912), Ch.8 (6,559), Ch.10 (6,973) and Ch.11 (7,446) are all at or over the top of their bands. Part One runs to 70k words, about 42% of the planned 165k, but covers 11 of 33 chapters. If Parts Two to Four run at this density, Book I will reach about 210k. Ch.7's inn sequence and Ch.8's dole and Humbert exposition (ll.37–39, l.69) are the nearest things to slack in Part One.
10. **Small plausibility gaps that a careful reader will catch.**
    - The Ch.5 ford is knee-deep for Angeska and chest-deep for Zoser, with no middle channel.
    - Ch.6 has Humbert reading the order's reports "for thirty years" from age 14.
    - Ch.8's hall: do the friars eat there or not?
    - Ch.8's 'cage-stand' against the hook in Ch.9 and Ch.11.

    Each is small. Together they are the difference between 'professional' and 'impeccable'.
