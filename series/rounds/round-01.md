# THE BLACKFLAMES — Round 1 Rating (Chief Assurance Rater)

**Scope.** Book I, *The Fragment Bearer*: front matter, Prologue, Chapters 1–6 (the first eight units in reading order). Every chapter was read closely in its current, post-repair state. Then each was re-checked against the PLAN, bible, magic system, mystery ledger, ideas register and its sibling chapters. The Assurance verdicts were weighed, but this rating is independent: where a later repair fixed a flagged problem, the fix counts; where a repair to one chapter reopened a break in another, the break counts.

**Standard.** 10 means a masterpiece ready to publish. A score of 8 means professional, publishable work with visible seams.

### Overall (weighted judgement): **7.9 / 10**

The text is professional work, the prose and world are near the top of the genre, and the opening forty pages are excellent. It is held below 8.5 by system discipline, not talent: two live continuity breaks, a dread device without an operating rule, a magic cost that has not yet been shown, and a central mystery (Angeska) that is being given away too early. It is held below 9 by modest formal ambition, and by the fact that nothing has paid off yet. A reader has been promised a great deal and repaid with only the first micro-payoffs.

## 4. Top 10 weaknesses holding the rating below 10

1. **Two live continuity breaks, and a repair process that reopens them.** Humbert's signet is on the forefinger in Ch.1 (l.37, 57) and Ch.3 (l.191), but on the third finger in Ch.2 (l.81). The imam's dinner is in Cairo in Ch.1 (l.223) and Ch.2 (l.111), but in Alexandria in Ch.3 (l.73). Both were "fixed" in opposite directions in parallel repairs. **Ruling recommended:** forefinger, and Cairo. Change Ch.2 l.81 and Ch.3 l.73, and record both in a canon-facts sheet.
2. **The Lean has no operating rule.** Ch.1 needs a hand's breadth and a watching hunter. Tamrat then sits for hours near the hold lamp (Ch.3), the imam's lamps and the khan's ring of lamps (Ch.2), and a Cassino guest-hall lamp "straight as a reed" (Ch.5), and nothing leans. Binding device 1 ("no flame may flicker for weather") is also contradicted on purpose by the jeli scene. Readers will ask, and Ch.10–11 depend on the answer.
3. **The first black fire has no price on the page.** Magic-system §2/§13 says only someone who remembers can light the black flame, and the lighter pays in memories. The Prologue gives one deniable sign (Talka's restarted verse, l.107), and Ch.4 shows no cost. This is a debt the book has already taken on. The warm scrip at the ford (Ch.5 l.243), the jeli's still lamps (Ch.2 l.227) and the dying woman's hot feet are three more phenomena with no ruling.
4. **Ch.6 was never repaired.** The knowledge leak (l.193), the unmotivated delay over the report, the l.161 formatting glitch and the ambiguous "markets of Cairo" are all live. Ch.6 is the reader's first look at the antagonist's mind, and close-third honesty (style guide §1.4) is broken there.
5. **Angeska is being given away.** Her tells so far: in Ch.1 she watches the hands, the wave does not drag her, and her look is "quite unafraid". In Ch.2, "Then it is him" and the packet. In Ch.3, the fluent Latin and the laugh with the mate. In Ch.5, the stone bridges and "She did not look surprised". In Ch.6, the woman's scent on the A. letter. That is about ten tells for a confession scheduled at I·27, and a careful reader will have solved ledger #2 by Ch.6. The clues need rationing, and some need to point two ways.
6. **Dread flattens after Ch.1.** The Prologue and Ch.1 build a vocabulary of terror (the name re-forming, the bowing flame). Ch.2–6 each carry at most one muted beat (still lamps, the wave on the palm, the warm scrip, unleaning Candlemas candles). These are quiet, but they do not escalate. The binding devices forbid flicker but supply no between-Leans dread engine.
7. **Formal innovation is modest.** The craft brief asks for archives, bulls, ledgers and testimonies. So far there are a 150-word catalogue entry, a one-line toll ledger, a three-line letter and a ten-line report. All are good, but none takes a structural risk. No chapter is yet built *as* a document, and the Prologue has only its bracketed transcription.
8. **A prose tic is forming.** "As a man [verb]s…" and "the way a [noun] [verb]s…" similes recur heavily: Ch.6 has nine "as a…" constructions, Ch.3 eight and Ch.2 six. Echoes like "back as straight as a processional cross" (Ch.2, Ch.5) are used without logging. Individually they are fine; together they become a mannerism that a line editor would flag.
9. **Pacing seams in the interior chapters.** Ch.3 is a single room and a single night with no outside intruder until the last page. Ch.1's flashback and "three roads" reasoning sit where the clock should tighten. Ch.6 spends about 40% of its length on curial combat and the mirror before the inciting document is opened.
10. **Small tellings and sermon shapes.** The Ark paragraph in Ch.4 (l.283) states the thesis. The Keeper *announces* her spiral instead of showing it. In Ch.4 l.243 and Ch.6 l.161 two speeches by one speaker run together on one line. The Ch.2 captain spits casually (l.85), which uses up the gesture before Ch.1's naming rite is shown.

## 5. FEEDBACK FOR THE IDEAS AGENTS (Round 2)

### A. Fix first (system discipline; these cost little and raise the rating most)

1. **Create `books/book-01/FACTS.md`, a canon-facts sheet** that the continuity agent maintains after every chapter and every repair. It should hold: the signet (**forefinger**, flat-faced, key upright in a circle); the imam's table (**Cairo**); the ship's rig (two masts: mainmast and foremast); who speaks which language to whom, and what Tamrat believes about Angeska's Latin ("market words", and in Ch.3 he learns otherwise); the packet's wrapping (plain wax, saffron silk inside the oilcloth); the year arithmetic (1310 → winter 1325/26 = fifteen years); the friars (six, half dead inside a season, the tall coughing one dies in Megabit); the abbot and Abba Talka as separate people; the date of each chapter. **Repair briefs must be checked against FACTS.md at the moment of repair**, not against a stale fix list.
2. **Rule on the Lean's operating clause** in magic-system.md before Ch.10 is briefed. Recommended rule: ordinary flame leans only within about a hand's breadth of an unhidden fragment, *or* when a hunter holds the flame and attends to it. A swallowed fragment reads only at that range. Then make Tamrat's habit of sitting away from lamps (already on the page in Ch.3 l.23 and l.163) a visible, cumulative tell that Angeska manages.
3. **Amend binding device 1** from "no flame may flicker for weather" to: *no flame may lean, tip and hold, or turn blue except by the Lean; wind makes flames shrink, smoke and die.* Then **register the jeli's stillness** as a second, counter-grammar: flames stand still under naming and remembrance, and bend toward seeking. That pairs with device 7 (counting against naming) and gives Books II–VII a two-sided flame grammar.
4. **Rule on the price of Amara's burning.** Who lit it (Talka's lamp), who paid (Talka, in memory), and what he lost. The restarted verse (Prologue l.107) is the deniable first sight. Pay it off in Ch.12 or Ch.16 with something Talka cannot account for from that night, and never explain it.
5. **Repair Ch.6** (knowledge leak, motive for the delay, l.161, "markets of Cairo") and do the single-line fixes in Ch.2 l.81, Ch.3 l.73 and Ch.4 l.243 before Ch.7 is written, so later writers inherit one version.

### B. Build on (what is working; extend it on purpose)

1. **The yellow rind** (Prologue). Black flame on ordinary fuel keeps a stubborn yellow edge: "black where she is, ordinary where she is not". Make this the visual rule for every non-acacia black burning (Tamrat's pyre in I·17, Gebre in II, Bois Caïman in V), so that the midpoint revelation that only Ark acacia burns completely black lands as a fair surprise.
2. **The archive smell of black smoke** (Prologue l.123: cold dust, old hide, ink gone brown, and the sea under it). Fix it in magic-system.md as the black flame's scent signature, so later chapters can trigger it in one phrase.
3. **The two unread witness lines** on the ceiling (Prologue l.169). Ch.16 and Ch.30 should reveal one as Talka's and one as the boy's. Talka's should record the restarted verse, his price, written by the fire itself.
4. **The horn-handled bread knife** (Prologue, Ch.4 l.73). Talka should scrape with it in Ch.16, and take it up and put it down in Ch.30.
5. **Angeska's translation rule:** "Answers she softened; questions she let him feel." It should drive the Ch.22 two-column chapter, and she should break it exactly once, at the Rite.
6. **The nightly-word mechanic** (Ch.5: "Not a new one until you hold this one"). Adopt it as canon in bible §10.1. It explains why seven words span a year. *Ostium* in Ch.7 should come without the book, and the reader should feel a lock turn.
7. **The kolo.** It began as Desta's handful, became the counting game, then the uncounted handful Zoser tips into Tamrat's hand (Ch.3), then three grains a night with the rest kept for *Fasika* (Ch.5). Plan the Fasika night (I·13 or I·14) as its payoff: Zoser opens the pouch with the man who would not take it from him.
8. **Humbert's "Keep me a place" / "You'll let me in"** and his unspoken birth name. Make the name the one word the Epilogue's mask scrapes out, or the word Zoser finally says in II ("Have mercy on your old teacher"), and so join R1-psych-06 to counting against naming.
9. **The tollman's *MAVRI III*.** Counting as erasure, in a single line. Give every road or transit chapter one framed micro-document (a ledger line, a psalter margin, a customs tally). It is cheap formal innovation and it cumulates.
10. **Tasfa Mikael and the lie God made true.** "The embassy is in Rome" becomes true a few weeks later. In Ch.23 he should return to the war camp with the holy water still on his forehead, and the lie should cost Talka something concrete.

### C. Deepen (what is promising but thin)

1. **Ration Angeska's tells.** Cut or ambiguate at least two of the existing ones. Make the "quite unafraid" look in Ch.1 readable as shock. Move the woman's scent off the A. letter and onto something the reader cannot yet tie to her. From Ch.7 to Ch.19 allow **at most one new tell per Angeska-adjacent chapter**, and make each one point two ways (toward Angeska *and* toward a plausible alternative A., such as a Genoese factor's wife or the Alexandria Jew in Humbert's pay).
2. **A between-Leans dread engine.** Give each chapter that has no scheduled Lean one controlled, deniable, escalating sign that comes from the body or from memory, not from flame: the warm scrip somewhere it should not be; the palm; a word Zoser has lost; a register that misspells him (as a precursor to the excommunication's failing records from Ch.11).
3. **The first-memory loss.** The book's magic table says Book I costs Zoser "who taught him to write". Ch.5 has seeded it beautifully (the finger in the dust, the አ, "Zoser had never asked himself where he learned it"). Name the exact chapter where it goes, so the reader feels the loss against Benevento.
4. **Constantinople (R1-psych-03).** Public tellings stay vague; private rehearsals bend toward the bronze door and the worn step. Add one more private beat before Ch.14 (in Ch.7 or Ch.10), and let the Latin word *Filioque* become a second wound alongside "wet clay".
5. **Desta's count (thirty-nine).** It has now appeared in Ch.2 and in Giyorgis's sea-count in Ch.4. Use it **at most once more** before Ch.13's "you will not be my forty" and Ch.29, so it does not wear out.
6. **Formal risk.** Pick one Book I chapter, ideally Ch.21 (Humbert, "seven, no more, no fewer") or Ch.24 (Giovanni reading the honest dossier), and build it *as* a document: a ledger with marginalia, or a dossier with the reader's annotations. This is the cheapest route to a 9 in Structure.

### D. Plan-level rulings the Showrunner owes the writers

- The date line for a chapter that crosses a month (Ch.5 ends on 5 Feb under a "January" head). Recommend: *Naples, and the road north — January to Ash Wednesday, 1326*.
- The Keeper's K-entry format, as a style-guide patch: `## FROM THE KEEPER'S CATALOGUE`, an italic Ge'ez-numeral date line, one italic paragraph, "— B.", and the spiral. Decide whether the spiral is shown as a glyph or only described, and use the same choice every time. Prefer showing it. Pay off or register the "three measures" plant against ledger #31: the masked men in I·32 measure the corpse "as one measures a skin" and get inconsistent answers.
- How much "this is skin" signal each book's front matter is allowed. Book I should unsettle, not convince.
- Whether Humbert reads the A. letter in Book I, and when.
- Spitting is reserved in Book I for the order's naming rite. Change the captain's spit in Ch.2 l.85 to another gesture.
