This rating is independent of the Assurance verdicts. Where a repair landed, it counts. Where a verdict's problem is still in the file, it counts, whether or not the chapter "passed". Where the canon sheet and the prose disagree, the disagreement counts against continuity.

**Standard.** 10 means a masterpiece ready to publish. 9 means work a demanding critic would call exceptional. 8 means professional, publishable work with visible seams.

### Overall (weighted judgement): **8.8 / 10** (Round 2: 8.5; +0.3)

**Why 8.8.** This is the round in which the book paid its debts. Amara's price is on the page twice, and Elleni is felt as an absence. Desta's death is reread correctly. Tamrat confesses, the Key changes hands, and its first Lean happens with a checkable distance. Three of the five new chapters (14, 15, 16) are at or near 9-grade work, and the midpoint is the strongest stretch of the series. The repair system worked on prose: every repaired chapter came back better and kept its protected beats.

**Why not higher.** Three things hold the series below 9:
1. **The canon sheet still lags the page.** Repairs fix prose and leave stale FACTS rows behind (Ch.5's image log, Ch.7's heart-height and stool rows), and unrepaired chapters (12, 13) went through with live contradictions. Every one of these will be briefed against.
2. **Parallel drafting creates object drift.** The tallow colour, the Key's size and the deacon simile all come from Ch.12–16 being written side by side without a shared object sheet.
3. **The figure budget is still policed by censuses that miss forms.** A Round 3 pass that was specifically about similes still left one ("soft as dung") and logged a wrong count.

**Why not lower.** At its midpoint Book I is a novel a demanding reader would keep reading for the prose alone. It also now has what Round 2 said it lacked: costs paid, a reveal earned by fair clues, and a POV relationship (Zoser and Tamrat) broken and remade within one night.
