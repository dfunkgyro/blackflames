# THE BLACKFLAMES — Magic System: *The Word and the Wound*

Binding for all writers. Its sources are the bible (§2, §6), Proposal C's laws and visuals, Proposal A's costs, and Proposal B's final law, as synthesised in `series-outline.md`. Line references (`l.`) are to `blackflames.md`.

**Governing principle.** Magic is rare, dreadful, costly and half-understood. The characters never hold this document. They hold rumours, scripture, ledgers and scars. Everything below is the truth the writers know. Each book shows the reader only the slice listed in §12.

**On-page rule.** No law below is ever stated as an infodump. Readers learn a rule only when it costs someone something in a scene.

## 1. The Eight Laws

| # | Law | Grounding | On the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Word writes the world.** Text in the warden-script, text written with Ark wood, and text witnessed by a Rite all bind what they name. | ll.548, 1069, 1430, 2255, 2998 | Bulls, deeds, charters and translations are spell-documents. What they declare begins to come true in the bodies they name. |
| 2 | **What you swallow, you carry; what carries you takes your time.** | ll.620, 1057, 1716, 1730 | Ingesting a fragment or the Entity's substance makes a vessel. The vessel stops ageing and loses time. |
| 3 | **There is one fire, and it reads two ways.** It burns **black** in one who remembers and **white** in one who denies or commands. | ll.47, 977, 1035–1041; resolves bible OPEN-18 | Melted in Angeska's brazier under Rome's purpose, the Key goes gold, red, then **white**. In a remembering hand the same wood burns black. |
| 4 | **Erasure feeds the Wound; remembrance starves it.** | ll.3732–3752, 2767, 3526, 3146 | Every renaming, burned library and unrecorded deportation is a meal. Every testimony takes something away. |
| 5 | **Every division begets its negation.** | ll.4533, 4778, 4788 | The negation is born out of the Entity's own diet: the names of the chained, chanted in a hold. |
| 6 | **Names carry weight.** | ll.1371, 2415, 5736 | Renaming a person is a small white-flame act. Speaking a true name in public is a small black one. |
| 7 | **Compassion repels; conviction wards.** | ll.1596, 4584 | The Entity cannot hold a host against kindness (Bellini) and cannot touch someone of total purpose (Nanny). Both are protection, never weapons. |
| 8 | **Nothing is unwritten without fire, and every erasure leaves a scar.** | ll.2765–2767 | Erased facts leave contradictions in the record: the **Curator's Scars**. The manuscript's own continuity errors become designed, solvable clues. |

**The final law (withheld until Book VII).** The two flames are the fire on the mountain that burned and was not consumed. It **remembers when it is witnessed with love and erases when it is wielded as decree**. The Entity lives in the space between the two: the Wound (l.1526), filled with everything white fire has taken.

## 2. The two fires: visuals, effects, costs, limits

| | **Black flame** (*Lux Obscura*, "dark light") | **White flame** (the Order's purifying fire) |
|---|---|---|
| **Look** | A flame of negative space, like a tongue of darkness cut out of the air, with a violet-black rim. Frost forms on its edges. Candles nearby lean *toward* it, turn blue and go out. Its smoke **falls before it rises**, then climbs and **writes** on ceilings and sails. It makes no sound. | Bright, smokeless, white-gold, with a faint hum like a struck glass. **Things lit by it cast no shadow.** |
| **Heat** | Cold. It can be held a moment by one who remembers. | Furnace-hot. Lead runs. |
| **Ash** | Grey ash that **settles into script**: the last true text of whatever burned (names, faces, sentences, a street plan). Legible forever to anyone who can read the warden-script, and in time to anyone at all (§12). | Fine white ash like salt or flour. It holds nothing, and the burned thing leaves no smell. |
| **Effect** | **Records.** It can also *seal* a text so that it cannot be forged or copied (the crescent tokens, l.3322). Writing made by it cannot be scraped off: the letters re-form under the knife. **Black-flame text about a living person is never finished.** It keeps writing for as long as that person lives: new words appear on Gebre's palm, and the Bet Maryam ceiling rearranges on the night of the Rite. | **Erases.** Witnesses within a few paces lose the memory of what burned. Archives "forget" the entries. A man burned white becomes "a heretic", with no name in any register. |
| **Feeds** | Starves the Entity. | Feeds the Entity, even when the wielder believes he serves God. |
| **Who can light it** | Only someone who **remembers who they are**. | Anyone who holds authority's seal (writ, bull, charter) and **commands**. |
| **Cost** | **The lighter's own memories**, most recent tender ones first, unchosen. Lit **collectively**, the cost spreads thin: found family is literally the cheapest way to burn black. If a person burns their **entire name**, they write themselves out of time: unseen, undying, unrecorded (Zaynab, 1492). | **The wielder's shadow and conscience.** Practised inquisitors slowly lose their shadows. Torquemada is "what's left of him" (l.3664); Diego has his "hollow ache". The wielder also becomes **unrememberable**: in the end their own mothers forget their faces. |
| **Limit** | It burns *completely* only on Ark acacia; on ordinary fuel it burns black only at the edges or core (Tamrat's pyre). It cannot be lit by the forgetful, which is why Zoser, who loses time, fails for centuries. | It cannot touch what black fire has already sealed. It can be fooled by a forgery it believes. |
| **Clever exploitation** | **The ledger technique** (Isabella, 1612): before a burn, write down the memory the fire will take, and read it back afterwards. The cipher ledger is literally a memory bank, and "*Cuida nuestra historia*" is an operating instruction. | **The shadow test**: the resisters spot Order agents by holding a lamp and watching for a missing shadow. The Order counters by carrying two lamps, so that the false second shadow passes. |

**The Key's melt.** In Angeska's brazier the Key goes gold, then red, then white, under the Rite's purpose. A remembering bearer would have burned it black. This is the first planted proof of the final law.

**Taste: salt is memory [R1-myth-02].** Black-flame ash **tastes of salt**, like tears or the Red Sea, because it holds what it burned. White-flame ash tastes of **nothing at all**: the mouth is filled with absence. The rule is never stated; it is only tasted (Gebre's lip in the Prologue; the white ash and the black sill in I·18; one heartbeat of salt from the black candle in I·33). Zoser loses taste at the Rite; when the 1793 half-transfer "gives back salt" (V), what it gives back is the ability to taste memory.

**The price of Amara's burning [R2-dread-06, R2-myth-03, ROUND 2 RULING].** Talka lit it from his own lamp, so Talka paid, in the currency the cost row names: his most recent tender memory, unchosen. It was his younger sister **Elleni**, dead in the famine spring of 1310, who used to walk up to the monastery gate with bread for him. He no longer knows she existed. The visible edge of the loss is the restarted verse (Prologue l.107): he was singing Psalm 101:10 in the Ge'ez numbering (102:9 Hebrew), "For I have eaten ashes like bread, and mingled my drink with weeping", and lost his place when the bread-bringer went out of him. The fire wrote it down: his witness line (I·16) gives the verse's first words twice, then "…ashes like bread…", then *Talka, who gave his lamp, and with it Elleni.* Shown in I·12 (the guardian asks after her), I·16, I·30. **Never explained.** It is also the earliest proof of the found-family cost rule: he burned alone and paid in full.

**Warmth and smell [R2-myth-04, ROUND 2 RULING].** Never stated. (1) **Warmth.** Ark acacia and black-fire text **warm toward a death that will be remembered**, and go **cold** in the hands of the Entity-touched. Amara's feet were "hotter than anything living" because the skin-record on her shins was warming toward the fire that would read it (Prologue). The scrip warmed at the Cassino ford (I·5) because the man on Zoser's back, carrying the Key, was close to drowning. The Key in a living host is "warm as a second heart". The strip warms under Zoser's thumb as he reads the Line (I·10); the Key is warmest at "Let me buy you time" (I·15); the scrip goes warm a minute before Tamrat speaks his name into the fire (I·18); the strip "burns cold" in Humbert's fingers (I·32). (2) **Smell.** The black flame's signature is **"the treasury chest"**: cold dust, old hide, ink gone brown, the sea under it (Prologue l.123). One phrase triggers it, sparingly (I·9 on Tamrat's waking breath; I·10 faintly from the unpicked lining; I·18 the black core's ash; I·30; I·33). White fire's signature is **a hole in the air**: the burned thing leaves no smell, and for a moment nor does anything near it (I·18).

**After a white burning, the name [R2-myth-05].** White fire erases existing records of the burned and the memory of witnesses within a few paces. Afterwards the name **takes in ink only from a hand that remembers**, and holds only while someone who remembers keeps it; other hands erase it without knowing why (the limewashed wall, I·18). Spoken aloud in public by one who remembers, it is a small black flame (Law 6): Talka names Tamrat before the emperor (I·23), so the court remembers the name while the Coptic pilgrims, who heard no one say it, sing a nameless "martyr of the bridge" (I·30).

**Black-flame text records its witnesses [R1-dread-03].** A black burning writes the burned person's text, and also enters those present at the burning as **witness lines**. Because they live, their lines are never finished either. The Bet Maryam ceiling holds Amara's text plus lines for Gebre, Talka and the boy; Gebre's line grows "opened his hand to the Latin" in 1324 (I·16). Gebre's palm carries the boy's line and keeps writing it (I·Pro, 4, 12, 23, 30). The ceiling cannot record what happened to people who were not there: it does **not** know of Tamrat's theft (I·16).

**The Line [R1-mystery-04, R1-lit-05].** The first line of Amara's strip, written twice by her (once on the strip, once in the ceiling's text), is the series' final law in the warden-script. Its verb is the warden root that means *burn / end / release / let die*, the same root Beatrice renders "kill" in Angeska's letter (II). Zoser renders it in I·10: *"Burn what you carry, while you remember whose you are."* He takes "what you carry" to mean the strip and resolves never to burn it. Its true sense is the willing black burning of the vessel by one who remembers (VII). Each book offers one new rendering by a POV with a motive (`series-outline.md` B.8), always set as **the Gloss**, a four-column table SIGN | SOUND | CANDIDATES | CHOSEN [R2-lit-03].

**Warden roots are many-sensed [R2-mystery-09].** The Line's verb is not alone. The ceiling's prophecy (I·16) uses another warden root that covers **bind / wed / bury**: *A woman of the [ — ] will [ — ] a priest for the sanctum.* Talka reads it as "bind" (an oath); the reader fills it as "wed" when Angeska's Ge'ez litany uses the same root at I·27. The first gap ("Wardens") stays unread until II.

## 3. Writing as magic: documents as spells

- A sealed document **binds** when authority stands behind it **and** people comply with it. A document written with **the Nib** (Fragment 3) or pressed with **the Seal** (Fragment 5) binds harder: its clauses enter the bodies it names. The ink shows as a grey line under the skin that only vessels can see.
- A **forgery binds while it is believed** (the Pius II dispensation, 1469; the Company's deeds, c.1840).
- A document **breaks** when it is contradicted by a **truer, witnessed record**: a testimony, a counter-ledger, a name spoken in public, an annotation in a second hand. Every Curator's Scar is a breaking point.
- **The Curator edits; the resisters annotate.** The series' palimpsest form (§11) is the magic made visible.
- **Translation is the subtlest spell.** Whoever renders a text decides what it binds. Angeska softens; Beatrice mistranslates; Nafisa corrects. *Untranslated speech is never neutral.*

## 4. Vessels

**Made by ingestion and writing.** The fragment or the Entity's substance enters the body, and the body is written on.

**Rules (bible §6.4, retained):**
1. A vessel does not age while the Entity holds it, and loses time (time-blindness).
2. Two vessels' entities can merge and splinter into a child (Rosa, 1439).
3. A dying vessel's essence passes to the nearest vessel (Angeska → Rosa, l.1936).
4. A vessel can be bound by sacrifice of self (Haimeric, l.2840).
5. **Killing a vessel releases what it holds** (l.1898). This is the trap the last Humbert springs.
6. Vessels make flames falter and plants wither.
7. **[SHOWRUNNER RULING]** A vessel's skin carries the record of what it hosts; this is why Zoser's veins "rearrange". A vessel who **chooses** a black burning while remembering who he is **records** the Entity instead of releasing it. The body becomes a page (vellum). That is the series' endgame.

**The candle test.** Ordinary flame **leans toward** an unhidden fragment or vessel and **burns blue**. This is how the Silence-Keepers hunt bearers (Captain Humbert, Book I Ch.1). Fragment 0 and the crescent tokens defeat the test.

**The operating rule [R2-myth-01, R2-magic-01, ROUND 2 RULING].** Never stated in prose; a reader assembles it from I·1, 6, 10, 11 and 22.
1. **Reach.** Any ordinary flame leans toward an unhidden fragment or vessel within about **a hand's breadth**, and only while it stays that close. A **swallowed** fragment reads only at that range (which is why Tamrat sits for hours a pace from lamps in I·2–5 and nothing leans, and why he keeps an arm's length from every flame).
2. **The hunter's candle.** A flame reaches a deck's or a hall's length only when it is a Humbert's candle: **beeswax blessed at the Pope's Candlemas**, held in the hand of a man who has **drunk the Nail-water**, with **a question put to it**, aloud or under the breath. The asking is stolen from the grandmothers' lamp-blessing, where the grandmother asks the flame whether the child is claimed; the order took the asking with the lamp. (I·1: the captain's candle is beeswax that "smelled of church", held cupped, watched, while he puts the catechism. I·6: Humbert "looked at his flame. An old habit" among four hundred blessed candles.)
3. **Waking.** When what is carried **wakes** in its bearer, every flame in the room answers it, and then answers whatever it attends to. The sign of waking is **blood from the nose** (Tamrat on the stair, I·10; the hall, I·11; Humbert's hidden nosebleed, I·32).
4. **Uncovering.** Warden-script read aloud is uncovered (Fragment 0's rule below). A **fresh copy** of warden-script, **read aloud by the one who copied it**, leans flames near it while its ink is wet (I·22: the curia's candles lean to Angeska's sleeve). The same clause let the Dominican's breviary copy of Gebre's palm teach the order (I·16). Angeska can therefore control the Lean by controlling when copies are read: a cost-and-limit a character exploits.
5. **Rite.** When a Rite opens a door, the flames of the place that keeps its record lean toward it, however far (Bet Maryam, I·30).

**Weather.** Wind makes flames shrink, smoke and die. It never bends a flame and holds it, and never turns one blue. Only the Lean does that.

**The Standing [R2-myth-02, ROUND 2 RULING].** The counter-grammar. While the dead are named aloud **with love**, flames within reach stand perfectly still in moving air, and their smoke rises in unbroken threads (the jeli, I·2; Angeska's litany, I·9; the cell, I·13; Talka naming Tamrat, I·23; the Timkat vigil, I·30). Naming without love (a register, a roll-call, a captive's names that no one present loves) gets no Standing. Never explained. Flame leans to seeking and stands for naming; white fire, which throws no shadow, is the third state. Books IV–VI (the ledger, Bois Caïman, the burning water) inherit this vocabulary.

**Mulier non portat [R2-mystery-02, ROUND 2 RULING].** The Silence-Keepers **do not try women**. Their founding gloss (Rule II) holds, with the scholastics, that woman is matter and not form, a vessel and never a bearer: *mulier non portat, sed portatur*, "a woman does not carry; she is carried". So no hunter holds a candle to a woman's face (I·1; "One woman, his wife; not tried", I·6). Angeska counted on the rule. The first flame held to a woman in the series is the bargeman's lamp (I·31), out of fear, not law, and its blue tip is the rule failing. After Rosa (III) the order amends its rule in the 1440s ("let women also be tried"): a seam in their own regulations.
- **The Lean (visual grammar) [R1-dread-02].** The flame tips like a head turning to listen, holds, and its tip blues. Never described the same way twice. In Book I no flame may lean, tip and hold, or turn blue except by the Lean; wind makes flames shrink, smoke and die [R2-magic-01]. At I·33 it **inverts**: every flame leans away from Zoser, the way animals shy.
- **Its origin is stolen [R1-myth-03].** Around Lalibela the grandmothers hold an oil lamp to a newborn at the lying-in: if the flame leans, "the child is claimed", and the family brings it to the priests **for blessing**. (An invented local custom, not a claim about Ethiopian practice at large.) Gebre showed it to the Latin missionaries in 1324; the Silence-Keepers turned a blessing into a test for killing. Desta knows it from his sister's lying-in, which is why he understands the plank in I·1 faster than anyone.
- **Fragment 0 hides only while its text is unspoken [R1-mystery-04, R2-mystery-03].** Reading the strip aloud "uncovers" it. Zoser does so in I·10, and the veil comes for him in I·11 ("You dared to uncover them!"); the candles swing to him. It hides again only once it is **sewn shut and unspoken**: from I·10 to I·15 Zoser keeps it unsewn against his skin, and only when he sews it back beside the Key (I·15) does the cell candle steady.

**Visual signatures of a vessel:** grey scales "like vellum"; ink-veins that move (l.1417); no taste and no warmth; a heartbeat out of step with the world (l.1379); plants wither and insects die nearby (l.1405); candles gutter; seers see "a relic-shaped hole" (l.1454).

## 5. The Ark and the fragments

The Ark is a **container for the Word**. Its acacia was cut "on the mountain where the fire spoke, before Moses came down" (bible §3.1), and it soaked up that fire. A splinter of it acts as a **wick**: a body, a pen or a box can hold the fire through it without being consumed. Every fragment is also a **seed** (l.1161) that can make a vessel. And the fragments are the only fuel on which black flame burns **completely**.

## 6. The Rite: one grammar

Every Rite has **five parts**:
1. a **Vessel**;
2. a **Crucible** (brazier, sarcophagus, Charter chest, conference table);
3. a **Splinter** or substance;
4. a **Reading**: a text spoken aloud;
5. **Seven Witnesses**, whose memory of the act is taken as a **tax**.

The rite at Avignon leaves seven witnesses amnesiac; the Clerk is the one who escapes the tax. Readers can **count witnesses** as a fair-play clue: seven cardinals at *Dum Diversas*; three crowns and four confessors at Alcáçovas; two times seven powers at Berlin, a **double Rite**.

**The tax falls only on the witnesses the Rite counts [R1-mystery-05, R1-myth-05].** A Rite's Reading names its seven. Those the record does not count (servants, slaves, women, notaries, the man "who is nobody") are present but untaxed, and keep everything. At Avignon Humbert names three cardinals and four clerks, "and Brother Anselm to keep the notes, who does not count". Eight figures stand in the chamber; seven small candles burn in the chalk circle, one per counted witness, and each goes **blue** as its man's memory is taken. The reader can solve the Clerk's survival by counting. The **reader** of a Rite (Humbert, for the *lectio* only) is half-taxed; the Nail-water in his blood holds part of the rest. This is the series' politics as magic: the people the record erases are the witnesses erasure cannot reach. Use it deliberately: Zaynab (unrecordable after 1492), the enslaved at Alcáçovas, the Merikin wives at the deeds. The Clerk's wax tablet is also the first record of a Rite kept by someone the Rite did not count; Isabella's ledger technique (IV) rediscovers the instinct on purpose.

An **anti-Rite** is a Reading that remembers instead of erasing, and needs no tax. Examples: Bois Caïman (1791); the Act of Independence read at Gonaïves (1804); the found family reading Zoser back to himself (1896). Every book's climax is a Rite or an anti-Rite.

## 7. Rosa's rule **[SHOWRUNNER RULING]**

- **Hunger.** About once a year she must take the last breath of a dying person, or her body ages toward its true age.
- **Inheritance.** She keeps every memory of everyone she consumes. She **cannot forget**, and she risks drowning in other lives. Her close third gathers italic intrusions from the dead (§11).
- **The tithe.** Every breakthrough of insight triggers a death near her that she did not choose. In Book III she deduces the pattern from the convent burial book.
- **Her ethic, earned.** From c.1500 she feeds only on the consenting dying. There is one lapse: Bengal, 1770, when she takes those not yet dying.
- **Her powers.** She reads the living script effortlessly; she has pattern-sight; she has "the silence of the owl, the subtlety of the cat, the patience of the spider" (l.2623). She can **set a black seal** on a document so that it cannot be white-burned or forged; this costs her one inherited life's memories.
- **Her end.** In Book VII she stops feeding, and ages.
- **She is the opposite of the Entity:** a body that consumes **and keeps**.

## 8. The Entity (the Wound): taxonomy and life cycle

**One being, many faces** (bible §6.3). Its faces are organs at different life stages, not separate gods.

| Face | What it is |
|---|---|
| The Curator | The archive face: it edits records, unmakes peoples, leaves scars. |
| The Schism | The division face: opened 1054, woken 1130, bound in Haimeric. |
| The God of Chaos | What its victims call it. It harvests *controlled* upheaval. |
| The Guild | Its second body from 1378: lodges, banks, trade. |
| The covenant | The pact rulers make with it; their shadows merge. |
| The Company | Its corporate mask, 1600–1874. |
| The General Act | Its last body, 1885. |

**Offspring.** Rosa's splintered inheritance (1439). The **Blood Thing** (1492; consumed by Rosa in 1503). The **winged being** (1783). The **noseless *ka*** of Giza (1798). Each is made of a specific erased group, and each can be named back.

**What it is (revealed in VII).** **The archive of the erased.** It is a wound filled with everything white fire has taken, monster and victim at once. Its hunger is the hunger of the erased to be recorded, twisted so that it can grow only by erasing others. Its oldest stratum is the **Il Moro**, unmade long ago by the Wardens of Aksum: the Wardens' secret guilt, and the root of Angeska's grey motive (to give the erased a body).

**Life cycle (the escalation ladder):**

| Stage | Biology | Historical body | Book |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spore | A tear in a shared text | 1054, Hagia Sophia (Teodoro, the previous vessel) | II (Testament) |
| Dormancy | Encysted in a self-sacrificing binder | Haimeric, 1130; the Nail; the Lid | II prologue |
| Host | Lives in one ingesting body and writes on it | Zoser, 1327 | I |
| Reproduction | Two vessels splinter into a child | Rosa, 1439 | II |
| Colonisation | Grows like mycelium in ink | The bulls, 1452–93 | III |
| Symbiosis | Grows institutions | The Compass and the crowns, 1499–1614 | IV |
| Distribution | Has no single host | The Company Charter, 1793 | V |
| War with its negation | The negation takes bodies; the magic goes mass | 1794–1816 | VI |
| Metastasis | Self-replicating writing (race, deed, debt) | 1816–1885 | VII |
| **Record** | Read aloud in black flame: finite and mortal | Lalibela, 1896 | VII |

**Its voice: it only quotes [R1-dread-01].** The Entity has no words of its own. Everything it says is assembled from words already spoken or written near a fragment or a vessel, given back verbatim in the original speaker's cadence: a cento of the archive. In Book I this is absolute and every quotation is sourced in a concordance (`books/book-01/PLAN.md`). In later books, as its archive grows, its sentences become longer collages, but writers must still be able to source every phrase. This is how an "archive of the erased" talks, and it plants VII without stating it.

**The excommunication from creation partly binds [R1-dread-05].** The veil's Word-act (I·11) begins to unwrite Zoser from the world's records: ink will not take his name, breath leaves no fog, registers leave a blank, memories of his name disagree. One small deniable proof per chapter, never spectacle. This is the first stage of "unseen means unremembered" (§4.1). **[R2-mystery-06, R2-dread-07] Ruling on records:** it does **not** alter originals made before it (Dimiana's diptych of I·7 keeps "the boy Zoser, his scribe"; the Naples toll-roll keeps *MAVRI III*). It prevents any record made **after** it from holding him, **copies of earlier records included**: a Lent fair copy of the toll-roll reads *MAVRI II* (I·21); his own signature on the stufa letter reaches Ethiopia as an empty reed-groove (I·23). The reader catches the erasure in a document before any character does.

**Diet.** Division, conquest, enslavement as sacrifice, renaming, erasure.
**Fears.** Kindness, conviction, ideas, its negation, **being read**.

## 9. The negation (it has no proper name)

- **Origin.** Born c.1615 in a slave ship's hold "between shackled souls" (l.4531), as captives chant their names. It is made of **names**, the same stuff as the Entity, turned the other way.
- **It has no body.** It works through volunteers (Nanny's people, Jay, Médard, Ayo, the Keepers). Each people names it in its own tongue; Jay calls it "Brother". Lady Liberty is the face empire gives it.
- **Its vessel's cost.** **They cannot stay.** Everyone Jay frees forgets his face.
- **Its limit.** "Ideas don't stand against maxim guns" (l.4449). It can also be **co-opted**: the 1739 treaty clause, the Black Companies sent against Fédon.

## 13. Forbidden in the prose

- No narrator explains a law. A character may *guess* one, and may be wrong.
- No spell without a price paid on the page in the same chapter or the next.
- No power appears without a prior plant. No power is used twice the same way without escalation or a new limit.
- No old power (obeah, Vodou, Kanu, Wardens) is framed as "dark magic". No darkening of skin is ever used as a sign of evil (E093). Angeska's change is material: char, glaze, ember-light.
- No modern magical vocabulary: "mana", "spell slots", "energy", "dimension", "aura". Characters use their own theological and craft vocabularies.
