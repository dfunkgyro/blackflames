# Blackflames — Chief Structural Editor's Audit

Subject: `blackflames.md` as it stands (6,475 lines, about 112,350 words, 158 headings).
Inputs: `series/craft-brief.md`; analyst notes `series/notes/part-01.md` to `part-08.md`. The analysts' key claims were spot-checked against the manuscript; see "Verification" at the end.
Line numbers refer to `blackflames.md`.

---

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

`blackflames.md` is rich raw material in a rough state. Its first 2,700 lines contain a real dark-fantasy novel: the Ethiopian embassy of 1325, Tamrat's vomited relic and martyrdom, Angeska's betrayal and the Black Rite in Avignon, Zoser "rewritten" and ageless, and Rosa born of two vessels. Its last 3,700 lines become a different kind of book: an anthology of essays, monologues and comic sketches about empire, slavery and erasure from 1130 to 2024, told by more than forty unlabelled narrators. The protagonists drop out of it. The title magic never appears: the phrase "black flame" occurs **zero times** in the manuscript. There are 17 empty headings, two leftover AI-assistant preambles (one on the first page), two verbatim duplicate chapters, and at least eight scenes told two to four times in contradictory versions. The themes (race invented by decree, the document as a weapon, erasure as the monster's food, the complicity of victims) are urgent and distinctive. The set pieces are often excellent. What is missing is the architecture a 10/10 epic needs: a series bible, a defined magic system, continuous POV protagonists, scenes in place of summary, and tonal control.

## 1. Baseline rating (the manuscript as it stands)

The overall score is a weighted judgement, as the brief requires, not an average. Structure, continuity and magic are the dimensions that carry the most weight for an epic series, and they are the weakest.

## 3. Structural diagnosis: what stops this being a 10/10 epic

### A. It is three works in one file, and only one of them is a novel
1. **A gothic novel (ll.1–2700, about 42%)**: Zoser, Tamrat, Angeska, Rosa, 1325–1492. It has protagonists, wants, a relic, betrayal and dread.
2. **An anthology of empire (ll.2700–5551, about 44%)**: Iberia, the Tudors, the Stuarts, the Company, Haiti and Grenada, narrated by about thirty new voices who rarely recur.
3. **A frame of tours (ll.4030–6475, scattered)**: the God of Chaos, Lady Liberty and the Observer moving through history up to 2024.

These parts share themes but not plot. A series must be one story told across books ("reading all of them tells the whole Blackflames story"). **Fix:** make the fragment hunt and Zoser's undying bond to the Wound the series' spine. Every era's POVs must be pulled into that spine: a fragment, a vessel, a document of the Curator's, or Zoser himself. Anthology material that cannot be connected is cut or becomes an embedded document.

### B. Chronicle summary instead of dramatised scenes
About a third of the 158 headings are essays, monologues or summaries in which no one wants anything on the page (E105). The brief requires micro-arcs, each with a proactive POV forced to choose between two bad options. **Fix:** apply a scene test to every chapter: a POV with an urgent want, an obstacle, a choice and a changed state. Anything that fails the test becomes (a) a dated embedded document (a bull, a ledger, a testimony, a charter or a journal), which is exactly the brief's formal-innovation request, or (b) a cut. Good examples already exist: Isabella's testaments and William Brown's journal.

### C. Telling, not showing
Emotion and theme are announced ("This is how empires echo", 2381; "Race… became a branding iron", 2457; "they were legends", 5698). **Fix:** carry the theme in objects and bodies. Examples: the bull's black ink; the label "slave" painted beside noble Moors (2531); the crescent mark carved into bows (3140); the overpainted faces (3640); Kwame clinging to his name. Cut the closing aphorisms.

### D. Centuries-long time-skips without anchors
The manuscript jumps 1325 → 1054 → 1095 → "15th century" → 1409 → 1450 → 1452 → 1492 → 1455 → 1484 → 1130 → 1493 → 1190… within a few thousand lines. Ageless characters make time unreadable ("decades had passed", 1716). **Fix:**
- Every chapter head carries POV, place and year (e.g. "ZOSER — Avignon, 1328").
- One rule for the ageless: Zoser surfaces once per generation (1770), and each surfacing costs him memory or a person (time-blindness, 1716). This becomes the chronicle's rhythm instead of its weakness.
- Each book covers a bounded period with one central crisis. Proposed shape, for the series plan to confirm:
  - I. The Fragment Bearer (1324–28)
  - II. The Rewritten (1328–1455, with 1054 as an embedded testament)
  - III. The Bulls (1452–92)
  - IV. The Crescent (1492–1612)
  - V. The Company (1612–1788)
  - VI. The Black Ships (1789–1816)
  - VII. The Ledger (1816–1885), with a frame resolving the Observer in the present.

### E. An unclear magic system, with the title magic missing
"Black flame" occurs zero times; about fifteen powers have no rules (E061–E066). The manuscript already holds the parts of a distinctive, visual and costly system:
- **Writing is magic.** The Aksumite wardens' pre-stellar tongue rewrites reality (548); the Key's ooze "wrote" (1069); "You were *rewritten*" (1430). Bulls, forgeries and archives are spells (1341, 2255, 2998). **The Curator** is erasure personified.
- **Bonding by ingestion.** Tamrat swallows the fragment (620); Angeska forces the Key into Zoser (1057). The cost is the host's body and time: no taste, grey scales, ink-veins, withering around him (1363–1417), agelessness and time-blindness.
- **Black flame = Lux Obscura, "dark light" (1520).** It is the wardens' old fire of memory: it burns cold, eats light, and makes ordinary flame gutter near a vessel (1385, 1844). It is what the Inquisition hunted as Moorish and African "forbidden knowledge" (4135–4139, 6223), and what burned at Amara's secret cremation (47).
- **White flame = the Order of the White Flame's purifying fire** (2972–3040): pyres and forgeries that erase. It looks holy; it feeds the Wound.
- **Counter-forces with rules:** compassion repels the Entity (1596); conviction wards against it (Nanny, 4584); remembrance starves it ("Cuida nuestra historia", 3146); "every division begets its negation" births the Anti-Entity (4533).
- **One taxonomy:** the Wound/Schism (one being, first opened in 1054 and given a body in Avignon) and its masks (the Guild, the Company, "the God of Chaos" as its victims call it); its counter-birth (the Anti-Entity and Lady Liberty); and the old powers (Kanu, obeah, the noseless Egyptian *ka*).

Lock all of this in a series bible before any rewriting.

### F. No continuous POV protagonists
The brief requires rotating close-third POV chapters, each titled with the POV's name, and each POV with an urgent want. At present: first-person memoir, about forty unlabelled narrators, and protagonists who disappear (E084–E086, E100). **Fix:**
- A core cast that recurs: Zoser, Angeska (POV until her death), Rosa, and the Observer (identity hidden until the end).
- Era POVs linked to the core: Catalina, Tomas and Zara, Kwame, Abercromby, Fédon, William Brown, Adanna.
- All first-person voices are kept only as documents inside close-third chapters.

### G. Tonal control
Farce interrupts dread at least eight times (E089–E098). Martin-style books allow gallows wit, not sitcom. **Fix:** humour lives in characters under pressure (Desta, Jay's mask, a veteran's rabbit story) and inside pockets of comfort: shared meals, the Black Ships anchorage, the Keepers' harvest. The narration itself is never comic.

### H. Ethics of representation
Several framings would sink a serious critical reception: African ethnicity as "black magic", an eroticised rape, an implication that same-sex love feeds the monster, a conspiracy-theory account of Freemasonry, and invented atrocity statistics presented as fact (E082, E091–E095). **Fix:** follow the rewrites in the register. Adopt a stated **secret-history rule**: real events are kept, hidden causes are invented, and invented numbers are always marked as unreliable documents.

### I. Mysteries without fair play
The reveals are announced rather than planted (Angeska, the Key theft, the Curator, the forged scroll). **Fix:** every major reveal gets three planted clues in earlier chapters, and every book closes at least one mystery while opening another. The manuscript's own continuity errors can be turned deliberately into the Curator's "temporal scars" (2767). That would be a formal innovation, provided the clues are designed rather than accidental.
