# THE BLACKFLAMES — Style Guide

The voice rules every chapter of every book must obey. Where this guide and the craft brief differ, the brief wins. Where this guide and a book's PLAN differ, ask the Showrunner.

**The register in one sentence.** Close third person, grave, sensory and morally grey. History is felt through a body that wants something urgent. Wit belongs only to characters under pressure, never to the narrator.

---

## 1. POV discipline

1. **One POV per chapter.** It is named in the chapter's `##` heading. Never switch heads inside a chapter, not even for one line.
2. **Close third, past tense.** The narration knows only what the POV knows, perceives, misremembers or refuses to think about. No "little did he know". No narratorial foreshadowing.
3. **First person exists only inside embedded documents**: letters, testaments, journals, memoirs, depositions, catalogue entries. A document is always framed: someone in the scene is reading it, writing it, hiding it or burning it.
4. **The POV may be wrong.** Errors of belief are allowed and encouraged. Errors of *narration* are forbidden: a POV chapter never states a falsehood as fact. Secrets are kept by **cutting away** (Angeska's chapters end before she speaks) or by the POV **choosing not to think** of something. Readers must be able to reread every POV chapter and find it honest.
5. **Each POV wants something urgent in every chapter.** The want is stated or shown in the first page, in concrete terms ("before the bell", "before the oil runs out"). No chapter may open with a POV reflecting in safety.
6. **Each POV has a perceptual signature.** It sets what they notice first, their metaphors and their vocabulary.
   - Zoser notices script and the hands of the powerful.
   - Angeska notices words, and what is left out of them.
   - Tamrat notices rank and his own body's betrayals.
   - Abba Talka notices ritual and weather.
   - Humbert notices his heart and his ledgers.
   - Rosa notices pattern, proportion and the dead she carries.
   - The Last Voice notices absences: what is missing from a room.
7. **Time-blind chapters.** In Zoser's chapters from Book I Ch.33 on, the date line may omit the year (*Florence — ?*). His narration can believe a week has passed when the next chapter's head shows decades. Never explain the gap in his POV. Let other POVs and the Keeper reveal it.
8. **Epithet POVs.** THE COPYIST, THE LAST VOICE, THE KEEPER and "WILLIAM BROWN" keep their epithet in the heading until the book in which the reveal happens. Then the heading changes, and stays changed.
9. **POV deaths happen in the dying character's own POV whenever possible** (Tamrat, Lucia Bellini, Angeska, Médard). The chapter ends at the edge of consciousness, never after it.

## 2. Chapter format

Every chapter file uses exactly this shape:

```
# CHAPTER 7

## TAMRAT

*Rome, by the Tiber — winter 1326*

First line of prose…
```

- `# CHAPTER N` uses Arabic numerals and runs continuously through the book (not per Part).
- `## NAME` is the POV in capitals: **ZOSER**, **ANGESKA**, **ABBA TALKA**, **CARDINAL HUMBERT**, **THE LAST VOICE**. Use titles only where the character thinks of themself by the title.
- The italic **place-and-date line** follows. Place first, then an em dash, then the date as the POV would reckon it (feast, season, regnal or Anno Domini; Ethiopian chapters may say *Timkat*, Granadan chapters may give the Hijri year in parentheses once per book).
- Prologue and epilogue: `# PROLOGUE` / `# EPILOGUE`, then `## NAME`.
- Parts: a separate page reading `# PART ONE — DIVISION` (later DECREE, CHAIN, ASH; Book VII adds RECORD) and, if wanted, one epigraph drawn from an in-world document. No epigraphs from real authors.
- **Scene breaks** inside a chapter: a single centred `⁂`. No other glyphs and no `---`.
- **A mid-chapter date head** (italic line alone) is allowed only for time-blind passages and for the Cardinal's crypt chapter (Book I Ch.32).
- **Embedded documents** are set in a block-quote, with a small-caps style heading line giving their nature and date, e.g. `> LETTER OF ZOSER MERI TO ABBA TALKA, AVIGNON, ADVENT 1326`. Curator's erasures are rendered as ~~struck-through~~ text. Translations shown side by side use a two-column table.
- **The Gloss** [R2-lit-03]: every rendering of the Line is a four-column table, `SIGN | SOUND | CANDIDATES | CHOSEN`, with warden-signs described in brackets ([a hand open]), unknown sounds as "—", and rejected candidates struck (~~end~~). It sits inside the scene: the prose between rows carries the room.
- **Keeper's entries (K format)** [Round 1 feedback D, R2-lit-06]: `## FROM THE KEEPER'S CATALOGUE`, then the italic Ge'ez-numeral date line, then one italic paragraph beginning *Item the [ordinal].*, then "— B.", then the spiral glyph ꩜ on its own line. The spiral is **shown**, never described in the entry's text. On a Part-title page the entry sits under `# PART ONE — DIVISION`.
- **Dialogue layout**: one speaker per paragraph. Two speeches by the same speaker are joined in one paragraph only with an action beat between them; never place two quoted speeches back to back without one.
- **Chapter length**: 3,000–7,000 words. A chapter under 3,000 must justify itself as a hinge (the Rite's three movements, an epilogue).
- **Ending**: every chapter ends on a **reversal, revelation or choice**, preferably in the last two lines and preferably in an image or spoken line. Never end on an aphorism or a moral ("The age of reckoning had begun").

## 3. Prose rules

- **Active verbs; concrete nouns.** "The lantern guttered", not "there was a guttering of the lantern".
- **Vary sentence length on purpose.** Long sentences for ritual, procession and dread building. Short ones for violence, revelation and the beat after.
- **Show the theme in objects and bodies**: the bull's ink, the slit lining, the label "slave" painted beside a noble face, a crescent carved in a bow. Never state the theme.
- **Sensory density**: at least three senses per scene, and **smell in every scene**. Cold, rot, food, wet stone, smoke, sweat, incense, tar.
- **Breadcrumb lore only when it changes a choice or a survival.** One new term per scene at most.
- **Prose that survives translation.** No puns that carry plot. No idioms whose meaning depends on modern English. Metaphors drawn from the POV's world: Zoser's from scriptoria and mountains, Isabella's from bowyery, Jay's from the sea and the ledger.
- **Simile budget and fields [R2-lit-08].** (a) **Budget:** at most three "as a [noun] [verb]s" / "the way a [noun] [verb]s" constructions per 5,000 words; turn the rest into metaphor, direct sensory statement or action. (b) **Fields:** each POV draws its figures from its own life, so the figure characterises. Zoser: books, ruling, ink, quires, stitches, mountains. Tamrat: vestments, feasts, coins, the pulpit. Angeska: thread, weaving, grammar, tides. Talka: terraces, rock, harvest, the knife, weather. Humbert: ledgers, seals, accounts, Acre's quay. Giovanni: law, marble, procedure, bread doles. Gebre: the body, the candle. A figure outside the POV's field needs a reason. (c) **Echo log:** a distinctive figure is used once per book unless it is a registered motif; log it in the book's `FACTS.md` ("Images used").
- **Dialogue tags**: "said" and "asked" by default. Action beats are better than adverbs.
- **Interior thought** in italics, sparingly, and only for verbatim thought.
- **Numbers**: spelled out in prose (except dates and documents). Documents may carry figures, and invented figures are always unreliable (see §8).

## 4. Banned habits (enforced at line edit)

| Banned | Instead |
|---|---|
| Summary-telling of key events ("Decades passed", "The situation was getting serious", "By the 15th century…") | Time-cut to the next dated head; dramatise the turning point. |
| Infodumps of lore, family trees, politics or mechanics | One fact, at the moment it costs someone something. |
| Static openings: waking, mirrors, window-staring, weather alone, reflection in safety | Open in motion, in conflict, against a clock. |
| Modern idiom: "okay", "optics", "downsize", "logistics", "I guess", "process" as a verb of feeling, "deal with", "issues", "impact" as a verb, "ecosystem", "trauma" as a clinical term before its time, "minions" | Period-plausible plain English. |
| Narratorial comedy and sitcom beats ("Oops, my bad!", scatology, punning heads) | Gallows wit in a character's mouth, under pressure. |
| Aphorism closers and "X, not Y" sermon endings | End on the image, the line, the choice. |
| Verbal tics: "whispers", "relentless", "pulsed with pleasure", "tapestry", "a testament to", "shivers down the spine", "eyes gleaming", "palpable" | Specific perception. |
| Purple stacking (three adjectives, two similes) | One exact detail. |
| Eye-dialect or phonetic spelling of any accent ("och aye the noo") | Syntax and word choice carry voice. |
| Darkening skin as a sign of evil; ethnicity tied to sorcery (E093) | Material transformation: char, glaze, ember-light, smoke. |
| Eroticised violence (E092) | Violation written as violation: paralysis, dissociation, aftermath. |
| Conspiracy tropes about real orders (Freemasons, Jesuits, Illuminati) as true | The invented Compass and White Flame; real orders appear only as unwitting covers or as people. |
| Markdown escapes, empty headings, working-label titles | Clean files in the §2 format. |
| "Black magic" as a label for African or Islamic traditions | Their own names: obeah, Vodou, Kanu, the Wardens' Word, *ruqya*. |
| Anachronistic names for things: "Vatican City", "Renaissance", "the Golden Ratio", "racism", "colonialism" in character speech | Period terms: "the Lateran" or "the Apostolic Palace", "the new learning", "the divine proportion", "the colour of their skin", "the Company's dominion". |

## 5. Sensory palette per culture and era

Food rules: **no New World crops in the Old World before c.1520**: no chili or berbere heat, tomato, maize, potato, cassava, cocoa, tobacco or vanilla in Books I–III outside the Americas. **Coffee-drinking** begins in Yemen c.1450 and spreads through the Ottoman world in the 1500s. Ethiopians in 1325 chew coffee berries at most; the Adwa camp coffee ceremony of 1896 is correct. **Sugar** is a costly spice in medieval Europe, an industry after 1500.

## 6. Dialogue conventions per language

**General law: foreign words sparingly, clearly, and never as decoration.**
- Render speech in another language as **plain English** and say which language it is in the narration ("he said in Arabic"). Do not italicise whole speeches in foreign tongues.
- At most **one** untranslated foreign word or phrase per exchange. Its meaning must be clear from context, an action or a following line. No glossaries and no footnotes in the text.
- Italicise non-English words, except proper nouns and words fully naturalised in English (*abbot*, *cardinal*, *pope*).
- When a language barrier matters to the plot (translation, mistranslation, eavesdropping), show the barrier: the POV hears sound without sense, or sees a two-column page. **Latin a POV does not understand stays unrendered or appears only as sound.**
- No eye-dialect or phonetic accents, ever. Class and region show in syntax, idiom and what people talk about.
- Each culture's **forms of address** are kept consistently.

| Language | Use | Forms of address and recurring words | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ge'ez** (liturgical) / **Amharic** (spoken) | Ethiopian prayer, oath, liturgy (Ge'ez); daily talk (Amharic, rendered as English) | *Abba* (father/abbot), *Qess* (priest), *Emama* (mother), *tabot*, *Timkat*, *Fasika*; "Lion of Judah" | Ge'ez appears as a prayer fragment only at moments of death or oath. Bulls quoted briefly and **accurately** ("Saracens and pagans", E075). Zoser understands Latin only after the Rite. |
| **Italian** (Tuscan, Roman) | Florence, Rome, the Bellini line | *Monna* or *Signora*, *Messer*, *fra*, *suora* | 14th-century Tuscan flavour only through address and oath ("by the body of Christ"). The veiled man's Tuscan accent is a clue. |

**Wit and banter.** It lives in Desta (dry, about counting and camels), in Jay's survival mask, in Rosa's acid patience with her father, in veterans' campfire stories (the Tilsit rabbits), and in shared meals. The rule: **every joke costs or protects someone.**

## 7. Dread and dark emotion

- Dread comes from **anxieties, not gore**: becoming what you carry, missing the lives of those you love, being forgotten, knowing your brilliance kills, forgetting your children to save them, never being said aloud.
- Violence happens quickly and is felt in its aftermath. **Atrocity is never spectacle.** The slave ship, the pyres, Bengal and the Congo are rendered through what one person cannot escape: a sound, a smell, one face.
- **Pockets of comfort come immediately before loss**: Bellini's broth before the time-blind chapter; the cell's last bread before Tamrat's burning; the bow-workshop supper before Southwark; the anchorage feast before Bridgetown.

## 8. How to handle real history (the secret-history rule)

1. **Real events happen as history records them.** Dates, battles, bulls, treaties, deaths and outcomes are accurate. The series supplies **hidden causes**, never a different visible outcome.
2. **Where the manuscript changed the visible record**, the change becomes vision, rumour, camp legend, or a **falsified document** (a Curator's Scar). Examples: the 1493 ash apocalypse; Napoleon "freeing Africa"; the Merikins in 1797; invented atrocity tallies.
3. **Invented figures are always invented in-world.** A number that cannot be sourced appears only in a document with a stated author and motive, and is contradicted somewhere in the text.
4. **Real people** (popes, kings, Nanny, Toussaint, Fédon, Abercromby, Apodaca, Brown, Taytu) may speak, but are written with their documented positions and fates. Their inner lives are invented only through a POV's eyes, except for a small number of carefully researched POVs (Nicholas V, Afonso V, Isabella, George III, Abercromby, Apodaca), whose recorded acts must not be changed.
5. **Invented institutions** (the Silence-Keepers, the White Flame, the Compass, the Company as occult fusion, the Hand of Anima, the Keepers of Shadows as a sisterhood) stand in for real-world conspiracy theories, so that the series never endorses one about a real order (E095).
6. **Accuracy pass per book**: Pope names and reign dates; the Avignon papacy (1309–77); bull titles and dates; Ethiopian regnal names; ship names; ranks (Abercromby is a General); the dates in bible §3; no "Vatican City" before 1929; no "Renaissance" as a term in speech; no gaslight before c.1810s.
7. **Cultural traditions** (Tewahedo, Islam, Judaism, obeah, Vodou, Kanu, Spiritual Baptist faith) are rendered from the inside with respect, as **other archives of memory**. None is a villain's tool or "dark magic". The Church is not the Entity; it is the Entity's richest feeding ground and holds some of its oldest enemies.
8. **Transformation into fantasy** happens only through the magic system's rules: documents bind, fire records or erases, vessels lose time. A historical scene may gain a hidden layer (the candle leans; the ink crawls) but never a different public result.

## 9. First-chapter checklist (every book's opening chapter, verified at delivery)

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| No infodump | No lore, family tree, mechanics or politics explained. |
| Not static | No waking, mirror, window-gazing or reflection without conflict. |
| Names | At most 3–4 unique names or terms in the first pages. |
| Real stakes | Failure changes or worsens something. |
| Voice | The opening sounds like the rest of the book. |
| Hooks | In medias res; a question or contradiction on page 1; a clear focal desire; sensory anchors; a ticking clock; a familiar trope subverted by the chapter's end. |

## 10. Line-edit and copy-edit sheet

- **Spellings**: Aksum; Saint-Domingue (until 1 Jan 1804), then Haiti; Merikins; Afonso V; Nicholas V; Innocent VIII; Fédon; Louverture; Haimeric (the chancellor; "Aymeric" only in Pietro's mouth); Giovanni Orsini; Tewahedo; Ge'ez; Bet Maryam; Bet Medhani Alem; Abba Talka; Cuida nuestra historia.
- **Quotation marks**: curly double quotes for speech, curly single for quotes within quotes. One style throughout.
- **Dashes**: em dash without spaces in prose (interruptions); spaced em dash only in the chapter's date line.
- **Ellipses**: three periods, no spaces, used sparingly.
- **Numbers**: centuries in words ("the fourteenth century"); years in figures.
- **Hygiene**: "animal(s)", never "Angeskal(s)"; "union"/"communion", never "Sindical"; no "Masonic Guild of Fascism"; no "Necronomicon" (use "the Book of Unmaking").
