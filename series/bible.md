## 1. Premise

Blackflames is a secret history of the second millennium in which power rules by rewriting: whoever controls the word (bull, ledger, deed, name, translation, archive) controls who counts as human. In 1325 a young Ethiopian monk, **Zoser Meri**, travels from Lalibela to Rome carrying the faith of an Africa that Rome refuses to recognise. A stolen sliver of the Ark of the Covenant, the **Immortal Key**, is forced into his body in a rite beneath the papal palace at Avignon. He dies, is "rewritten", and becomes the undying vessel of an **Entity** that feeds on division: schism, race, slavery and erasure. Across nine centuries it wears the faces of popes, inquisitors, kings, lodges and finally a chartered **Company**. Zoser, his betrayer **Angeska**, and their crimson-eyed daughter **Rosa** are bound to it as carriers, victims and accomplices. They are opposed by the people its decrees erase: Moors and Moriscos, the Black cardinals, Blackamoor exiles, maroons, the Merikins, and the women's networks that keep the record. Their weapon is memory ("*Cuida nuestra historia*", l.3146). The central question is Tamrat's dying gift, "a *question*" (l.747): where do holiness and authority truly live, and who has the right to write them down?

## 2. Canon principles (the series' governing rulings)

1. **Writing is magic [RULING, grounded in MS].** The wardens of Aksum spoke "words capable of rewriting the laws of reality" (l.548); the Key's "molten ooze wrote" on Zoser (l.1069); "You were *rewritten*" (l.1430); the Entity lives "in the ink, in the parchment" of the bulls (l.2255); the Curator edits the archive (ll.1748, 2765–2767); forged documents bind dynasties (l.2998). Bulls, ledgers, deeds and translations are spell-documents. This is the formal licence for the brief's embedded documents.
2. **Erasure feeds the Entity; remembrance starves it [RULING].** Grounded in Torquemada's damnation (ll.3732–3752), the Curator (l.2767), and "even their disappearance disappeared" (l.3526).
3. **Every division begets its negation [MS, ll.4533, 4778, 4788].** The Entity begets the Anti-Entity; the Guild begets Sindical.
4. **One Entity, many faces [RULING].** See §6.3 for the taxonomy that unifies the God of Chaos, the Schism, the Curator, the Guild and the Company.
5. **Zoser's canon life begins c.1304 [RULING].** The 1064 famine and the 1054 schism survive as an ancestral prologue and as the Entity's memory of an earlier vessel (§8, C1–C3).
6. **No sitcom register.** Comic chapters (the plank's "Oops, my bad!", Nicholas V's "color chart", the Napoleon nose capers, the Message Bearer romps, the King's Bible banter, the haggis duel) keep their events where useful, but are re-voiced as black humour, unreliable memoir or campfire legend.

### 4.1 The bloodline of the Key

#### ZOSER MERI — the vessel
- **Identity [MS/RULING].** An African monk-scholar raised by the monks of Lalibela, narrator of most of the first half of the manuscript (first person). Born c.1304 [RULING] to Amara and Ika, a Nile family. The manuscript calls them "Nilotic" and Egypt-born (ll.15, 19) and also "born in Holy Lalibela" (l.524). [RULING] Nubian Christians of Makuria; Zoser says "Lalibela raised me". Named for the pyramid-builder Djoser, a clue to the Il Moro/Kemetic layer (notes p.03). [R1 RULING] No one can read "Djoser" before Champollion; in Books I–V the clue is only "a name the old stones keep". The explicit reading belongs to VI or VII.
- **Appearance.** Dark skin "ashy as parchment" after the dungeon (l.797). After the Rite: dry skin with **grey scales**, **ink-like veins that move and rearrange** (l.1417), no scar where the Key went in but a "ghost" lodged in the spirit (l.1419), a voice "deeper. Slower. Measured like liturgy" (l.1369), a heartbeat out of step with the world (l.1379). He does not age from 1326: Marcus, forty years on, says "You haven't aged a day" (l.1730). Plants wither and insects die near him (ll.1405–1407). Seers see "the relic-shaped hole in his soul" (l.1454) and say he wears "a dead man's skin" (l.1399).
- **Wants.** At first, to prove worthy (l.103) and be a "living witness" of Ethiopian Christianity (l.375); to take the relic back to Lalibela (l.630). After the Rite, to be free of the Entity, to protect those he loves (Bellini, Rosa), and later only to stay hidden. Among the Moors (1450s–90s), to protect the hunted and find them a haven (Granada).
- **Fears.** Becoming what he carries ("I had *become* it", l.1464); harming the kind; the Church's fire; being unseen and forgotten. He doubts whether he is still a man ("I'm... something else", l.1311).
- **Secrets.** He carries the Entity; he killed his teacher Humbert by the Entity's speech (l.1684); he fathered Rosa while under vows (l.1856); a name is forming in his mind letter by letter (l.1371); Angeska ordered their daughter to kill him (l.1898).
- **Arc.** Pack-bearer → the envoy who answers for Tamrat at sea (ll.309–321) → bearer of the stolen Key (l.630) → drugged, killed and rewritten in the Black Rite (ll.1005–1225) → carrier of "Chaos. Schism. Sickness." ### 4.2 The Entity and its opponents

#### THE ENTITY
- **Identity.** An ancient hunger "woven through the marrow of the world" (l.4529). It calls itself, or is called, the schism (l.1526), the Curator (l.1496), the Risen One's source, the God of Chaos (l.2341), the Schism (l.2792), the Guild (l.4771), the Company (l.4708), the covenant (l.3100), the whispers (l.2319), "the architect of the divisions" (l.4529). Its **true name** is unspoken, forming in Zoser's mind "letter by letter" (l.1371). See §6.3 for the unifying ruling.
- **Appearance.** Formless; "a hunger without a mouth" (l.2866). Manifestations: golden molten eyes and writhing scars in the veiled man (ll.538–546); shadow coiling into a chest (l.1574); "eyes of fire, a mouth of black smoke, a body like a storm of screams" (l.2794); "a shifting mass of shadows and gleaming teeth, moving through the corridors of power like smoke through keyholes", "voice like grinding continents" (ll.6235–6237); the Company as a mollusk-machine trailing liquid silver and gold, pearls pulsing in its skin, wires under translucent flesh, black-void eyes, a collective voice "We are One" (ll.4694–4712).
- **Wants.** To feed; "release" (l.1484); to be "free" (l.1544); "Eternal war in my name" (l.2824); "endless cycles of conquest and despair" (l.2387); order through division ("Divisions are the marrow of order", l.4539); to merge ritual with technology (l.2884).
- **Fears.** Kindness (it recoils from Bellini, l.1596); certainty of purpose (Nanny, l.4584); ideas ("Then why do you fear them so?", l.4451; "it was afraid", l.6469); its own negation, the Anti-Entity.
- **Secrets.** Its name. Its origin (1054 tear, 1326 seed, or older; see §8 C2). That it is fed "unknowingly" by lodges (l.2970) and by bulls. That it whispered "white" into the 17th century (l.4529).
- **Arc in MS.** Seeded or woken in Zoser → grows with each papal schism (ll.1862–1870) → lives in the ink of bulls (l.2255) → wears Torquemada's face (l.3664) → merges royal shadows into a covenant (l.3022) → moves from Church to Crown to lodge to Company → invents race (l.4529) → begins to fade before the Anti-Entity (l.4569) → still touring empire with the Observer in 1884 and 2024 (ll.6233–6279).
- **Fate.** Not destroyed.

#### THE ANTI-ENTITY / SINDICAL / LADY LIBERTY — the negation
- **Identity [MS].** The Anti-Entity is born "in the crevices of the 17th century, between shackled souls and bleeding lands", with a voice "rough with raw vitality… born from clay still wet" (l.4531). Sindical (first called "Anarchy") is born of the Guild's excess in 1378: "I am your counterbalance, your child born of excess" (l.4778). Lady Liberty is "crowned with stars", with a torch "that made even Chaos flinch" (l.4403); she calls the God of Chaos "my old friend" (l.6467).
- **[RULING]** They are one counter-force at different ages and in different masks. It has no body of its own and works through people (Nanny, Jay Anarchy, Ayo, Toussaint's followers, the Keepers of Shadows).
- **Wants.** For humans "to see themselves beyond the cages" (l.4559); "true power lies not in being unstoppable, but in knowing when to stop" (l.4798).
- **Fears / limits.** "Ideas don't stand against maxim guns" (l.4449). Its light "dimmed" at Berlin (l.6433).
- **Fate.** Ascendant but not victorious; jointly asks the Observer "Are you sure you want to see?" (l.6473).

#### THE VEILED MAN
- **Identity.** A being that rises through Tamrat in Cardinal Giovanni's chamber, with golden molten eyes, skeletal dagger-nailed fingers, living scars and a layered voice (ll.538–562). He "excommunicates" Zoser from creation and lectures on the wardens (ll.544–552). Seen again in 1328: "calm and cold… he wasn't a man at all. It was a vessel." (ll.1474–1478).
- **[RULING]** The veiled man is the Entity's **previous vessel**, the Florentine monk of 1054, worn as a mask, so his appearance in Tamrat is the old vessel reaching for the new one. The veil is made of shadow rising from Tamrat. [OPEN] whether he is also the Curator's "shadow figure" (l.1488).

#### THE THIRD ENTITY (the Blood Thing)
- Born July 1492 from the transfused blood of three boys and Innocent VIII's corpse: "neither flesh nor shadow… twisted limbs and faces that screamed silently" (l.2665). It asks "Why did you let this happen?" (l.2673) and absorbs the pope's corpse (l.2681). [RULING] Rosa's blood, bled into the corpse by physicians (l.2631), is the catalyst; it therefore blames and hunts her. Counting: first the Entity (in Zoser), second Rosa's inheritance (the splintered entity of 1439, l.1856), third this. [OPEN] its later career (the Borgia papacy is a natural host).

#### THE OBSERVER
- **Identity [MS].** An unnamed, time-walking first-person witness, "here, but not here" (l.6239), watching "for a century now", taken through history by the God of Chaos and Lady Liberty ("We three", l.4401; "our observer", l.4457), from Granada to Berlin to 2024.
- **[RULING]** The Observer is **the last voice of Granada** (ll.2341, 3962), a Granadan scholar cursed in 1492 to witness until the Entity ends. [OPEN] Alternative candidates: Torquemada's freed soul, Alfonso's ghost, or a descendant of Zoser. Want: "Show me how it ends." (l.6475). Cost [RULING]: each vision takes a year of their remaining life, or a memory.

## 5. Factions and institutions

### 5.1 The Church (Latin)
- **[MS]** A power that rules by decree: bulls, excommunications, dispensations. "Justice is whatever the Church decrees" (Giovanni, l.476). Rome treats the Ethiopian church as heretical (married priests, Enoch, 81 books against Rome's canon; ll.410–478). The Avignon papacy is "Rome transplanted" (l.881), a golden cage that offers "leverage", not mercy (l.853).
- **Its relationship to the Entity [MS].** Each schism feeds it (1054, 1130, 1328, 1378, 1409, 1439; ll.1862–1870). It lives "in the very foundations of the Church" (l.2255). Popes become its vessels (Innocent VIII, l.2487).
- **[RULING]** The Church is not the Entity. It is its richest feeding ground, and it also holds the Entity's oldest enemies (Bellini, Marcus's faith, Beatrice's oath, the Black cardinals, Pastor Jeremiah's congregation). Ethiopian Tewahedo Christianity is presented as an equal tradition, never as a heresy the narrative endorses as such.
- **Key documents:** the 1054 bull (l.1556); Dum Diversas 1452 (l.2241) "written in black blood ink" (l.2235); Romanus Pontifex 1455 (l.2245); Summis desiderantes 1484 (l.2489); Inter Caetera 1493 (l.2895); the forged Pius II dispensation (l.2998).

### 5.2 The Black Cardinals
- See §4.3. **[MS]** African churchmen in Rome in 1452 whose own Pope's bull condemns them by skin ("My faith means nothing. My skin, however, damns me", l.2199). **[RULING]** The Ethiopian and Nubian clergy of Santo Stefano degli Abissini, red hats *in petto* after Florence, named "the Black cardinals" by Rome. They are the living rebuttal to the bull's logic and a standing diplomatic link to Ethiopia (Prester John). [OPEN] whether any survive to carry Tamrat's "question" home.

### 5.3 The Silence-Keepers
- **[MS]** A Church order in crimson robes and iron masks (l.1532), later with robes "embroidered with ancient, forgotten symbols" (l.1662); "Silent Keepers" at l.1660 [RULING: one name, Silence-Keepers]. They hunt Zoser: "Release the parchment and submit to judgment" (l.1534). They do not move when Humbert dies (l.1688).
- **[RULING]** The Church's oldest secret police for relics and vessels, keepers of the Lux Obscura chapel beneath the Vatican. Their masters take the name **Humbert** as a title, which explains the three Humberts. [OPEN] whether they serve the Entity or wait for its "release".

### 5.4 The Order of the White Flame
- **[MS]** A secret Church faction that forged the dispensation for Isabella and Ferdinand's marriage in the dead Pius II's name, with Rodrigo Borgia's help, to make "a united, Catholic Spain" (l.2998). It has "tendrils" in the Castilian court (l.3000), intercepts decrees from Rome (l.3040), and directs the Inquisition: "Africans all faced the White Flame's wrath" (l.3038).
- **[RULING]** The Order is the transnational arm of the Inquisition's secret purpose: to hunt and burn the "forbidden knowledge" of Moors and Africans (ll.4133–4139, 6223). Its fire is the **white flame** (§6.2). It operates covertly in Protestant England (which repairs the "Inquisition in Greenwich", l.3598). Father Miguel is its agent. [OPEN] whether it can make the dead sign (necromantic forgery).

### 5.5 The Inquisition
- **[MS]** Torquemada (possessed), Diego Velasquez, Brother Alonso, Father Miguel. Its order is "to erase, not just to convert" (l.4113). It burns libraries, removes grave markers and repaints faces pale (ll.3630–3642, 3720). Its "Grim Tally" is a falsified ledger: "the numbers are lies" (l.3702). It ends in Spain under Napoleon in 1808 (l.6209).
- **[RULING]** The Spanish Holy Office answers to the Crown under papal licence; its hidden steering comes from the White Flame. The Inquisition, the slave trade and the brotherhood are three tools of one war against the erased tradition.

### 5.6 The Entity's Guild and the Company
- **The Guild [MS].** Born from the 1378 schism: "It was chaos that gave me breath… the screams of burning heretics that gave me form" (l.4770). It claims the Black Death through its "trading ships", the Renaissance, Machiavelli, and Dum Diversas as "sacrifices" (ll.4771–4780). Earlier the Schism calls itself "Masonic Guild of Fascism" (l.2886) [RULING: name cut; "the Guild" stays]. Its doctrine is a **sacrifice economy**: the enslaved as "burnt sacrifices to fuel your insatiable hunger" (l.4780).
- **The Company [MS].** "neither fully living nor simply an abstract construct… destined to become the empire of empires" (ll.3930–3933). ## 6. Relics and magic as written

**Headline finding.** The manuscript **never uses the phrase "black flame(s)"**. "White Flame" appears only as the name of a secret order (ll.2972–3040). Parts 04–08 of the manuscript contain no Ark, fragment or flame magic at all. Everything below separates what is written [MS] from what the series must decide [RULING]/[OPEN].

### 6.1 The Ark and its fragments
- **[MS]** The Ark is held by "the church", with fragments missing; "those who have these fragments are cursed" (l.19). Tamrat: "I have seen the Ark of the Covenant, and it does not lie beneath your altars of gold and dead apostles" (l.719). Zion = the Ark ("Zion will shelter you", l.49).
- **[MS]** The curse, by Tamrat's account: Desta's death, the failed audiences, the dungeon, the death sentence (l.622).
- **[OPEN]** Whether the curse is real or a guilty man's reading of his own pride. The bible's position: real, but it works through the bearer's flaws.
- **[OPEN]** How many fragments exist, where they are, and what each does. Candidate fragments: Aymeric's crucifix (1130); the scuttled Spanish squadron in Chaguaramas Bay; Olayinka's spirit-box (1789–93); Papa Neezer's **Obee seed**, "a small stone said to carry insight and revelation" (l.5802).

### 6.2 Fire: black flames and white flames
**What is written [MS]:**
- Amara is cremated inside Bet Maryam, an anomaly for an Ethiopian church (l.47).
- Amara's courage is an "ember" and "a quiet promise waiting for its moment to ignite" (ll.49, 53).
- Tamrat burns without screaming (l.763); in vision he laughs "in fire that turned to flowers" (l.995).
- At the Rite: "Black candles lit corners that should have been shadow" (l.977); the Key "touched flame" and "screamed… with presence" (l.1035); gold → red → white → melt (ll.1037–1041); "It wrote across me in living fire" (l.1071); Angeska's eyes are "two molten embers… sockets that bled smoke" (l.1127).
- Candles sputter near Zoser and Rosa (ll.1385, 1973, 1991).
- The Lux Obscura entity shifts "between shadow and fire" (l.1526).
- The Entity snuffs Zoser's pyre "as if by a great wind" (l.1844).
- The Schism has "eyes of fire, a mouth of black smoke" (l.2794); Aymeric's crucifix gives "Light... blinding and pure" (l.2838) and his flesh burns as he becomes the vessel (l.2842).
- Saint Martha weeps black tears (l.1442); bulls are written in "black blood ink" (l.2235).
- Lady Liberty's torch "made even Chaos flinch" (l.4403).
- Apodaca's squadron burns at night (l.5571); Diego remembers "all those lives we cast into the flames" (l.6221).
- The Order of the White Flame forges, intercepts and persecutes (ll.2998–3040).

**[RULING] Canon definitions (the series' signature magic):**
- **The black flame** is the old fire of the erased tradition: the fire-cults of Aksum, the wardens' living script and the Il Moro's "Sekhem". It is *Lux Obscura*, "dark light". It burns cold and dim, eats ordinary light (candles gutter near it), and **writes and remembers**: what it burns leaves its text behind, legible to those who can read the script. It is the fire of record. It first appears in Amara's cremation. **Cost:** it is fuelled by the bearer's own memories and name; every use burns something the bearer knew. It cannot be lit by one who has forgotten who they are. This is why Zoser, who loses time, can rarely use it.
- **The white flame** is the Order's purifying fire: the inquisitorial pyre, the burned library, the forged seal. It burns hot and bright and leaves **no record**: what it burns is erased from memory and archive alike. It feeds the Entity (erasure is its food) even when the Order believes it serves God. **Cost:** each burning hollows the wielder's conscience (Diego's "hollow ache"; Torquemada as "what's left of him").
- **The Key's melt** passes from gold through red to white in Angeska's brazier: the moment the old relic is turned to the Entity's purpose. In the hands of a bearer who remembers, the same relic burns black.
- **Vessels** make ordinary flame falter; the Entity can quench fire around its host (l.1844).
- **[OPEN]** Whether black and white flames are two fires or one fire seen from two sides. Recommended: one fire; its colour depends on whether it remembers or erases.

### 6.8 The Cycle
- **[MS]** Three cycles are named: (1) the Entity's food, "the endless cycles of conquest and despair" (l.2387); (2) the Schism's "Two popes. Two factions. Eternal war in my name" (l.2824); (3) Thomas's wheel (ll.6335–6365): emancipation undone by deeds, laws and debt ("Slaves who don't even know they're slaves", l.6355), and the counter-argument "make the wheel itself irrelevant" (l.6409) and "a baton only has power if you let it" (l.6415).

### 10.1 People

- **Angeska's three-part confession [R1-mystery-02].** (1) *The theft was allowed.* She called the guardian of the sanctum at Aksum away from his post, speaking the old tongue at the door, so that her ambitious husband would take the wood and carry its curse and the candle's attention while she stayed unmarked. (2) *She summoned the candle.* In Alexandria she gave the captain's mate a sealed letter in Latin, signed only "A.", for the master of the order; Humbert pockets it unread in I·6, and Rosa finds it in the order's files in III. The captain's plank was his own cruelty, a cost she did not foresee. (3) *She chose the boy.* The suggestion to send "the orphan with the good hand" reached Talka through her. She delivers all three flatly at I·27, and the last breaks her voice.
- **Angeska and Amara.** They never met. Angeska was trained to revere the "southern carriers" of the Wardens' lore, of whom Amara's line was one. She spares the strip for that reason (I·31) [R1-psych-08]. She chooses Zoser because he is warden-kin, "the only body on earth already half-written". (Rejected: R1-mystery-03's childhood bond.)
- **Angeska's mercy is language [R1-psych-05].** She teaches Zoser one Latin word a night: *clavis, ostium, vas, germen, ignis, mater, custos*. Read together they are her plan. The Wardens permit no other mercy than truth given in advance.
- **Tamrat's irregular marriage [R1-cont-05].** A Tewahedo priest must marry before ordination and may not remarry. Tamrat's first wife, Ilfinesh, died c.1308–10. His marriage to Angeska was private, blessed by no bishop, quietly suspected by Talka. It is his first secret sin and her first leverage; the theft is his second transgression. The Wardens chose a priest "who would break canon once and could therefore be made to break it again".
- **Tamrat's Constantinople [R1-psych-03].** His boast (l.109) is a thirty-year lie. C.1295 he was kept three days at the Patriarchate's door, his satchel emptied for "Egyptian sorcery", a deacon wiping his hand after the kiss of peace. "Untouchable" is literal: he swallowed the wood so there would be something holy in him no searcher could reach. Being searched is his wound (I·1, 3, 14, 17).

Knowing the highland lamp-blessing, he understood that Humbert hunted a "claimed" body. He believed the candle leaned toward Zoser's scrip and walked off the plank to stop the search before it reached the boy. The candle was leaning toward Tamrat and could never see the scrip. First reading (I·15): he died for Tamrat's theft. Second (IV·~22): he died for the boy, for the wrong reason, and saved a theft he never knew about.

### 10.2 Institutions and objects

- **Bet Maryam's ceiling [R1-cont-08].** Talka had it painted over in 1311 (the church's ceiling is historically painted); the black script shows through the pigment. He scrapes a hand's breadth in I·16 and a long strip in I·30, and orders it to stand.
- **The guardian of the sanctum [R1-cont-08].** He discovered the loss at once, hid it for shame, and went blind by degrees (the curse takes the eyes of the keeper who failed). He was exposed only when the Timkat rite of 1326 made him open the inner chest before witnesses.
- **The lamp-blessing [R1-myth-03].** An invented local Lalibela custom, never presented as general Ethiopian practice: a grandmother holds an oil lamp to a newborn, and a leaning flame means "the child is claimed", for blessing. The Silence-Keepers' candle test is a stolen copy of it.
- **The jeli [R1-myth-08].** Fodé Kouyaté, hereditary keeper of genealogies in the Malian party at Alexandria, 1325. His recitation of the dead makes lamps burn upright: remembrance as a physical counter-force, shown before it is understood. Instrument: the *ngoni* or the *bala*, never the kora (later).
- **The tabot [R1-myth-06].** The book's thematic key: holiness made present on every consecrated altar, against Rome's one object in one place. Tamrat stole because he had come to think like Rome ("Every village has a tabot. I wanted the wood.").
- **Aksum's chiselled god [R1-myth-07].** An in-world throne-base among the stelae where "son of Mahrem" was cut away under a cross: Ethiopia's own erasure, a Curator's Scar in stone. The guardian's tale of the serpent Arwe plants the mythic solution: "You do not kill a devourer. You give it something it cannot swallow."
- **The victory register [R1-lit-08].** Ethiopia erases too: Amda Seyon's scribe counts Ifat captives without names (I·23). Only the scribe and the captive are invented; the wars happen as recorded.
- **Zoser's psalter [R1-lit-06].** It holds his child's አ, his adult አ beside it (I·10), and Angeska's seven Latin words. Left among the "martyred prophet's" effects in Avignon; recovered and carried to Lalibela (II, via Giyorgis, 1441); gathered by Tewodros II to Maqdala; looted by Napier's expedition in April 1868 and shipped to London (history unchanged; only this book's meaning is invented); stolen back by Rosa and placed in Zoser's hands at the climax (VII).
- **Zoser's letters [R1-cont-04].** The stufa letter (spring 1326) reaches Amda Seyon's camp in winter 1326–27 by way of the Coptic pilgrims. The Avignon letter (Christmastide 1326) is never delivered in Book I; it surfaces in II, carried home in 1441 or found in Humbert's papers, and is read a decade late.
- **Castel Sant'Angelo and the bridge [R1-cont-10].** The Orsini hold the castle. Giovanni Orsini relaxes Tamrat to the secular arm; Orsini men burn him at the family's own bridge-head.

### 10.3 Motifs added to §7

- **Counting against naming [R1-lit-09].** The Entity's documents **count**; the rememberers **name**. One dramatised beat per book (see `series-outline.md` B.8). At the climax the Last Voice returns Desta's joke: the man who could not count is the one who is named.
- **Salt** (memory) and **flour-white ash** (erasure) [R1-myth-02].
- **The Lean** of a candle [R1-dread-02].
- **The searched body** (Tamrat; later the vessel measured "as one measures a skin").
- **Gursha and kolo** [R1-myth-04]: love fed by hand; the body remembers love after the man cannot taste (I·33).

### 10.4 Calendar rulings

- **Easter 1326** fell on 23 March (Julian); Latin Lent began 5 February; the Ethiopian *Hudade* about a week earlier [R1-cont-02].
- **The fourteen nights** run from the night of 23 December 1326 to Epiphany Eve, 5–6 January 1327, the night the tabots stand by the water at Lalibela on the eve of Timkat [R1-cont-01].

## 11. Round 2 rulings (Editor-in-Chief)

Binding from Round 2. Ids refer to `series/ideas-register.md`. Dated facts and the computed chronology are in `books/book-01/FACTS.md`.

### 11.1 People

- **Zoser's age and first tongue.** Born c.1304; twenty-one in December 1325 (Ch.2 is corrected). Until he was six he spoke **the river tongue**, the Nubian of Christian Makuria, then collapsing under famine and Mamluk pressure (in 1317 the throne hall at Dongola was turned into a mosque, recorded by a dated inscription: a real document that edited a church). Lalibela overwrote it with Ge'ez and Amharic. His mother's word for him, rendered "my lion", is in it. The gift of tongues will never give it back (`magic-system.md` §4.1) [R2-myth-06].
- **Cardinal Humbert's mother.** Baptismal name **Aelis**; in Cairo "Maryam the Frank", in a sugar-house of Fustat. Angeska's Alexandria letter (I·6, embedded I·21) promised her to him, found through his own former searcher, **Avram ben Shelomo**, a Jew of Alexandria and clove-dealer by the Sea Gate who packs his letters in his own goods. "She asks whether her son kept the key." The passagium was always a ransom. She dies in the week after Epiphany 1327, the week of the Rite (revealed II·~16). Whether Angeska found her or invented her stays open until Rosa reads Avram's file in III [R2-mystery-01]. In I·22 Humbert has Zoser write *Aelis* in Ge'ez, a script no Silence-Keeper can read, and wears the slip on the cord beside the house key; the Roman chapter leaves it (I·32); dying in Florence (II), he presses the cord into Zoser's hand, and Zoser, who cannot remember the lessons but can read his own hand, reads the name aloud: the last word Humbert hears [R2-psych-10].
- **Angeska's two letters.** (1) A letter from Cairo to the master of the *Santa Maria*: "four came aboard at Alexandria, as was written" (I·6). This is "I wrote to the captain." (2) The A. packet for the Humbert, given to the mate at Alexandria, promising Aelis. Humbert keeps it unread in I·6 for fear it holds her death, and reads it that night, off the page.
- **Angeska's roll of the dead [R2-psych-05].** Each night she says under her breath the names of the Wardens of Aksum who died before her, about nine, ending in her teacher's; flames near her stand still (the Standing). She adds Desta (I·9) and Tamrat (I·19), is silent after him (I·25), and on the barge her mouth makes a cento sound instead of Zoser's name (I·31). In II she recovers the list; the last name she speaks before the birth is Zoser's, as a living man's. Her own POV only.
- **Abba Talka's price [R2-dread-06].** He lit Amara's fire and paid with his sister **Elleni**, d. famine spring 1310, who brought him bread at the gate. He does not know she existed. See `magic-system.md` §2.
- **Tamrat.** Father **Habta Maryam**. His tell from I·7: the thumb of one hand rubbing the hollow of the other palm, the deacon's wipe turned on himself [R2-psych-02]. He keeps an arm's length from every flame and does not let himself think why [R2-dread-01]. The splinter turns toward the boy at night [R2-dread-02]. In I·11 he chooses to confirm Zoser's lie: "Yes. I ordered it." [R2-psych-03].
- **Cardinal Giovanni Orsini's motive [R2-psych-08].** A Roman whose city has been a widow for twenty years. He wants a relic so great that the Pope must come home to receive it. He feeds Rome's poor with his own hands and starves strangers on Humbert's word ("I feed Rome, Father. You are not Rome"). Feeding them after Angeska's night bargain is his first disobedience to Humbert. He sells the survivors, and his dream, to Humbert, and ends Book I as one more Italian petitioner at the Rhône; his candle at Notre-Dame-des-Doms is for Rome. Historical anchor kept: Giovanni Gaetano Orsini, papal legate in Italy; Ludwig's descent on Rome in 1327–28 is real.
- **Ser Matteo of Mazara.** Giovanni's Sicilian notary-clerk who reads Arabic (I·8 collation, I·11 minute) [R2-lit-02, R2-lit-04].
- **Dimiana of Aswan.** Coptic widow who keeps the pilgrims' hostel in Trastevere; speaks Arabic and the Nubian of the First Cataract; keeps a diptych of the departed, in which Tamrat writes the names of all three (I·7) [R2-myth-05].
- **Bishoy of Akhmim.** The Coptic pilgrim of Cassino; lodges at Dimiana's; stands at the bridge (I·17); carries the stufa letter to Egypt (I·19) [R2-cont-04].
- **Fra Raimon of Albi.** Dominican, the one survivor of the six friars of 1324; heard Gebre's palm-confession; now a sworn Humbert in the Cardinal's household. White scar through one eyebrow. Spits after naming Zoser (I·22); among the masked Roman chapter (I·32) [R2-psych-09].
- **Desta** had watched the King's baggage for twenty years before the embassy (I·4); the Prologue's sandal in the court is **not** his (ledger #56: ruled in Round 3 as Elleni's, §12).

## 12. Round 3 rulings (Editor-in-Chief)

### 12.1 People

- **Elleni and the sandal [R3-cont-09].** Talka's younger sister, married in the town below the churches, brought him a loaf at the monastery gate for twenty years; he cut the heel off first and handed it back to her over the gate. On the night of the burning (1310) she crossed the court before the bell with the last of her bread, saw what her brother was lighting, and turned back: the sandal that "stopped, and did not come on". The fire had already taken her from him. At first light her loaf lay on the court stones under her woven red-and-black mesob lid; Talka cut it with the horn-handled knife and fed the boy. Zoser's first bread at Lalibela was hers. She died in the town within the week. Gebre knows; he is mute; never stated in prose. The ceiling records only those under its roof, so she has no witness line.
- **Hadera** is Angeska's teacher, the last name of her litany. She has no presence at Lalibela in 1310 (R3-mystery-01 declined).
- **Abba Hiruy**, guardian of Zion at Aksum, Talka's fellow novice; blind "from the rains" of 1325; struck from the roll of Zion's keepers by Talka's finding; asks only "Tell them I did not sleep"; dies at dawn on 17 March 1326 [R3-lit-01, R3-psych-05].
- **Cencio**, the Orsini turnkey of Castel Sant'Angelo (I·13–18): sells the prisoners light by the inch, tells the castle's lore, hears the sentence, loses the priest's name to Zoser's question, and that night sells the Moor-saint's ash on the bridge: the man who sold the light sells the erasure [R3-lit-02, R3-myth-07, R3-dread-05].
- **Tamrat's last chapters [R3-psych-02, R3-psych-03, R3-mystery-08, R3-myth-05, R3-dread-07].** In I·14 he gives the wood to the boy deliberately (Genesis 22:6 in Ge'ez; two fingers to the throat), knowing both that the plank's light passed the boy and that the thing turned toward him every night. In I·15 he answers "God will see to it." At the stake he refuses the garrotte offered for the sorcerer-boy's name and answers the forgotten question with his own name, which turns the core black; the price is the gursha his wife fed him; he dies believing she wanted a child of him. He asks "What did it say with my mouth?" and learns it took his joke.
- **Angeska [R3-psych-01, R3-psych-07].** Her silence when Zoser asks that she carry the Key (I·15) is a choice with a price: the knife at I·25. Her care and her plan show in the same coins: the boy's shoes (I·19), the candle for the dead man's psalter, the fare kept exact, the shoes unlaced at the chamber's threshold (I·27) and left outside the chapel (I·32).
- **Cardinal Giovanni [R3-psych-08].** He ends Book I with a coward's kindness: the safe-conduct he wrote for the woman, her name scraped out, the bearer's line left blank, sent "for the Ethiopian scribe". In Book II Zoser leaves Avignon on it and chooses Florence. Giovanni never learns it was used.
- **Cardinal Humbert [R3-psych-09].** "Did you keep something of hers?" (I·22) is empathy and the order's hunt in one sentence; he lets the boy's "Nothing, Eminence" pass, and in I·32 keeps silent while his own chapter fails to find the strip: he keeps the boy's mother's thing as he kept his own mother's key. Master of the order from c.1310.
- **Ser Matteo of Mazara [R3-mystery-04, R3-lit-08].** At the burning he renders the garrotte offer into Arabic and crosses himself small and exact; he wrote the minute of the burning in advance, and its name went. Against orders he kept a leaf, *Quae audita sunt et non scripta*, ending *Scripsi, ne oblivisceremur*: the series' first record made by an outsider to the cult purely so as not to forget (VII).
- **Brother Anselm [R3-mystery-05].** His prickings begin the hour the name refuses ink (twelve beside the blank in the steward's register, I·20).

### 12.2 Institutions, objects, places

- **The Key lives in the scrip's lining**, beside the strip, never the tunic [R3-cont-05].
- **The Aksum stone [R3-myth-01].** A three-band throne-base: Greek, Ge'ez, and lowest the leaning warden-signs. "Son of Mahrem, who is not conquered by the enemy" was chiselled out of the upper two under a cross; in the third, which nobody could read, the name stands: what no one can read cannot be erased. Mahrem is a war god and the kings' divine father (E067), never "the Sky God". In-world object.
- **Talka's finding at Aksum [R3-lit-01].** *Nothing is wanting*: the Ethiopian church's minute, matching Rome's minute that will not hold a name.
- **The sentence at the cell door [R3-cont-03].** Read on Spy Wednesday by a notary of the Capitol after the city's judges found sorcery on the Cardinal's referral; Tamrat relaxed to the secular arm, to be burned after the Easter octave, at the Orsini bridge-head (private justice).
- **The Humbert mask [R3-cont-07].** Names of past wearers engraved and scraped at each death; a polished space for the next; the uncounted Clerk's name is never engraved; in 1897 the blank still fogs.
- **Desta's sword's tag [R3-dread-08]:** *XL. Aethiops, miles. Mare Siculum. Non portabat.*
- **The road to Avignon** runs by the Via Francigena to Lucca, the coast road, Aix and Tarascon, then up the Rhône bank [R3-lit-07]. At Tarascon, the **Tarasque** (Saint Martha's beast, led on her girdle, stoned by the men of the town): with Arwe, the series' two mythic models of how a devourer ends (`series-outline.md` B.8.16) [R3-myth-08].
- **Faras [R3-myth-10].** The cathedral of Faras in Nubia, its bishops painted with their names beside their faces, sanded over and forgotten; read again by the 1961–64 excavation, its frescoes cut free and carried to Warsaw and Khartoum months before Lake Nasser behind the Aswan High Dam drowned the site and the Nubian villages were resettled. Planted by Dimiana in I·19; paid in the Observer's present or a late Keeper's item; in VII the first voices of the river tongue that return to Zoser through the Kernel are these named bishops. Treat the resettlement with specificity and respect; no spectacle.
- **Castel Sant'Angelo [R3-myth-07].** The jailer's tales of the angel sheathing its sword when a plague stopped, and of the emperor whose coffin-lid went to a pope's tomb at the Lateran (Innocent II, the pope of the 1130 schism: Book II's prologue).
- **Ponte Sant'Angelo in 1326** has no angel statues (Bernini's are 1669; Peter and Paul 1534): the Orsini bridge-head tower and the river mills.
