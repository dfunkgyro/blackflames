### A.2 Rationale

**Proposal B (mystery): 9/10. Winner, and the spine of the series.**
- *Why it wins.* It is the only design in which the final revelation reaches back to page one and changes how every earlier book reads. That is the brief's "puzzles perfectly built and fair". The Name Ladder is the best device any proposal offered. Five closing ornaments in scripts that run backwards toward the first alphabet spell **AMARA**. The fair misdirect **AMR** ("the decree") arrives in Book IV. It turns Zoser's per-book cost (the loss of his mother) into a puzzle the reader can solve before he does. The Curator's Scars (one forged document per book, each with three tells built from the manuscript's own historical errors) answer the audit's §3I directly. The Keeper's catalogue frame grows from dry provenance into a daughter's grief. Its chapter-1 sample paragraph is the best prose in the three submissions. "The Entity is the archive of the erased" is the most morally serious cosmology offered: monster and victim at once.
- *What it costs.* Rosa is a POV only in III and VII, which breaks E100 (the protagonists must recur). Captain Humbert = Cardinal Humbert strains logistics: a sitting cardinal of Avignon running a cargo ship in winter. Its climax in a Roman crypt is less resonant than a homecoming. It also leaves Abba Talka's home front thin. All four are fixed below by grafts.

**Proposal A (saga): 8.5/10. The richest grafts.**
- *Strengths.* The best character engine: Zoser and Rosa as opposite costs (he forgets, she cannot forget). The best single twist in any proposal: Angeska's letter was **mistranslated**. The warden verb Beatrice rendered "kill" also means "let die / release", so the translator's last word is mistranslated. Other strengths: Abba Talka's home-front POV with a two-continent midpoint; Gebre's prologue, which passes every first-chapter test with a POV who *wants* something; the Clerk's epilogue ("There is always a Humbert"); the Charter Day transfer rite, in which Zoser's freedom and the world's danger are one transaction; and the Aksum/Lalibela homecoming after Adwa.
- *Weaknesses.* The Name as the Il Moro's own name is abstract; the reader cannot feel it. The Interregna add a second first-person frame voice alongside the Witness's POV, which would be one frame too many beside B's Keeper.

**Proposal C (mythic): 8/10. The magic system wins outright.**
- *Strengths.* Concrete visuals: negative-space flame, smoke that falls before it rises, ash that settles into script, white fire under which things cast no shadow. Inquisitors losing their shadows, which gives the **shadow test**. The **ledger technique**: write down the memory before the burn and read it back after, which makes "*Cuida nuestra historia*" an operating instruction. The five-part Rite grammar with seven witnesses taxed of memory. Splinters with functions (the Nail that binds, the Nib that writes law into bodies). The Entity's life cycle as a measure of escalation. Four Cycle Parts per book (DIVISION, DECREE, CHAIN, ASH), with a fifth, RECORD, in Book VII only. The Copyist who burns her own name and so becomes the undying unseen Witness. Ramkanai's forged Bengal ledger.
- *Weaknesses.* The Name as "an index of erased names" (D for Desta, B for Bellini) is unsolvable and arbitrary. Leaving Desta "never confirmed" withholds a payoff. Keeping "Sindical" contradicts the bible's hygiene ruling. It has the thinnest prose sample.

### A.4 Showrunner rulings that settle the conflicts between proposals

1. **Captain Humbert and Cardinal Humbert are two men under one name-in-office.** The Cardinal of Toulouse is the current master of the Silence-Keepers. Every hunter he sends takes the name "Humbert", as his own master's men did. The Captain is a defrocked Silence-Keeper hunter. Book I's clue chain is Desta's sword on the Cardinal's study wall, the shared signet ring of the order, and the shared habit of spitting. The bible's C4 stands.
2. **The Name is AMARA** (B). Mechanism: the Entity has no name of its own; inside each host it **spells itself out of what it takes from that host**. In Zoser it eats his mother, letter by letter. The oldest stratum of the erased it holds is the **Il Moro**, unmade long ago by the Wardens of Aksum (A). That is the Wardens' secret guilt and Angeska's grey motive.
3. **Angeska's letter is mistranslated** (A). Beatrice reads "kill"; the verb means "end / release / let die". The full sense, reconstructed in Book VII: *"Rosa: let Zoser die, when he remembers who he is, and not before."*
4. **The ending is a willing black burning, not a killing** (B, C and the bible's §6.4 rule 6). Killing a vessel releases the Entity. A vessel who **chooses** a black burning while remembering who he is **records** it instead. Rosa lights the fire, and so obeys the letter as it was meant.
5. **The climax is at Bet Maryam, Lalibela, March 1896**, days after Adwa, where Amara burned (A and C). The series' first and last fires burn in the same church.
6. **There are seven fragments plus Fragment 0** (B's frame, C's functions). Full table in `magic-system.md` §5.
7. **Frame devices are limited to two.** (1) The **Keeper's catalogue entries**, about 150 words each, open and close each book: Rosa, Rome, 1897, signed "B.". (2) **Embedded documents** inside chapters. There are no separate first-person Interregna. Gap centuries are crossed by documents the Keeper catalogues. The Witness (Zaynab) speaks in **close-third POV chapters** under the epithet THE LAST VOICE from Book III.
8. **"Sindical" is retired.** The negation has no proper name: each people names it in its own tongue. Jay calls it "Brother" (B; bible hygiene ruling).
9. **Rosa's rule** (A + B + C). See `magic-system.md` §7. She must take a dying person's last breath about once a year or age toward her true age. She keeps every memory she takes, and cannot forget. Each breakthrough of insight costs an unchosen death near her (the **tithe**). Over the centuries she learns to feed only on the consenting dying, with one lapse, in Bengal in 1770.

## PART B — THE DEFINITIVE SERIES

### B.1 Series title logic

**THE BLACKFLAMES.**
1. **The fire.** A black flame is fire that **records**. Whatever it burns leaves its true text in the ash. Its twin, the white flame, erases. They are one fire, and its colour depends on whether the burner remembers or denies.
2. **The people.** The Silence-Keepers' files never use the resisters' names. They label them *flammae nigrae*, "the blackflames": the dangerous rememberers. The label lands on Amara, the Black cardinals, the Crescent women, the maroons, the Keepers of Shadows. In Book VII the rememberers take it as their own name.
3. **The dead.** A person burned in black flame becomes a legible page. The title is therefore also a roll of the recorded dead, and only the last book shows that its final entry is Zoser himself.
4. **The object.** The seven novels are presented in-world as the reassembled record the black flames preserved, catalogued by the Keeper. The reader holds the ledger the Entity was built to prevent.

**Book titles** name each era's spell-object: relic → body → bull → ledger → charter → ships → book.

### B.2 The spines (what makes seven books one story)

1. **Zoser's bond to the Wound.** He surfaces "once per generation" (l.1770). Each book costs him one memory of his mother (Books I–V), then everything else. *His freedom and the world's danger are the same transaction.* Every loosening of the Entity's hold on him (Charter Day 1793) makes it harder to kill.
2. **Forgetting against remembering.** Zoser forgets because time does not write on him. Rosa cannot forget, because she keeps everyone she consumes. The series ends when the two costs are traded: Rosa gives back what she holds, and Zoser remembers enough to burn.
3. **The Name Ladder.** One closing ornament per book, I–V: አ (Ge'ez), M (Latin uncial), ا (Arabic *alif*), ר (Hebrew *resh*), 𐤀 (proto-Sinaitic *ʾalp*). Read as A-M-A-R-A. Solvable from Book V; spoken in Book VII.
4. **The fragment hunt.** Fragments 0–7 drive each book's micro-arcs. At the series midpoint (end of IV and V) the heroes learn the fragments are *seeds* that make vessels, and that hunting them has fed the enemy. They also learn the fragments are the only wood that burns *completely* black. From then on they gather them as **fuel**.
5. **The Cycle.** Each book has four Parts: **DIVISION, DECREE, CHAIN, ASH**. Book VII adds a fifth, **RECORD**.
6. **The Curator's Scars.** One forged document per book, each with three tells. Matthias names all seven in Book VII, and each identification restores an erased truth.
7. **The Keeper's catalogue.** Rosa (as "B.", the Bellini mask) writes a dry entry that opens and closes each book. It is written in 1897, after the end; the reader does not learn this until Book VII. **[R2-lit-06] Seven items a book, forty-nine in all.** Each book carries seven numbered items: the front matter, one on each Part-title page, and the back matter (Book I: front 1 + four Parts + back 2; Book VII: front 1 + five Parts + back 1). Numbering runs continuously: Book II's front matter opens "Item the eighth". The forty-ninth is "Item the last: you". The blank ledger page after it is the fiftieth: the jubilee of Leviticus 25, when slaves go free and land returns to its people. Never glossed. The Keeper's cold-but-tender count shadows the Entity's INVENTORY (I·28) across the series.
8. **The witness frame [R2-lit-10].** Every book's prologue and epilogue belong to a witness the record does not count, who saw what the main text cannot hold. Frame chapters are short (1,500–3,500 words), always contain one physical record the witness makes or keeps (a palm, a wax tablet, a burial book, a bow inlay), and never explain the main plot. Provisional mapping, binding when each PLAN is written: I, Gebre / the Clerk; II, the 1130 prologue keeps Pietro Pierleoni's action but is told from, or ends on, the uncounted lamp-boy who trims the candles at Haimeric's binding and later writes the 1190 seal-crack memorandum; epilogue Beatrice; III, epilogue to the convent lay-sister who keeps the burial book; IV, the Last Voice; V, Kwame (prologue) and an enslaved porter at Leadenhall Street on Charter Day; VI, Henry, the young soldier who saw Chaguaramas burn black and was told it was the rum; VII, the coda's Last Voice, named at last. In VII's anti-Rite, Desta Wolde's reading of the ceiling includes the witness lines of Gebre and the Clerk. **[R3-cont-09]** Book I's frame gains a third uncounted witness inside the Prologue itself: the sandal that "stopped, and did not come on" (Elleni, who has no witness line because she was not under the roof).
9. **The bracket [R3-lit-09].** Each book's climactic Rite or tribunal is preceded by an official record drafted in advance by the institution that stages it, and each carries `[ ]` (one space inside) where Zoser's name would stand, because the excommunication "from the order of creation" stops every later record from holding him. Provisional ladder, binding when each PLAN is written: I, the arrest clause (I·11), the sentence that names only two (I·13), the steward's register (I·20) and the Lectio's minute (I·29); II, the Florence tribunal's sentence of 1439 drafted for the legates of Eugene IV and of Basel ("the said vagrant [ ], a Moor"); III, the physicians' report on Innocent VIII's last bleeding ("blood of two donors, [ ] and a woman"); IV, the 1612 warrant for the crescent house ("a blackamoor called [ ]"); V, the Court of Directors' minute for Charter Day 1793, the transfer pre-resolved and the transferor left blank; VI, the 1795 covenant's muster roll, fifty thousand counted and one entry blank; VII, no minute. The RECORD Part is the only record in the series written after the event, by the uncounted, and its last line fills the bracket: `[Zoser, son of Amara]`. Matthias collates the six brackets beside the five ornaments: the blank that waits and the letters that answer. The Keeper never comments on it.

### B.8 Round 1 threads (Editor-in-Chief) — binding across the books

Each id is summarised in `series/ideas-register.md`. Book I execution is in `books/book-01/PLAN.md`.

1. **The Line** [R1-mystery-04, R1-lit-05]. The first line of Amara's strip is the final law in the warden-script, and its verb is the root Beatrice renders "kill". Each book gives **one new rendering**, set as **the Gloss**, a fixed four-column table SIGN | SOUND | CANDIDATES | CHOSEN [R2-lit-03], from a POV with a motive (each POV fills the columns with its own motive: Beatrice strikes nothing and chooses "kill"; the Company's reading in V adds a fifth column, TERMS, a formal tell of its corruption; Matthias's gloss in VII carries all six earlier choices in CANDIDATES, unstruck, and the RECORD Part's final row strikes none of them): I (Zoser, "Burn what you carry, while you remember whose you are"; Angeska's correct copy in I·22, withheld); II (Beatrice, halting, gets the verb as "kill"); III (Rosa, by pattern); IV (Isabella's grandmother, who reads it as a recipe and so half-invents the ledger technique; Rosa meets the two-meaning root at IV·~18); V (Blackwood's or the Company's self-serving reading: "release" as a contract); VI (Nafisa, with the Rosetta key); VII (Matthias, then the RECORD Part: the bush that burned and was not consumed). In VII it is the instruction Rosa and Zoser finally obey.
2. **Counting against naming** [R1-lit-09]. One dramatised beat per book in which a document reduces people to a number and a POV answers with a name. I: "seven, no more, no fewer"; the victory register against the Ifat captive's whispered names; the jeli. II: the 1348 plague-pit tallies against Bellini's list of who ate at her table. III: the bull's "Saracens and pagans" against Tewolde naming the chained envoys aloud. IV: the Grim Tally against Isabella's cipher ledger. V: the Bengal granary figures against Ramkanai's forged names. VI: the covenant's "fifty thousand" against the names on the burning water. VII: Berlin's arithmetic and Omdurman's "ten thousand against forty-eight" against the black-fire roll at Lalibela. The Last Voice returns Desta's joke at the climax: the man who could not count is the one who is named.
3. **The Clerk as a Humbert** [R1-mystery-06]. Brother Anselm, who forged Scar I in I·Epi, wears the mask in II and hunts Zoser: the one hunter who saw the Rite, is complicit in it, and secretly records it. VII·~18: Matthias finds that Scar I's tells were planted on purpose (his stylus prickings spell MEMINI). Every Scar may carry deliberate dissent.
4. **Gebre and the living ceiling** [R1-lit-04]. II (reported by Giyorgis in Florence, 1441, or in one short Ethiopian chapter): Gebre asks to be burned in Bet Maryam as Amara was; the fire goes black; his ash joins her text; from then on the ceiling keeps writing Zoser's unseen life in every script he passes through. VII: Desta Wolde reads to Zoser not only Amara's name but his own lost centuries, ending in the Amharic of 1896.
5. **Lalibela's escalation** [R1-cont-09]. I ends with a white-fire burning of Bet Maryam's sister church in the eastern marches by a raiding party a masked stranger has bought. II's Ethiopian thread (through Giyorgis) follows the Silence-Keepers' war on Amara's record at home.
6. **The Avignon letter** [R1-cont-04]. Zoser's undelivered letter of Christmastide 1326 surfaces in II (carried home in 1441 or found in Humbert's papers) and is read at Lalibela a decade late.
7. **The second Desta reveal** [R1-mystery-01]. IV·~22, when the crescent tokens explain that Fragment 0 hides its bearer: the candle could never have seen Zoser's scrip; Desta died for the boy, by Talka's charge, and miscounted. Ledger #4b.
8. **The false choice** [R1-psych-02]. In each of I–V Zoser believes he chooses to save one memory over his mother's; the reader knows (I·28) the mother's was struck first. VII's read-back frees him of the guilt. Ledger #50.
9. **The psalter's provenance** [R1-lit-06]. Avignon (1328) → Lalibela via Giyorgis (1441) → Tewodros's Maqdala (1860s) → looted by Napier's expedition (April 1868) and shipped to London → stolen back by Rosa → in Zoser's hands at Bet Maryam (VII). The real loot and museum history stay unchanged; only this book's hidden meaning is invented. It answers Book I's central question ("who owns holiness?") with a live restitution debate.
10. **Restored Leaves** [R1-lit-07]. VII's RECORD Part includes three embedded documents headed RESTORED LEAF, ADDED BY THE KEEPER (600–900 words each), in Rosa's hand from Angeska's inherited memory. Each begins with the exact last line of a Book I cut (I·9, I·18, I·25) and completes the scene. I·9's answer: "I want to be the last of my order who has to choose." I·25: she weeps as she cuts, and leaves the strip because she recognises a Warden-taught hand. They complicate, never excuse.
11. **The unfinished scar** [R1-myth-01]. The cut below Zoser's collarbone is the one text on his body the Entity cannot overwrite. The last line of the vellum is written beside it (VII).
12. **The mythic solution** [R1-myth-07]. "You do not kill a devourer. You give it something it cannot swallow" (the tale of Arwe, I·12) rhymes with VII: Zoser, recording the Entity in black flame, is the thing it cannot digest.
13. **The Keeper's calendar** [R1-mystery-08]. Every K entry carries a Ge'ez-numeral Ethiopian date. VII·~22: when Rosa is revealed as the Keeper, a character converts the dates aloud in one line.
14. **Uncounted witnesses** [R1-mystery-05]. Every later Rite has a built-in loophole: those the record does not count keep their memory (Zaynab after 1492; the enslaved at Alcáçovas; the Merikin wives at the deeds).
15. **Trauma** [R1-psych-09]. Zoser does not name what was done to him until III; it shows in behaviour (`bible.md` §10.1).
16. **Two endings for a devourer** [R3-myth-08, R1-myth-07]. Ethiopia's Arwe (I·12: "give it something it cannot swallow"; the recording, black ending) and Provence's Tarasque (I·20: led meek on a woman's girdle and stoned by the men of the town; the erasing, white ending). Angeska is about to lead a devourer on her girdle. Book II's angel weeping black tears on Saint Martha's statue (l.1442) lands on a figure the reader has met. VII chooses Arwe.
17. **Habits with no owner** [R3-mystery-02, R3-psych-06]. A black-fire price takes the memory and leaves the habit. Taught on Talka (I·4–30), applied to Zoser from I·33: every later loss is first shown as a habit with no owner (a way of folding bread, a hymn's cadence, a knot, the hand that wipes a letter in the dust). Rosa sees the wiped letter in II and recognises it in VII; Rosa and Matthias audit his losses by his habits.
18. **Matteo's leaf** [R3-mystery-04, R3-lit-08]. The record made against orders "so that we do not forget" (I·24) is found by Matthias in the opened papal archives after 1870 and read into the RECORD Part beside Teodoro's Testament: the Bellini archivist's fourteenth-century predecessor.
19. **Faras** [R3-myth-10]. The painted bishops of the river people (planted I·19) return in the Observer's present (the 1961–64 rescue of the frescoes and the drowning of the site under Lake Nasser) or as a late Keeper's item (*Item: a bishop's face cut from a wall, with his name beside it in a tongue its last speakers were moved away from.*), and as the first voices of the river tongue in VII.

### BOOK I — THE FRAGMENT BEARER
**Era.** Prologue c.1310 (Lalibela); winter 1325 to 1328: the Ethiopian embassy, Rome with no pope, Avignon under John XXII, Amda Seyon's war with Ifat at home.
**Central question.** *Who owns holiness, and what do you owe the man who stole it for you?* (Tamrat's "question", l.747.)

**POV cast (7 + prologue + epilogue)**

| POV | Urgent want | Arc / fate |
|---|---|---|
| GEBRE (prologue), deacon of Bet Maryam | Finish a forbidden burning before the matins bell without betraying a dying woman's son. | Lies to his abbot; his palm is stained with letters. Mute afterwards; speaks once, at the end. |
| ZOSER, scribe, 21 | Get the embassy to the Pope alive; keep his mother's strip hidden; earn his place. | Courage at sea → bearer of the Key → betrayed, violated, rewritten, dead and returned. |
| TAMRAT, Qess priest, 64 | Be received as Ethiopia's voice; keep what is in his belly hidden. | Buffoon → coward → penitent → martyr who turns his execution into testimony. **Dies in his own POV chapter.** |
| ANGESKA, translator, 30 | Bring the relic to Avignon intact, on her own terms. | Her chapters never lie and never enter the rooms that would expose her. Unmasked at the Rite. |
| ABBA TALKA, Lalibela | Keep the court's faith in the embassy while Ifat raids grow; hide the Aksum theft from the emperor. | Learns the thief was his own priest, and that he sent the boy as courier *and* bait. |
| CARDINAL GIOVANNI ORSINI, Rome | A relic great enough to bring the Pope home to Rome; Avignon's favour as the road to it [R2-psych-08]. | Catches Angeska's softened translations; burns Tamrat; sells the survivors on in a night bargain. |
| CARDINAL HUMBERT OF TOULOUSE, Avignon | The Ark to fund the *passagium* to Acre; Zoser as his prophet; his own long life. | Master of the Silence-Keepers. Hires Angeska as seer; she takes over his rite. Ends the book drugged, shaking, hunting. |
| THE CLERK (epilogue) | Survive what he saw. | The only one of the seven witnesses who remembers. Handed the iron mask. |

**Antagonist force.** The Latin Church as a power of decree ("Justice is whatever the Church decrees", l.476). The Silence-Keepers under the Humbert name. The veiled man: the previous vessel reaching through Tamrat. Angeska, hidden in plain sight.

**Arc.** The embassy is broken, and so is Zoser. He sails as a pack-bearer who resents his master. He answers a killing question to save that master. He carries a theft he did not commit, defends a woman who is betraying him, watches the man he resented become a saint, and is turned by a rite into a door. By the end he has lost Desta, Tamrat, Angeska, his body's time, and the first memory of his mother.

**Micro-arcs.** The plank. Angeska hires the ship. Tamrat hides his cramps. Talka's riders. Rome without a pope. The translation duel. The veil. The vomited Key. The bridge-head. The overnight bargain and the bath. Desta's sword on the Cardinal's wall. The thirteenth-night theft. The Rite in four movements. "What key?"

**Midpoint twist (two continents, one week).** In Castel Sant'Angelo Tamrat vomits a pulsing splinter of acacia: *he* robbed the sanctum at Aksum, and Desta died for his theft. In Lalibela, Abba Talka reads Amara's black ash and learns three things. The thief was a priest of his own church. "A woman of the [ — ] will [ — ] a priest for the sanctum": a gapped warden root Talka reads as *bind* and the reader, at I·27, as *wed* [R2-mystery-09]. And the Silence-Keepers have known about Amara since 1310.

**Climax.** The Black Rite, staged as a Lectio Divina in four movements. "I led you" (l.1025). The Key is melted from gold to red to white and forced into Zoser three times. He dies and returns. The violation is written as violation: drugged paralysis, dissociation, theft of self. Its consequence is shame, silence, and the Church reading it as his sin.

**Antagonist force.** The Company, now an immortal Charter. **Obinna**, its human face, revealed. The covenant of "fifty thousand" Black seamen and soldiers, a contract with teeth (l.4865). **Midpoint twist (Bridgetown, then Trinidad, 1796–97).** The one Bridgetown reveal (E025): "black ships don't dock here". Obinna's money is the Company's (E059), and the fleet's "independence" is a clause in the Charter. The Black Companies, Zoser among them, are sent to crush Fédon's free Black rebels, and Ayo's commune falls to Black soldiers (l.5497). Abercromby breaks the seal on the covenant's full terms. The breakable clause ("My men will turn on you", l.4865) is **bait**: the Company *intends* Britain to break it, because the betrayal will be a feast.

**Climax (Trinidad, 16–18 February 1797).** Apodaca burns his squadron in Chaguaramas Bay. His sailors call out the names of their ships' dead, and the fire turns **black** around the Nib. For one night the names of the drowned of the Middle Passage rise legible on the water. It is the first mass black flame, and it costs Apodaca his memory of his sons. Henry, a young British soldier, sees it; Sergeant Tom calls it the rum, and the "one shot" story survives only as camp legend (E027). Fédon vanishes into the Black Companies who beat him.

**Falling action.** "The Noses of Giza", one dark chapter, with Napoleon's memoir as Scar VI. Abercromby at Alexandria. The **Act of Independence of Haiti** read aloud at Gonaïves on 1 January 1804: the first document in the series that frees instead of binding. The 1807 Act, with an Elias descendant's testimony reaching Clarkson (E055). Diego's heir in a tavern in 1808: Alonso believes Napoleon freed Africa, and Diego knows he restored slavery (E080). Diego ships the White Flame's records to Rome rather than burn them.

**Cliffhanger.** Naparima, 1816. The Merikins land on ground **Fédon secretly held for them**. Adanna founds the Keepers of Shadows. Zoser has forgotten his mother's face, voice, nickname and name, and asks Adanna to keep one memory for him: "that she burned." Keeper's entry: *Item: a land deed, Company Villages, 1816. The ink moves.* There is no ornament; the ladder is complete.

**Curator's Scar VI.** A chapter of the St Helena memoir: "how I undid the slavery". Tells: "Your Majesty" in 1798; Napoleon at the Rosetta Stone; the Continental System dated 1804 (E080, E081).

### BOOK VII — THE BOOK OF NAMES
**Era.** Prologue 1808 is folded into VI's falling action. Main action 1816 – March 1896: the Company Villages; apprenticeship and the forged-deed land grab (c.1840); Porta Pia and the opening of the Papal archives (20 September 1870); the dissolution of the East India Company (1874); the Berlin General Act (26 February 1885); Adwa (1 March 1896); Lalibela. Coda: Omdurman (1898) and a reading room in 2024.
**Central question.** *Can a wheel be broken instead of turned, and can the erased be remembered without being owned?*

**Five Parts:** DIVISION, DECREE, CHAIN, ASH, **RECORD**.

**Antagonist force.** **The Cycle** itself: deeds, debt and apprenticeship, "slaves who don't even know they're slaves" (l.6355). The **General Act of Berlin** as a double Rite (fourteen powers sign; the witnesses are taxed). The last Humbert. The Compass, wearing the Resistance as a mask. The Entity reaching for the telegraph: "rituals… into circuits" (l.2884).

**Midpoint twist (Rome, 1881–85: the series' central reveal, in three movements).**
1. Matthias assembles the Curator's Scars and the five ornaments and reads **AMARA**.
2. Zoser holds the Obee seed (Fragment 7, petrified acacia) and hears not one voice but thousands: the Il Moro, Desta, the three boys, Granada's books, Kwame's shipmates. **The Entity is the archive of the erased.** Every manifestation reads differently afterwards: "a body like a storm of screams" (l.2794), "faces that screamed silently" (l.2665), "We are One" (l.4712). Its hunger is the hunger of the erased to be recorded, twisted so that it can grow only by erasing others. Its oldest layer is the Il Moro, unmade by the Wardens of Aksum.
3. The Keeper gives her name: Rosa. Using Matthias's paleography and the Rosetta key from VI, she reads her mother's letter **in the original**. The verb Beatrice rendered "kill" means "end / release / let die". *"Let Zoser die, when he remembers who he is, and not before."* For 442 years a nun's halting translation has stood between father and daughter.

**Climax (Bet Maryam, Lalibela, March 1896, days after Adwa).** Ethiopia is the "one tiny spot of independence" (l.6455). Zoser comes home carrying the Key in his body, cross-cut with the General Act's machinery grinding on in Africa. The last Humbert arrives to kill him and release the Entity. A black flame "cannot be lit by one who has forgotten who they are", and Zoser has forgotten. So **the found family reads him back to himself** from the record the reader has held across seven books:
- Imani returns the memory Adanna kept, "that she burned";
- Rosa returns Bellini's broth, and her mother's memory of Zoser before the Rite (taken in 1439);
- Desta Wolde reads aloud the black writing on Bet Maryam's ceiling, which Gebre could not scrape off in 1310. It returns Amara's voice, "my lion", and her name;
- the Last Voice, who walks the archive in both directions, returns Desta's joke on the plank.

The fragments become **fuel**: the Key in his body, the Nail (taken from Humbert), a crescent token, the Company Seal, the spirit-box, the Obee seed. The Nib burned in 1797. Zoser **chooses** the fire, as Desta walked "of his own will". Rosa lights it, obeying her mother's letter as it was meant. He burns black and **into vellum**. Every name the Entity held becomes legible, beginning with **Amara**; the three boys are named and the Blood Thing goes quiet; **Desta** is named. The Entity is not destroyed. It is **turned from a god back into a history**, recorded and therefore finite. The last Humbert, standing too close, is written in with the rest.

**Secret-history rule at the end.** The Act stands, the partition happens, the guns arrive. Omdurman (1898) still kills ten thousand against forty-eight: "Ideas don't stand against maxim guns." The hunger is human now, with no god to hide behind.

**Coda (THE LAST VOICE, 2024).** In a Roman reading room a young archivist of the Bellini line digitises black ash under ultraviolet light. *Rituals finding their way into circuits.* She reads one line aloud: a woman's name, Granadan, written in 1492, **Zaynab**. The figure who has watched since Granada hears her own name for the first time in five centuries, ages, and is gone: *"So that is how it ends. Someone says your name."* The Keeper's final entry, written in 1897: *"They say it lives now in what is deleted without fire. Item the last: you."* The last page is a **blank ledger page** headed *Cuida nuestra historia.*

**Curator's Scar VII.** Varley's land deeds, Company Villages, c.1840. Tells: ink that moves under lamplight; a surveyor who died in 1832; fresh beeswax again.
