# Blackflames — Craft Brief (the standard every agent works to)

Source material: `blackflames.md` (the author's original manuscript, ~112k words). It is the seed and canon of the
Blackflames series. Nothing in it is thrown away lightly: every character, relic, entity, era and thread is raw
material to be honoured, deepened, re-ordered and fixed — not discarded.

## The goal
Turn `blackflames.md` into **The Blackflames series**: an epic dark/high fantasy told across multiple books, each book
continuing from the previous, so that reading all of them tells the whole Blackflames story from beginning to end.
No word limit per book or on the number of books — length is good when it creates a better story. Target: a story a
demanding critic would rate **10/10**.

## Style model
Storytelling inspired by George R. R. Martin's *A Song of Ice and Fire*:
- Rotating close-third-person POV chapters, each titled with the POV character's name; each POV wants something urgent.
- Grounded, morally grey politics; no character is safe; consequences are permanent.
- Dense sensory texture (food, weather, heraldry, smell, cold, rot); history and prophecy dropped as breadcrumbs.
- Magic is rare, dreadful, costly and half-understood; it grows as the series goes on.
- Chapters end on reversals, revelations or choices, not just action beats.
(Inspiration, not imitation: no copied names, phrases or plot beats from Martin.)

## Addictive-fantasy elements (must be present)
1. **High-stakes curiosity and secrets.** Breadcrumb lore about ancient history, hidden magic and forgotten gods. A distinct,
   visual magic system with heavy costs and limits that characters exploit cleverly. Layers of secrets: hidden motives, secret
   identities, past betrayals.
2. **Fast narrative momentum.** Micro-payoffs (short-term goals inside long-term plots). Asymmetric conflict: an underdog
   against overwhelming, competent, terrifying forces. Cliffhangers of consequence: emotional shifts, revelations, choices.
3. **Deep emotional and character anchors.** Found family with banter, loyalty and friction. Earned competence: power
   gained by struggle, failure and blood. Grey morality: dilemmas with no good choice, and villains with empathetic motives.
4. **Visceral world-building and atmosphere.** The world is felt, smelt and tasted, a living character. Pockets of comfort
   (shared meals, humour, sanctuary) that make the danger hit harder.

## Literary ambition
- Lasting literary value and scholarly depth; innovative world-building; speculative depth that stretches the imagination.
- Each book is a rigorous, complex novel that evokes dark emotions and psychological or supernatural dread beyond gore,
  exploring the characters' deepest anxieties.
- Mastery of suspense, procedural accuracy and clever subversion of mystery tropes: puzzles perfectly built and fair,
  characters psychologically real.
- Technically precise, structurally brilliant, tight prose. Artistic merit, literary risk-taking, formal innovation
  (unconventional structures such as embedded documents, archives, journals, bulls, ledgers, testimonies; daring voices).
- Global relevance and many cultural perspectives. Fantasy tropes mirror real-world issues (empire, slavery, faith, erasure,
  exile, revolution, identity) and say something distinct. Thematic urgency: it must feel necessary now.
- Prose that would survive translation: clear, fluid, impactful. Excellent structural editing and pacing; no fluff.
- Never plays safe; masters and redefines genre rules.

## Page-turner mechanics
- **Hook early:** a compelling problem, mystery or threat in chapter 1.
- **Micro-arcs:** self-contained conflicts with regular payoff. End chapters on cliffs: a new question, a revelation or an unresolved choice.
- **Raise the stakes** steadily across chapters and books.
- **Clear, urgent motives.** Force choices between two bad options. Characters are proactive, even when they fail.
- **Strong relationships:** sharp dialogue, hidden secrets, clashing personalities.
- **Cut the fluff:** skip mundane travel and training with time-skips.
- **Lore in drops,** only when it affects the scene or someone's survival.
- **Tight prose:** active verbs, varied sentence length, sensory detail. Show, don't tell.

## First-chapter failure checklist (every book's opening chapter must pass)
- No infodump of lore, family trees or mechanics.
- Not a static opening (alone, window-staring, waking from a dream, reflecting without conflict).
- No name flood: at most 3–4 unique names or terms in the first pages.
- Real stakes: failure changes something or makes it worse.
- No voice disconnect: the opening sounds like the rest of the book.
Hook techniques: in medias res; a question or contradiction on page 1; a clear focal desire; sensory anchors;
a ticking clock; an early subversion of a familiar trope by the chapter's end.

## Rating rubric (the Assurance Checkers score 1–10 on each; the overall score is a weighted judgement, not an average)
Hook and momentum · Character psychology and arcs · Mystery and reveal architecture · Magic system (costs and rules) ·
World-building and atmosphere · Dread and dark emotion · Prose and style (ASOIAF-inspired) · Structure and formal innovation ·
Thematic depth and global relevance · Continuity with canon and series bible.
