# BOOK I — THE FRAGMENT BEARER: Chapter Plan

Series: THE BLACKFLAMES, Book I of VII. Governing files: `series/series-outline.md`, `series/magic-system.md`, `series/mystery-ledger.md`, `series/style-guide.md`, `series/bible.md`, `series/ideas-register.md`. Source lines refer to `blackflames.md`.

**Revision.** Rounds 1–3 (Editor-in-Chief). The accepted ideas are woven into the chapter tables below and tagged by id, e.g. `[R1-lit-01]`, `[R2-myth-05]`, `[R3-cont-09]`. **Round 3:** the plans for Ch.12–16 are consolidated (each replaces its earlier rows; read it alone); Ch.17–33, the Epilogue and the back matter carry a **Round 3** row that adds to, and where it says so overrides, the rows above it. Writers execute every tagged beat in their chapter. The register (`series/ideas-register.md`) gives one-line summaries of each id.

**Canon facts.** `books/book-01/FACTS.md` holds every fixed date, name, object and language rule, with a computed day-by-day chronology. **Every writer checks their chapter against it before delivery and appends any new fixed fact.** Where this PLAN and FACTS.md disagree on a date, FACTS.md wins and the Editor is told.

**Span.** Prologue c.1310; main action winter 1325 to Lent 1328.
**Shape.** Keeper's entry, then Prologue, then 33 chapters in four Parts, then Epilogue, then a closing Keeper's entry.
**Target length.** About 165,000 words (chapters 3,000–7,000 each).
**Central question.** *Who owns holiness, and what do you owe the man who stole it for you?*

## Running clocks and calendars (binding for date lines)

- **The matins bell** (Prologue).
- **The lantern-candle** (Ch.1).
- **Tamrat's cramps** (Ch.3–14).
- **The two fasts** (Ch.5–13) [R1-cont-02]. Easter 1326 fell on **23 March** (Julian, in both the Roman and the Alexandrian reckoning). Latin Lent began on Ash Wednesday, **5 February**. The Ethiopian great fast, *Hudade* (vegan, one meal after the ninth hour), began about a week earlier. Romans and Angeska alike read Tamrat's refusal of food as piety. The reader slowly learns he dare not eat because of what is in his belly.
- **Hunger in Rome** (Ch.7–10) [R2-cont-04]. Engineered: Humbert's "Feed them nothing" (Ch.6, 2 Feb) reaches Orsini about 14 February; from then Orsini men shut the inns to them. Only the Coptic hostel defies it, because nobody thought to send word to Egyptians. The bread that ends the hunger (Ch.9–10) is bought by Angeska from Giovanni, and is his first disobedience to Humbert.
- **The Aksum clock** [R3-cont-01]. The embassy left Lalibela **mid-June 1325**, at the end of the dry season (Ch.4 l.173); the Aksum detour and the theft fall in **late June**, "before the rains"; Massawa in July; Cairo c.20 November ("five months on the road", Ch.2). The guardian opens the inner chest at **Gena, 25 December 1325** (Ch.4 l.309, "twelve days before Timkat"): two days after the plank. The rider reaches Lalibela on Timkat; Talka rides north on the morrow (7 Jan) and reaches Aksum about 19 January. He counts his days at the bed up to forty, the day at home the dead are let go, and stops counting. Never stated: an auditing reader can compute that the theft was found two days after Desta walked off the plank.
- **The candle by the inch** (Ch.13–15) [R3-lit-02]. The turnkey Cencio rents the prisoners a tallow candle marked in knife-nicks, a Roman denaro a nick, paid from Angeska's purse. On Low Sunday night he will sell no new inch at any price (the steward's order: no light for the condemned after compline; they may keep the candle they have, if nobody sees it). In Ch.14–15 each section opens with the bare count in italics (*Four nicks.* … *The stub.* … *Dark.*): the night's only clock.
- **Desta's forty** [R2-cont-01]. The plank is the night of **23 December 1325**. Counting it as day one, Desta's fortieth day (*arba*, when the dead are released) is **31 January**, the Cassino ford (Ch.5). His year-mind (*tazkar*) is 23 December 1326, Night 1 (Ch.20). Never stated; left in the dates for the auditing reader.
- **The dawn of the burning** (Ch.15–17): dawn of **Monday 31 March 1326**, after the Easter octave.
- **The fourteen nights** (Ch.20–29) [R1-cont-01]. They are counted from the night Zoser and Angeska reach the cardinal's palace. **Night 1 is the night of 23 December 1326; Night 14 is the night of 5–6 January 1327, Epiphany Eve.** In Lalibela the same night is the eve of *Timkat* (11 Ter, which falls on 6 January in the Julian reckoning). That night the tabots stand overnight by the water. "Two continents, one night" is chronologically true. Every Avignon chapter's date line counts the night: *Avignon — the ninth night, Christmastide 1326*.

## Binding devices for the whole book

1. **The Lean** [R1-dread-02, R2-myth-01, R2-magic-01]. A flame that senses a fragment or vessel tips like a head turning to listen, holds there, and its tip goes blue. Describe it differently every time; never describe the mechanism. **No flame in Book I may lean, tip and hold, or turn blue except by the Lean. Wind makes flames shrink, smoke and die; it never bends one and holds it.** The reader learns to fear every flame. **Operating rule** (`magic-system.md` §4, never stated in prose): (a) any ordinary flame leans to an unhidden fragment or vessel within about a hand's breadth, and only while it stays that close; a swallowed fragment reads only at that range; (b) a hunter's candle reaches a deck's length: Candlemas beeswax, in the hand of a man who has drunk the Nail-water, with a question put to it; (c) when what is carried **wakes** in its bearer (its sign is **blood from the nose**), every flame in the room answers it, and then answers whatever it attends to; (d) warden-script read aloud is "uncovered", and a fresh copy read aloud by its copier leans flames near it while the ink is wet; (e) a Rite pulls the flames of the place that keeps its record. **Round 3 clause on the strip** [R3-cont-04]: an **unsewn** strip is visible to any flame within a hand's breadth, read aloud or not; only **reading it aloud** makes it answer at a distance, and it stays uncovered from the reading until the reader next sleeps. Zoser does not read the Line aloud again in Book I after the dawn of 12 March. Schedule: Ch.1 (one candle, one man: clause b); Ch.10 (the night-lamp at the narrow door as Tamrat passes within a hand's breadth: clause a); Ch.11 (every candle in the hall leans to Tamrat when the thing wakes, then all swing at once to Zoser, whose strip lies uncovered and unsewn on his skin: clauses c, d); Ch.15 (Zoser carries the vomited splinter to the candle stub: at a hand's breadth the flame lays over and blues, and he snatches it back; after he has sewn Key and strip into the lining, the scrip held at a finger's width leaves the flame upright and yellow: clause a and Fragment 0's rule, both visible) [R3-cont-04]; Ch.22 (the curia's candles lean toward Angeska's sleeve, which holds her wet copies of Amara's script, read under her breath; Zoser blames "the draught from her sleeve": clause d); Ch.30 (every candle in Bet Maryam leans north-west: clause e); Ch.31 (the bargeman's tallow lamp held close to a belly: clause a); Ch.33 (**inverted**: every flame leans *away* from Zoser, the way animals shy, before all but one go out). An **aborted** Lean (a tip that begins to turn and is denied by a step back) is allowed once, in Ch.7 [R2-dread-01]. Tamrat's habit of keeping an arm's length from every lamp is a cumulative tell that Angeska manages: Ch.3 (on the page), Ch.7 (she moves the hostel lamp to the far end of the board "so the old man's eyes can rest"), Ch.13 (in the cell the candle stands on her ledge, and at the Fasika vigil she holds it in her own lap [R3-myth-04]); Ch.14 (she rises and comes to him in the middle of the cell, so that his belly never goes to her corner).
2. **Gebre's palm** [R1-mystery-07, R1-dread-04, R1-lit-04]. Black-flame text about a living person is never finished, and the line printed on Gebre's palm is about the boy. Talka checks it in every Talka chapter "as one takes a pulse". Its current text appears once per chapter as a one-line italic transcription, with unreadable signs as gaps (*[ — — — ]*). Prologue: one line, unreadable. Ch.4: the same line, plus a mark like a wave. Ch.12: a cluster Talka slowly reads as *sea*: *[ — — — — — ] sea*. Ch.16 [R3-mystery-07]: by the one lamp under the ceiling, one new sign at the end of the line, the first Talka can read, the Ge'ez አ, the letter every child makes in the dust: *[ — — — — — ] sea [ — ] አ*; he takes it for the start of a word not yet written. Ch.23: the line runs on from that same አ into a word he reads slowly as *Avignon* (the cliff); the transcription keeps a space after the አ. VII: read whole, the አ stands alone: the fire had begun her name first. Never glossed. Ch.30: see that chapter.
3. **The Entity only quotes** [R1-dread-01]. In Book I the Entity never speaks an original sentence. Every word it says (the veil in Ch.11, Angeska's lacquered mouth in Ch.29, the Latin in Ch.33) is assembled from words already spoken or written near the Key or the strip, given back verbatim in the original speaker's cadence. Writers keep a concordance: every quotation must be sourceable to a line the reader has already read. The table is at the end of this plan. **Round 3** [R3-dread-04]: it quotes **to divide**: every line it speaks cuts one named bond in its hearing (the concordance's WHAT IT CUT column). Its **catchment** in Book I is what is said in a carrier's hearing, waking or asleep; a sleeper takes in a whisper only within an arm's length. A line that cuts nothing, or comes from outside the catchment, is cut.
4. **The excommunication takes, quietly** [R1-dread-05, R2-mystery-06, R2-dread-07]. From Ch.11 the world starts failing to record Zoser. There is never more than one small, deniable detail per chapter, and only in the chapters listed: Ch.11, 13, 15, 20, 21, 23, 33. **Ruling:** it does not alter originals written before Ch.11 (the Ch.7 diptych, the Naples toll-roll); it prevents any record made **after** it from holding him, copies of earlier records included. Ch.21: a Lent fair copy of the Naples toll-roll reads *MAVRI II*. Ch.23: the stufa letter's signature reaches Ethiopia as an empty reed-groove. **Round 3:** Ch.13's proof is **the sentence read at the cell door**, which names two and has nothing where a third name would stand [R3-cont-03]; the breath that leaves no fog is held for Book II. Ch.15's is Tamrat losing the boy's name mid-plea. Ch.20's register blank carries twelve stylus prickings in its margin [R3-mystery-05].
5. **Angeska's nightly word** [R1-psych-05]. From Ch.5 she teaches Zoser one Latin word each night, drawn from a mundane occasion. He cannot hold Latin, so he writes each word in his psalter beside the አ. The list, in order: *clavis* (key, Ch.5, a gate), *ostium* (door, Ch.7), *vas* (vessel, Ch.10, a cooking pot), *germen* (seed or sprout, Ch.13, the last bread), *ignis* (fire, Ch.19), *mater* (mother, Ch.19), *custos* (warden, Ch.22). Read together the words are her plan. In the Rite (Ch.27–29) the Latin is rendered as sound, except for her taught words, which surface in English because she taught them to him. *(Use* germen*, not* semen*: the register of Ch.28–29 must never flirt with the erotic, E092.)* **The trade** [R2-psych-06]: from *vas* (Ch.10) Zoser, stung that he only receives, pays for each Latin word with a word of his own: highland names for things (the dung-and-juniper fire, the hour-star Desta taught him, kolo as Desta said it). She pretends boredom, writes none of them down, and repeats every one back perfectly a week later. Tamrat joins from his corner (Ch.10). For *germen* (Ch.13) Zoser has nothing left to pay with and gives his mother's word for him, the one the text renders "my lion", in the river tongue; she does not repeat it: "Keep that one."
6. **"Wet clay"** [R1-psych-04, R2-dread-04]. Tamrat's insult (l.115) travels: contempt (Ch.2), revision (Ch.3), joke (Ch.5), **theft** (Ch.11: the veil says "Clay carrying the potter" in Tamrat's own strangled ford-voice, as its self-description), blessing (Ch.15: Tamrat takes his word back from the thing), last thought (Ch.17), desecration (Ch.28).
7. **Counting against naming** [R1-lit-09, R2-lit-07, R2-myth-05]. The powerful count; the rememberers name. Book I's beats: Desta's count (Ch.1, 2, 13; in Ch.13 nobody knows what the thirty-nine were called, and the number is not said again), the jeli's names (Ch.2), *MAVRI III* (Ch.5) and *MAVRI II* (Ch.21), "seven, no more, no fewer" (Ch.21), the victory register against the captive's whispered names, and Talka naming Tamrat aloud in the same chapter where he withholds the boy's name and the captive's (Ch.23), and the uncounted Clerk (Ch.27, Epilogue). **Round 3:** Ch.9's petitioner's tally, a count with no name at either end; Ch.13, the sentence names two and the turnkey counts three bowls onto the floor [R3-cont-03]; Ch.13's Fasika kolo, counted by three hands that do not agree [R3-lit-03]; Ch.20, Desta's sword tagged *XL* by the order that killed him, the man who "doesn't do forty" made someone's fortieth (never remarked) [R3-dread-08]. **The single exception:** Anselm's prickings count what will not take ink (twelve beside the blank in Ch.20 = M): the one count in Book I that remembers [R3-mystery-05].
8. **Restored-leaf cuts** [R1-lit-07]. Three Angeska scenes end on an exact, quotable last line, because Book VII will resume each from that line: Ch.9 (on Giovanni's "What do you want, woman?"), Ch.18 (the start of her missing night hours) and Ch.25 (needle and knife). Writers record the exact last line in the chapter's closing HTML comment, `<!-- RESTORED-LEAF CUT: "…" -->`.
9. **The Keeper's calendar** [R1-mystery-08]. The Keeper dates her entries in the Ethiopian reckoning, in Ge'ez numerals: *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* (Yekatit 1889 E.C., which is February 1897). Adwa fell in Yekatit 1888 E.C. A converting reader learns that the Keeper writes after the series ends, in an Ethiopian hand. No gloss is ever given in Book I.
10. **The Standing** [R2-myth-02, R2-psych-05]. The counter-grammar of flame. While the dead are named aloud **with love**, flames within reach stand perfectly still in moving air, "as if set in a closed room", and their smoke rises in unbroken threads. Never explained. Book I: Ch.2 (the jeli, on the page); Ch.9 (Angeska's litany in the cardinal's antechamber); Ch.13 (the cell candle on Fasika night, while they say Desta's name); Ch.23 (the brazier smoke goes up straight in the war-camp wind when Talka speaks Tamrat's name; the Ifat captive's whispered names get no Standing, because no one there names them with love); Ch.30 (the vigil candles by the tabots, before the north-west Lean). Flame leans to seeking and stands for naming; white fire, which throws no shadow, is the third state.

13. **Habits with no owner** [R3-mystery-02, R3-dread-02, R3-psych-05]. Black fire takes the memory and leaves the habit the memory made (`magic-system.md` §2). A loss shows first as a habit whose reason is gone; never explained, never remarked by the narration. Book I: **Talka's heel**. For twenty years Elleni brought him a loaf at the monastery gate and he cut the heel off first and handed it back over the gate to her, the bringer's share. Since 1310 he cuts the heel off first every morning with the horn-handled knife and lays it on the stone by the gate; the novices say it is for the birds (plant: Ch.4, one sentence, set this round). Ch.12: the blind guardian hears the knife: "You still cut the heel first." Ch.16: the knife stops in the loaf at the heel cut. Ch.30: Giyorgis, sent out with it, asks "Who is it for, Abba?", and Talka's mouth gives the restarted verse instead. Ch.33: Zoser's hand lifts gursha to a stranger's mouth, and his palm crosses the inked አ to wipe it (device 14): the same law, which a reader taught by Talka recognises before Zoser does.
14. **The first letter, wiped** [R3-psych-06]. From Ch.15, once in each listed chapter and never in more than one sentence before Ch.33, Zoser draws አ with one finger in the place's own material and wipes it with his palm, "so no priest sees", without thinking why: Ch.15 (the grime of the cell floor, by the stub), Ch.19 (the steam on the stufa's marble), Ch.20 (the ash of the palace hearth on Night 1), Ch.22 (the cold ash of Humbert's lesson-brazier; Humbert sees and asks, and Zoser lies: "A scribe's habit, to warm the hand"), Ch.29 (paralysed, he cannot move the finger; the paragraph is the effort), Ch.33 (the inked አ on the back of his hand: his palm crosses it to wipe it, it does not wipe, and he cannot remember why he wiped). Never glossed.
15. **Every framed document carries its room** [R3-myth-09, Round 2 C5]. Each embedded document gets one physical anchor from the room it is read in, named in its brief: the reader's breath on it, the tallow, the cold on the knuckles, its smell. Book I's fixed anchors: the Aksum finding (Ch.12), the scribe's reed squeaking and rue-and-olive smoke; the sentence at the cell door (Ch.13), the turnkey's lantern and the moat; the ceiling (Ch.16), lime flakes, fifteen years of candle soot, then the treasury smell under the paint; the A. letter (Ch.21), cloves (ten months in his breviary; in Ch.32 the masked chapter finds the breviary by the smell); the stufa letter (Ch.23), eight months folded, worn through at the cross so the brazier shows through the hole, cockled by Red Sea damp, smelling of pilgrims' tar and camel; the honest dossier (Ch.24), mouse and the rust of the masks, gall-ink gone brown, old grey-brown wax that smells of nothing; the corrected dossier (Epilogue), a cake of fresh beeswax warm from a brother's hands that smells of honey for days. The back matter's "Seal of fresh beeswax" then chimes with something the reader has smelt.
16. **Proverbs are quotations** [R3-lit-05]. A POV keeps at most one proverb or maxim a chapter, and it is attributed inside the sentence to a named speaker, preferably dead: Tamrat's to his mother on the lake road and the Tana fishermen; Humbert's to his order's Rule ("Reg. II"); Giovanni's to the canon law by distinction (Gratian). The narration owns none. In Ch.17, for the first time in the book, Tamrat makes a sentence of his own and knows it: "Nobody had said it to him. He said it." (This is the humans' answer to device 3: they quote their dead; the Entity quotes its victims.)
17. **The bracket** [R3-lit-09, R2-lit-04]. Every official record made after Ch.11 that ought to hold Zoser's name shows `[ ]`, one space inside: the arrest clause (Ch.11), the sentence at the cell door (Ch.13: not even a bracket; nothing), the steward's register (Ch.20, "the prophet" and a blank), the Lectio's pre-drafted minute (Ch.29). It is the series' longest spine (`series-outline.md` B.2.9): filled only by the RECORD Part's last line in Book VII.

## FRONT MATTER — The Keeper's entry (about 150 words)

Head it with the catalogue line *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* [R1-mystery-08]. Draft for the writer to polish (keep every plant):

> *Item the first. One sheet of vellum, unusually supple, scorched black at every edge and yet entire. The script is one I was taught to read by my mother, who died in the hour I was born, and whom I have been carrying since. The veins of the hide are visible and do not sit still under the lamp. Provenance: Avignon, by way of Lalibela, by way of a sea I will not name. I have catalogued worse things. I have not catalogued anything I loved.* — B.

**Plants.**
- "Died in the hour I was born, and whom I have been carrying since" [R1-cont-10]: Rosa consumed Angeska at birth (bible C18/C19) and holds her memories.
- The vellum is Zoser. Its words "supple", "veins" and "scorched … yet entire" are echoed by the three Book I clues in Ch.32–33 [R1-mystery-09].
- "B." is the Bellini mask.
- The Ge'ez-numeral date.

No proper names in the body of the entry.

## PROLOGUE — GEBRE

| Focal desire | Gebre, a young deacon, wants the forbidden burning done before the bell, and wants the woman to live. |
| Conflict | Abba Talka orders him to hold her feet ("When she stops, we burn her. Here. Before the bell."). Monks do not burn the dead, never inside a church. At the door stands her son, about seven, his fist closed. In Ge'ez she tells the boy: "Let no priest see it." Gebre is a priest. Under his hands Gebre feels **raised lines on her shins and the tops of her feet**, a deliberate sequence of short and long cuts like a tally [R1-myth-01]. He does not know what they are. | Micro-payoff | She dies; they burn her on the floor. The fire comes up **black**: cold, soundless. Candles lean toward it and gutter. It burns the lines on her legs first, so they show as darker strokes inside the dark flame, and the smoke that climbs and writes on the hewn ceiling repeats their rhythm as letters [R1-myth-01]. Talka hands Gebre a knife: "Scrape it off. Before the bell." The letters re-form under the blade. **Behind his knife a new line grows, letter by letter, under Amara's: his own name, *Gebre*.** The fire has entered him in its record as the witness [R1-dread-03]. He scrapes his name away and it returns, and he keeps scraping until the bell. Ash settles on his lip; he tastes **salt**, and thinks of the woman's tears, though she did not weep [R1-myth-02]. At the last he can read one more word: her name, *Amara*. |

## BACK MATTER — The Keeper's closing entry (about 150 words) and ornament

Head it *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* [R1-mystery-08]. Draft for the writer to polish:

> *Item the sixth: a dossier of the order called the Silence-Keepers, concerning "Angeska, a Nilotic, black-magic born." Addressed to the Holy Father at the Vatican. Seal of fresh beeswax. The hand is fine, with a looped* et*. The facts are not. I have read an older copy that called her what she was. One of these was written in 1326. Item the seventh: a consistory of the year ፲፫፻፳, of which the record keeps no minute. — B.*

Followed by the ornament **አ**, alone on the page.

**This entry presents Scar I.** Its tells: the Vatican in 1326 (the papacy was at Avignon); "Urban or Clement" if the dossier names a pope; fresh beeswax on an "old" document; the looped ligature that matches the Clerk's hand; the contradiction with the dossier the reader saw in Ch.24. The year ፲፫፻፳ (1320 in the Ethiopian reckoning, which is Lent 1328 in the Julian) restores the year Ch.33's date line hides, and reads to a careless eye as an eight-year "error": a deliberate false Scar that teaches the reader to audit dates.

**Round 3** [R3-myth-09]: "Seal of fresh beeswax" now chimes with a smell the reader has met (Epilogue). Keep it.
