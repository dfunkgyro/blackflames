# BOOK I — THE FRAGMENT BEARER: Chapter Plan

Series: THE BLACKFLAMES, Book I of VII. Governing files: `series/series-outline.md`, `series/magic-system.md`, `series/mystery-ledger.md`, `series/style-guide.md`, `series/bible.md`. Source lines refer to `blackflames.md`.

**Span.** Prologue c.1310; main action winter 1325 – Lent 1328.
**Shape.** Keeper's entry, then Prologue, then 33 chapters in four Parts, then Epilogue, then a closing Keeper's entry.
**Target length.** About 165,000 words (chapters 3,000–7,000 each).
**Central question.** *Who owns holiness, and what do you owe the man who stole it for you?*

**POV rotation (counts).**

| POV | Chapters |

| ZOSER | 14 |
| ANGESKA | 5 |

Zoser is never absent for more than four chapters in a row. Tamrat's POV ends with his death (Ch.17). Humbert and Giovanni are antagonist POVs, placed at the turns of the book.

**Book I's Curator's Scar.** The Silence-Keeper dossier on Angeska. The reader reads the *honest* version in Ch.24 (Giovanni's POV). The Keeper's closing entry catalogues the *altered* version. The difference is the first Scar.

**Running clocks.**
- The matins bell (Prologue).
- The lantern-candle (Ch.1).
- Tamrat's cramps (Ch.3–14).
- Hunger in Rome (Ch.7–10).
- The dawn of the burning (Ch.15–17).
- The fourteen nights (Ch.20–29). Every Avignon chapter's date line counts the night: *Avignon — the ninth night, Advent 1326*.

## FRONT MATTER — The Keeper's entry (about 150 words)

> *Item the first. One sheet of vellum, unusually supple, scorched black at every edge and yet entire. The script is one I was taught to read by my mother, who died before I was born. The veins of the hide are visible and do not sit still under the lamp. Provenance: Avignon, by way of Lalibela, by way of a sea I will not name. I have catalogued worse things. I have not catalogued anything I loved.* — B., Rome

**Plants.**
- "Taught by my mother, who died before I was born": Rosa holds Angeska's memories.
- The vellum is Zoser.
- "B." is the Bellini mask.

No proper names in the body of the entry.

## PROLOGUE — GEBRE
*Bet Maryam, Lalibela — c.1310*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Night before the matins bell; the rock-hewn church; frankincense, wet tuff, hot feet of a dying woman. |
| Focal desire | Gebre, a young deacon, wants the forbidden burning done before the bell, and wants the woman to live. |
| Conflict | Abba Talka orders him to hold her feet ("When she stops, we burn her. Here. Before the bell."). Monks do not burn the dead, never inside a church. At the door stands her son, about seven, his fist closed. In Ge'ez she tells the boy: "Let no priest see it." Gebre is a priest. |
| Micro-payoff | She dies; they burn her on the floor. The fire comes up **black**: cold, soundless. Candles lean toward it and gutter. Its smoke falls, then climbs and **writes** on the hewn ceiling. Talka hands Gebre a knife: "Scrape it off. Before the bell." The letters re-form under the blade. Gebre can read only one word, her name: *Amara*. |
| Breadcrumbs | The black flame records (series magic). The ceiling text survives; it pays off in VII. The boy's fist holds the pendant and strip (Fragment 0). The monks know the "curse" of fragment-bearers. Ash prints a line of letters on Gebre's palm that will not wash off. |
| Closing cliffhanger | The bell begins. Talka, at the doorway: "What did she give him?" Gebre looks at the boy's fist, then at his own palm. *"Nothing, Abba."* |
| Names | Gebre, Abba Talka, Bet Maryam, Amara (named only by the fire, at the end). The boy is unnamed. |
| Target length | 3,500 words |
| Source lines | 11–53 (cremation at 47; "my lion" 37; "Let no priest see it" per bible §4.1). The famine (13–17) is cut from here and held for Ch.12. |

## PART ONE — DIVISION

### CHAPTER 1 — ZOSER
*The* Santa Maria*, off Sicily — winter 1325*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Night. A plank already lashed to the rail; a building storm; tar, wet rope, spray; four Ethiopians kneeling on deck. Captain Humbert, with a precise scholar's voice and manicured nails, walks the line with a **lit candle**, asking catechism questions. His men empty the bags. |
| Focal desire | Keep his mother's strip (sewn into his scrip, against his thigh) hidden and keep everyone alive. These are in conflict. |
| Conflict | The search is working down toward his scrip. At each man Humbert holds the candle close and watches it. Desta sees the candle lean as it nears the next man and answers a question nobody put to him, in his church's true words: "One. United. Without mixture." "Wrong. Recant." Desta makes one dry joke to Zoser ("I was never good at counting") and **walks off the plank of his own will**. The searchers stop to watch, and the scrip goes unopened. Then the candle reaches "the priest" (unnamed) behind a barrel. It **bends toward his belly and goes blue**. Zoser must choose between his church's answer, which kills, and Rome's formula, which is apostasy, or else silence. He answers *for the priest*, in Rome's words, at length, until a wave puts out the candle. |
| Micro-payoff | The priest is saved. Humbert: "Good. You lie like a scholar." The plank is drawn in. Humbert keeps Desta's sword and gives them a map: "Ask for me in Genoa." Zoser hears the name spoken like a rank. |
| Breadcrumbs | Manicured finger and scholar's voice (Humbert is a title-holder). The candle bending to the priest's belly (Tamrat's theft). The priest's wife watches the captain's hands, not the plank (Angeska). The candle **never stirs** at Zoser's scrip (Fragment 0 hides). Desta's sword is kept. |
| Closing cliffhanger | As the lantern is relit, Humbert passes close and asks, almost tenderly: **"Your mother burned black, didn't she, boy?"** |
| Names | Zoser, Desta, Humbert. |
| Target length | 6,000 words |
| Source lines | 231–323 (plank 233–295; the answer for Tamrat 309–321); 201–229 (Humbert's description, "Moors", "prove themselves"); 339 (Genoa). Cut: 291 ("Oops, my bad!"), 233–235, 307 (E089). Desta's answer per E070 / C13. |

### CHAPTER 2 — ANGESKA
*Alexandria — autumn 1325 (three weeks earlier)*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Docks: fish, tar, gulls, Coptic bells, pilgrims, mercenaries. Memories of Cairo: gold that buys half what it did since the Malian king's passage. |
| Focal desire | Get the embassy onto a ship to Italy before their coin is worthless, on her terms. |
| Conflict | Tamrat insists on leading and on insulting everyone. Their stones buy almost nothing. The only master who will take "Moors" is a Venetian-built ship's captain whose men laugh like wolves. In a flashback braid, at the red-bearded imam's table, Tamrat is asked "how does one distinguish between divine truth and human pride?" and Al-Hajj Mahmud speaks of "lights from the same divine source". At the Malian hardliner's "kneel or bleed", Desta's hand goes to his sword, and Angeska defuses it in three languages. |
| Micro-payoff | Passage won, and the "work for passage" humiliation accepted. Desta's night talk with Zoser under Egyptian stars, seen from her bedroll: the book's first pocket of comfort. |
| Breadcrumbs (fair, and her POV never lies) | She notes the captain's signet ring, an iron key in a circle, and thinks only "*Good. Then it is him.*" She knows the papacy sits at Avignon (she reads a Genoese factor's letter) and does not tell Tamrat. She thinks of the embassy as "the cargo" once, then corrects herself to "my husband". |
| Closing cliffhanger | As they board, she sees Tamrat touch his belly. She asks softly, "Is it heavy?" He thinks she means his pride and snaps at her. She smiles at the water. |
| Target length | 5,000 words |
| Source lines | 125–167 (Musa's gold, the imam 143–147, Mahmud 151–159, the hardliner 161–165); 169–183 (Desta's night talk); 187–229 (the docks and the hiring). The march (117–137) is skipped by time-cut (E104). |

### CHAPTER 3 — TAMRAT
*The* Santa Maria *— the same night as Chapter 1*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Below deck: bilge stink, a guttering lamp, Desta's empty sleeping place, a stolen ship's biscuit. |
| Focal desire | Keep what is in his belly hidden until he can lay it at the Pope's feet and be made untouchable. |
| Conflict | Cramps. The boy he has bullied for a thousand miles saved his life with a lie about God, and Tamrat cannot bear the debt. Grief for Desta, who carried him "like a sack of barley" up the Afar escarpment. He goes to the rail "to be sick"; the reader is not shown what he does there. |
| Micro-payoff | Alone, he finally weeps for Desta. Then he goes to Zoser and, instead of thanks, gives orders, and a single sentence of true praise for the boy's Latin. The relationship moves one inch. |
| Breadcrumbs | "It is still with me" (ambiguous). He refuses the biscuit. He saw the candle bend toward him and tells himself it was the wind. Humbert's map carries a seal: an iron key in a circle, the same as the ring (Ch.2). |
| Closing cliffhanger | Lying awake, Tamrat hears his wife speaking softly on deck in Latin to the first mate, a language he does not know she speaks so well. |
| Target length | 4,000 words |

### CHAPTER 5 — ZOSER
*Naples, and the road north — winter 1326*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|

| Focal desire | Get Tamrat to Rome alive, and become the man Desta thought he was. |

### CHAPTER 7 — TAMRAT

| Focal desire | Be received as Ethiopia's voice despite having no Pope to be received by. |

### CHAPTER 11 — ZOSER
*San Francesco a Ripa — the second audience*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | The cardinal's hall: cold, candles, Giovanni's notary, guards. |
| Focal desire | Keep the embassy, and Angeska, alive. |
| Conflict | Giovanni accuses Angeska of falsifying the emperor's words. Zoser has two bad options: let her burn, or lie. He **lies**, saying the softening was Tamrat's own instruction, and sacrifices his master's honour to save her. Tamrat cannot contradict him without condemning her. Then the candles gutter. Tamrat's nosebleed thickens, and **a veil of shadow rises out of him**: golden molten eyes, living scars, Tuscan-accented Latin. It speaks of the wardens "whose names are erased" and **excommunicates Zoser "from the very order of creation"**. |
| Micro-payoff | The thing withdraws. Tamrat collapses, remembering nothing. |
| Breadcrumbs | The veiled man's accent is Florentine, and he smells of San Miniato incense (Teodoro). It says "You will carry what I carried." Angeska alone does not cross herself. |
| Closing cliffhanger | Giovanni, white-faced: "Prepare the stakes." Arrest. |
| Target length | 6,000 words |
| Source lines | 486–568 (the veil staged per E039; the Latin translation per C10; 548–552 the wardens and the excommunication; 512, 516 Giovanni). |

*Castel Sant'Angelo — the same night*

### CHAPTER 17 — TAMRAT
*Ponte Sant'Angelo — dawn*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Crowds on the bridge; the river; pitch; the executioner's brazier; a cold sky. |
| Focal desire | Turn his execution into testimony, and buy the boy time. |

### CHAPTER 18 — ZOSER
*Castel Sant'Angelo, a barred window — the same dawn*

## BACK MATTER — The Keeper's closing entry (about 150 words) and ornament

> *Item: a dossier of the order called the Silence-Keepers, concerning "Angeska, a Nilotic, black-magic born." Addressed to the Holy Father at the Vatican. Seal of fresh beeswax. The hand is fine. The facts are not. I have read an older copy that called her what she was. One of these was written in 1326. — B.*

Followed by the ornament **አ**, alone on the page.

**This entry presents Scar I.** Its tells: the Vatican in 1326, fresh beeswax, and the contradiction with the dossier the reader saw in Ch.24.
