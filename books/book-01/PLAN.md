Series: THE BLACKFLAMES, Book I of VII. Governing files: `series/series-outline.md`, `series/magic-system.md`, `series/mystery-ledger.md`, `series/style-guide.md`, `series/bible.md`, `series/ideas-register.md`. Source lines refer to `blackflames.md`.

**Revision.** Round 1 and Round 2 (Editor-in-Chief). The accepted ideas are woven into the chapter tables below and tagged by id, e.g. `[R1-lit-01]`, `[R2-myth-05]`. Writers execute every tagged beat in their chapter. The register (`series/ideas-register.md`) gives one-line summaries of each id.

**Canon facts.** `books/book-01/FACTS.md` holds every fixed date, name, object and language rule, with a computed day-by-day chronology. **Every writer checks their chapter against it before delivery and appends any new fixed fact.** Where this PLAN and FACTS.md disagree on a date, FACTS.md wins and the Editor is told.

**Target length.** About 165,000 words (chapters 3,000–7,000 each).
**Central question.** *Who owns holiness, and what do you owe the man who stole it for you?*

## Running clocks and calendars (binding for date lines)

- **The matins bell** (Prologue).
- **The lantern-candle** (Ch.1).
- **Tamrat's cramps** (Ch.3–14).
- **The two fasts** (Ch.5–13) [R1-cont-02]. Easter 1326 fell on **23 March** (Julian, in both the Roman and the Alexandrian reckoning). Latin Lent began on Ash Wednesday, **5 February**. The Ethiopian great fast, *Hudade* (vegan, one meal after the ninth hour), began about a week earlier. Romans and Angeska alike read Tamrat's refusal of food as piety. The reader slowly learns he dare not eat because of what is in his belly.
- **Hunger in Rome** (Ch.7–10) [R2-cont-04]. Engineered: Humbert's "Feed them nothing" (Ch.6, 2 Feb) reaches Orsini about 14 February; from then Orsini men shut the inns to them. Only the Coptic hostel defies it, because nobody thought to send word to Egyptians. The bread that ends the hunger (Ch.9–10) is bought by Angeska from Giovanni, and is his first disobedience to Humbert.
- **Desta's forty** [R2-cont-01]. The plank is the night of **23 December 1325**. Counting it as day one, Desta's fortieth day (*arba*, when the dead are released) is **31 January**, the Cassino ford (Ch.5). His year-mind (*tazkar*) is 23 December 1326, Night 1 (Ch.20). Never stated; left in the dates for the auditing reader.
- **The dawn of the burning** (Ch.15–17): dawn of **Monday 31 March 1326**, after the Easter octave.
- **The fourteen nights** (Ch.20–29) [R1-cont-01]. They are counted from the night Zoser and Angeska reach the cardinal's palace. **Night 1 is the night of 23 December 1326; Night 14 is the night of 5–6 January 1327, Epiphany Eve.** In Lalibela the same night is the eve of *Timkat* (11 Ter, which falls on 6 January in the Julian reckoning). That night the tabots stand overnight by the water. "Two continents, one night" is chronologically true. Every Avignon chapter's date line counts the night: *Avignon — the ninth night, Christmastide 1326*.

## Binding devices for the whole book

1. **The Lean** [R1-dread-02, R2-myth-01, R2-magic-01]. A flame that senses a fragment or vessel tips like a head turning to listen, holds there, and its tip goes blue. Describe it differently every time; never describe the mechanism. **No flame in Book I may lean, tip and hold, or turn blue except by the Lean. Wind makes flames shrink, smoke and die; it never bends one and holds it.** The reader learns to fear every flame. **Operating rule** (`magic-system.md` §4, never stated in prose): (a) any ordinary flame leans to an unhidden fragment or vessel within about a hand's breadth, and only while it stays that close; a swallowed fragment reads only at that range; (b) a hunter's candle reaches a deck's length: Candlemas beeswax, in the hand of a man who has drunk the Nail-water, with a question put to it; (c) when what is carried **wakes** in its bearer (its sign is **blood from the nose**), every flame in the room answers it, and then answers whatever it attends to; (d) warden-script read aloud is "uncovered", and a fresh copy read aloud by its copier leans flames near it while the ink is wet; (e) a Rite pulls the flames of the place that keeps its record. Schedule: Ch.1 (one candle, one man: clause b); Ch.10 (the night-lamp at the narrow door as Tamrat passes within a hand's breadth: clause a); Ch.11 (every candle in the hall leans to Tamrat when the thing wakes, then all swing at once to Zoser, whose strip lies uncovered and unsewn on his skin: clauses c, d); Ch.22 (the curia's candles lean toward Angeska's sleeve, which holds her wet copies of Amara's script, read under her breath; Zoser blames "the draught from her sleeve": clause d); Ch.30 (every candle in Bet Maryam leans north-west: clause e); Ch.31 (the bargeman's tallow lamp held close to a belly: clause a); Ch.33 (**inverted**: every flame leans *away* from Zoser, the way animals shy, before all but one go out). An **aborted** Lean (a tip that begins to turn and is denied by a step back) is allowed once, in Ch.7 [R2-dread-01]. Tamrat's habit of keeping an arm's length from every lamp is a cumulative tell that Angeska manages: Ch.3 (on the page), Ch.7 (she moves the hostel lamp to the far end of the board "so the old man's eyes can rest"), Ch.13 (in the cell she always sets the candle on her own side).
2. **Gebre's palm** [R1-mystery-07, R1-dread-04, R1-lit-04]. Black-flame text about a living person is never finished, and the line printed on Gebre's palm is about the boy. Talka checks it in every Talka chapter "as one takes a pulse". Its current text appears once per chapter as a one-line italic transcription, with unreadable signs as gaps (*[ — — — ]*). Prologue: one line, unreadable. Ch.4: the same line, plus a mark like a wave. Ch.12: a cluster Talka slowly reads as *sea*. Ch.23: *Avignon* (the cliff). Ch.30: see that chapter.
3. **The Entity only quotes** [R1-dread-01]. In Book I the Entity never speaks an original sentence. Every word it says (the veil in Ch.11, Angeska's lacquered mouth in Ch.29, the Latin in Ch.33) is assembled from words already spoken or written near the Key or the strip, given back verbatim in the original speaker's cadence. Writers keep a concordance: every quotation must be sourceable to a line the reader has already read. The table is at the end of this plan.
4. **The excommunication takes, quietly** [R1-dread-05, R2-mystery-06, R2-dread-07]. From Ch.11 the world starts failing to record Zoser. There is never more than one small, deniable detail per chapter, and only in the chapters listed: Ch.11, 13, 15, 20, 21, 23, 33. **Ruling:** it does not alter originals written before Ch.11 (the Ch.7 diptych, the Naples toll-roll); it prevents any record made **after** it from holding him, copies of earlier records included. Ch.21: a Lent fair copy of the Naples toll-roll reads *MAVRI II*. Ch.23: the stufa letter's signature reaches Ethiopia as an empty reed-groove.
5. **Angeska's nightly word** [R1-psych-05]. From Ch.5 she teaches Zoser one Latin word each night, drawn from a mundane occasion. He cannot hold Latin, so he writes each word in his psalter beside the አ. The list, in order: *clavis* (key, Ch.5, a gate), *ostium* (door, Ch.7), *vas* (vessel, Ch.10, a cooking pot), *germen* (seed or sprout, Ch.13, the last bread), *ignis* (fire, Ch.19), *mater* (mother, Ch.19), *custos* (warden, Ch.22). Read together the words are her plan. In the Rite (Ch.27–29) the Latin is rendered as sound, except for her taught words, which surface in English because she taught them to him. *(Use* germen*, not* semen*: the register of Ch.28–29 must never flirt with the erotic, E092.)* **The trade** [R2-psych-06]: from *vas* (Ch.10) Zoser, stung that he only receives, pays for each Latin word with a word of his own: highland names for things (the dung-and-juniper fire, the hour-star Desta taught him, kolo as Desta said it). She pretends boredom, writes none of them down, and repeats every one back perfectly a week later. Tamrat joins from his corner (Ch.10). For *germen* (Ch.13) Zoser has nothing left to pay with and gives his mother's word for him, the one the text renders "my lion", in the river tongue; she does not repeat it: "Keep that one."
6. **"Wet clay"** [R1-psych-04, R2-dread-04]. Tamrat's insult (l.115) travels: contempt (Ch.2), revision (Ch.3), joke (Ch.5), **theft** (Ch.11: the veil says "Clay carrying the potter" in Tamrat's own strangled ford-voice, as its self-description), blessing (Ch.15: Tamrat takes his word back from the thing), last thought (Ch.17), desecration (Ch.28).
7. **Counting against naming** [R1-lit-09, R2-lit-07, R2-myth-05]. The powerful count; the rememberers name. Book I's beats: Desta's count (Ch.1, 2, 13; in Ch.13 nobody knows what the thirty-nine were called, and the number is not said again), the jeli's names (Ch.2), *MAVRI III* (Ch.5) and *MAVRI II* (Ch.21), "seven, no more, no fewer" (Ch.21), the victory register against the captive's whispered names, and Talka naming Tamrat aloud in the same chapter where he withholds the boy's name and the captive's (Ch.23), and the uncounted Clerk (Ch.27, Epilogue).
8. **Restored-leaf cuts** [R1-lit-07]. Three Angeska scenes end on an exact, quotable last line, because Book VII will resume each from that line: Ch.9 (on Giovanni's "What do you want, woman?"), Ch.18 (the start of her missing night hours) and Ch.25 (needle and knife). Writers record the exact last line in the chapter's closing HTML comment, `<!-- RESTORED-LEAF CUT: "…" -->`.
9. **The Keeper's calendar** [R1-mystery-08]. The Keeper dates her entries in the Ethiopian reckoning, in Ge'ez numerals: *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* (Yekatit 1889 E.C., which is February 1897). Adwa fell in Yekatit 1888 E.C. A converting reader learns that the Keeper writes after the series ends, in an Ethiopian hand. No gloss is ever given in Book I.
10. **The Standing** [R2-myth-02, R2-psych-05]. The counter-grammar of flame. While the dead are named aloud **with love**, flames within reach stand perfectly still in moving air, "as if set in a closed room", and their smoke rises in unbroken threads. Never explained. Book I: Ch.2 (the jeli, on the page); Ch.9 (Angeska's litany in the cardinal's antechamber); Ch.13 (the cell candle on Fasika night, while they say Desta's name); Ch.23 (the brazier smoke goes up straight in the war-camp wind when Talka speaks Tamrat's name; the Ifat captive's whispered names get no Standing, because no one there names them with love); Ch.30 (the vigil candles by the tabots, before the north-west Lean). Flame leans to seeking and stands for naming; white fire, which throws no shadow, is the third state.
11. **The dread ladder** [R2-dread-08, R2-myth-04]. Every chapter without a scheduled Lean carries **exactly one** controlled, deniable sign, each from a different channel (body, smell, animal, geometry, record, object, warmth), each worse than the last, never explained:

## FRONT MATTER — The Keeper's entry (about 150 words)

Head it with the catalogue line *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* [R1-mystery-08]. Draft for the writer to polish (keep every plant):

> *Item the first. One sheet of vellum, unusually supple, scorched black at every edge and yet entire. The script is one I was taught to read by my mother, who died in the hour I was born, and whom I have been carrying since. The veins of the hide are visible and do not sit still under the lamp. Provenance: Avignon, by way of Lalibela, by way of a sea I will not name. I have catalogued worse things. I have not catalogued anything I loved.* — B.

**Plants.**
- "Died in the hour I was born, and whom I have been carrying since" [R1-cont-10]: Rosa consumed Angeska at birth (bible C18/C19) and holds her memories.
- The vellum is Zoser. Its words "supple", "veins" and "scorched … yet entire" are echoed by the three Book I clues in Ch.32–33 [R1-mystery-09].
- "B." is the Bellini mask.
- The Ge'ez-numeral date.

No proper names in the body of the entry.

## PROLOGUE — GEBRE
*Bet Maryam, Lalibela — c.1310*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Night before the matins bell; the rock-hewn church; frankincense, wet tuff, the hot feet of a dying woman. |
| Focal desire | Gebre, a young deacon, wants the forbidden burning done before the bell, and wants the woman to live. |
| Conflict | Abba Talka orders him to hold her feet ("When she stops, we burn her. Here. Before the bell."). Monks do not burn the dead, never inside a church. At the door stands her son, about seven, his fist closed. In Ge'ez she tells the boy: "Let no priest see it." Gebre is a priest. Under his hands Gebre feels **raised lines on her shins and the tops of her feet**, a deliberate sequence of short and long cuts like a tally [R1-myth-01]. He does not know what they are. She calls the boy "my lion" (l.37). |
| Micro-payoff | She dies; they burn her on the floor. The fire comes up **black**: cold, soundless. Candles lean toward it and gutter. It burns the lines on her legs first, so they show as darker strokes inside the dark flame, and the smoke that climbs and writes on the hewn ceiling repeats their rhythm as letters [R1-myth-01]. Talka hands Gebre a knife: "Scrape it off. Before the bell." The letters re-form under the blade. **Behind his knife a new line grows, letter by letter, under Amara's: his own name, *Gebre*.** The fire has entered him in its record as the witness [R1-dread-03]. He scrapes his name away and it returns, and he keeps scraping until the bell. Ash settles on his lip; he tastes **salt**, and thinks of the woman's tears, though she did not weep [R1-myth-02]. At the last he can read one more word: her name, *Amara*. |
| Breadcrumbs | The black flame records (series magic). The ceiling text survives (pays off in Ch.16, Ch.30 and VII). The boy's fist holds the pendant and strip (Fragment 0). The monks know the "curse" of fragment-bearers. Ash prints a line of letters on Gebre's palm that will not wash off; he cannot read it (the palm's first transcription: *[ — — — — — ]*). The witness line is the true terror under his lie. |
| Closing cliffhanger | The bell begins. Talka, at the doorway: "What did she give him?" Gebre looks at the boy's fist, then at his own palm. *"Nothing, Abba."* |
| Names | Gebre, Abba Talka, Bet Maryam, Amara (named only by the fire, at the end). The boy is unnamed. |

### CHAPTER 1 — ZOSER
*The* Santa Maria*, off Sicily — winter 1325*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Night. A plank already lashed to the rail; a squall building in the west; tar, wet rope, spray. The four of the embassy on deck. Captain Humbert, with a precise scholar's voice and manicured nails, walks the line with a **lit candle**, asking catechism questions. His men empty the bags. The line along the rail runs: the priest's wife (whom Humbert does not question), Desta, Zoser, and, crouched behind the water barrel at the end, "the priest". |
| Focal desire | Keep his mother's strip (sewn into his scrip, against his thigh) hidden and keep everyone alive. These are in conflict. |
| Conflict | Zoser's first spoken words, as the plank is run out, are "It's begun." (verbatim; the Entity will replay them in Ch.29 [R1-dread-01]). The search works down the line toward his scrip. At each man Humbert holds the candle close and watches it, not the answers. Past Desta the flame **leans** down the line, toward the space where Zoser kneels and the barrel beyond him. **Desta looks once at the candle and once at Zoser's scrip**, not at Zoser's face. Zoser registers the look and takes it as meant for him [R1-lit-01, R1-mystery-01]. Desta answers a question nobody put to him, in his church's true words: "One. United. Without mixture." "Wrong. Recant." Eyes on Zoser, Desta makes one dry joke, played straight and opaque: "I was never good at counting." He **walks off the plank of his own will** [R1-psych-01]. The searchers stop to watch, and the scrip goes unopened. After the splash Zoser asks, too loudly, "Was he wrong?" Humbert, eyes on the candle, which still leans past the empty place: **"He was early."** Zoser hears mockery [R1-lit-01]. Then the candle reaches the barrel. It **bends toward the priest's belly and goes blue**. The priest is frozen. As the men tip out his bag Zoser sees him shake, a tremor older than this deck (seed for Ch.3 and Ch.14 [R1-psych-03]). Zoser must choose: his church's answer, which kills; Rome's formula, which is apostasy; or silence. He answers **for the priest** in Rome's words. He learned the Latin missionaries' catechism and the Definition of Chalcedon by rote in 1324, telling himself it was to refute them. The truer reason, which he will not look at, is that he hoped to be chosen for exactly this. He recites more than he understands [R1-cont-03, R1-psych-07]. Part of him does it out of spite, to make the old man who calls him "wet clay" owe him his life. He watches the squall line and **talks until the wave comes**: the long answer is stalling. A wave puts out the candle. |
| Micro-payoff | The priest is saved. Humbert: "Good. You lie like a scholar." It lands on exactly Zoser's shame: he is good at apostasy. The plank is drawn in. Humbert keeps Desta's sword and gives them a map: "Ask for me in Genoa." Zoser hears the name spoken like a rank. Desta's leather pouch of *kolo* (roasted barley and chickpeas, unspiced) lies where he knelt; Zoser takes it [R1-myth-04]. |
| Breadcrumbs | Manicured finger and scholar's voice (Humbert is an office-holder). The key-in-circle seal on the captain's papers, the papal licence that lets him trade with Mamluk Egypt in the closed season [R1-cont-10]. The candle bending to the priest's belly (Tamrat's theft). The priest's wife watches the captain's hands, not the plank (Angeska). The candle **never stirs** at Zoser's scrip (Fragment 0 hides). Desta's glance at the candle and the scrip. "He was early." Desta's sword is kept. |
| Closing cliffhanger | As the lantern is relit, Humbert passes close and asks, almost tenderly: **"Your mother burned black, didn't she, boy?"** Then he spits over the rail, as a man spits out a name (the order's rite after naming a bearer [R1-cont-06]). End on the question or the spit, nothing after. |

### CHAPTER 2 — ANGESKA
*Alexandria — Advent 1325 (three weeks earlier)*

**Round 2 revision (written chapter)** [R2-cont-01, R2-cont-02, R2-lit-08]: Saint Martin becomes Saint Andrew throughout (l.17, 19, 41, 213), so the scene falls on 2 December, the day after Advent Sunday, and the closed season the captain's licence beats is real; signet on the **forefinger** (l.81); "twenty" → "twenty-one" (l.35); the captain's spit (l.85) becomes another gesture (spitting is the order's naming rite); trim "as a… / the way a…" similes to three, each from Angeska's field (thread, weaving, grammar, tides).

| Field | Plan |

## BACK MATTER — The Keeper's closing entry (about 150 words) and ornament

Head it *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* [R1-mystery-08]. Draft for the writer to polish:

> *Item the sixth: a dossier of the order called the Silence-Keepers, concerning "Angeska, a Nilotic, black-magic born." Addressed to the Holy Father at the Vatican. Seal of fresh beeswax. The hand is fine, with a looped* et*. The facts are not. I have read an older copy that called her what she was. One of these was written in 1326. Item the seventh: a consistory of the year ፲፫፻፳, of which the record keeps no minute. — B.*

Followed by the ornament **አ**, alone on the page.

**This entry presents Scar I.** Its tells: the Vatican in 1326 (the papacy was at Avignon); "Urban or Clement" if the dossier names a pope; fresh beeswax on an "old" document; the looped ligature that matches the Clerk's hand; the contradiction with the dossier the reader saw in Ch.24. The year ፲፫፻፳ (1320 in the Ethiopian reckoning, which is Lent 1328 in the Julian) restores the year Ch.33's date line hides, and reads to a careless eye as an eight-year "error": a deliberate false Scar that teaches the reader to audit dates.
