Series: THE BLACKFLAMES, Book I of VII. Governing files: `series/series-outline.md`, `series/magic-system.md`, `series/mystery-ledger.md`, `series/style-guide.md`, `series/bible.md`, `series/ideas-register.md`. Source lines refer to `blackflames.md`.

**Revision.** Round 1 (Editor-in-Chief). The accepted ideas are woven into the chapter tables below and tagged by id, e.g. `[R1-lit-01]`. Writers execute every tagged beat in their chapter. The register (`series/ideas-register.md`) gives one-line summaries of each id.

**Span.** Prologue c.1310; main action winter 1325 to Lent 1328.
**Shape.** Keeper's entry, then Prologue, then 33 chapters in four Parts, then Epilogue, then a closing Keeper's entry.
**Target length.** About 165,000 words (chapters 3,000–7,000 each).
**Central question.** *Who owns holiness, and what do you owe the man who stole it for you?*

## Running clocks and calendars (binding for date lines)

- **The matins bell** (Prologue).
- **The lantern-candle** (Ch.1).
- **Tamrat's cramps** (Ch.3–14).
- **The two fasts** (Ch.5–13) [R1-cont-02]. Easter 1326 fell on **23 March** (Julian, in both the Roman and the Alexandrian reckoning). Latin Lent began on Ash Wednesday, **5 February**. The Ethiopian great fast, *Hudade* (vegan, one meal after the ninth hour), began about a week earlier. Romans and Angeska alike read Tamrat's refusal of food as piety. The reader slowly learns he dare not eat because of what is in his belly.
- **Hunger in Rome** (Ch.7–10).
- **The dawn of the burning** (Ch.15–17): dawn of **Monday 31 March 1326**, after the Easter octave.
- **The fourteen nights** (Ch.20–29) [R1-cont-01]. They are counted from the night Zoser and Angeska reach the cardinal's palace. **Night 1 is the night of 23 December 1326; Night 14 is the night of 5–6 January 1327, Epiphany Eve.** In Lalibela the same night is the eve of *Timkat* (11 Ter, which falls on 6 January in the Julian reckoning). That night the tabots stand overnight by the water. "Two continents, one night" is chronologically true. Every Avignon chapter's date line counts the night: *Avignon — the ninth night, Christmastide 1326*.

## Binding devices for the whole book

1. **The Lean** [R1-dread-02]. A flame that senses a fragment or vessel tips like a head turning to listen, holds there, and its tip goes blue. Describe it differently every time; never describe the mechanism. **No other candle in Book I may flicker for weather or draught.** The reader learns to fear every flame. Schedule: Ch.1 (one candle, one man); Ch.10 (the night-lamp at the door as Tamrat passes); Ch.11 (every candle in the hall leans to Tamrat, then all swing at once to Zoser); Ch.22 (the curia's candles lean toward Angeska, and Zoser blames the draught from her sleeve); Ch.30 (every candle in Bet Maryam leans north-west); Ch.31 (the bargeman's lamp at a belly); Ch.33 (**inverted**: every flame leans *away* from Zoser, the way animals shy, before all but one go out).
2. **Gebre's palm** [R1-mystery-07, R1-dread-04, R1-lit-04]. Black-flame text about a living person is never finished, and the line printed on Gebre's palm is about the boy. Talka checks it in every Talka chapter "as one takes a pulse". Its current text appears once per chapter as a one-line italic transcription, with unreadable signs as gaps (*[ — — — ]*). Prologue: one line, unreadable. Ch.4: the same line, plus a mark like a wave. Ch.12: a cluster Talka slowly reads as *sea*. Ch.23: *Avignon* (the cliff). Ch.30: see that chapter.
3. **The Entity only quotes** [R1-dread-01]. In Book I the Entity never speaks an original sentence. Every word it says (the veil in Ch.11, Angeska's lacquered mouth in Ch.29, the Latin in Ch.33) is assembled from words already spoken or written near the Key or the strip, given back verbatim in the original speaker's cadence. Writers keep a concordance: every quotation must be sourceable to a line the reader has already read. The table is at the end of this plan.
4. **The excommunication takes, quietly** [R1-dread-05]. From Ch.11 the world starts failing to record Zoser. There is never more than one small, deniable detail per chapter, and only in the chapters listed: Ch.11, 13, 15, 20, 33.
5. **Angeska's nightly word** [R1-psych-05]. From Ch.5 she teaches Zoser one Latin word each night, drawn from a mundane occasion. He cannot hold Latin, so he writes each word in his psalter beside the አ. The list, in order: *clavis* (key, Ch.5, a gate), *ostium* (door, Ch.7), *vas* (vessel, Ch.10, a cooking pot), *germen* (seed or sprout, Ch.13, the last bread), *ignis* (fire, Ch.19), *mater* (mother, Ch.19), *custos* (warden, Ch.22). Read together the words are her plan. In the Rite (Ch.27–29) the Latin is rendered as sound, except for her taught words, which surface in English because she taught them to him. *(Use* germen*, not* semen*: the register of Ch.28–29 must never flirt with the erotic, E092.)*
6. **"Wet clay"** [R1-psych-04]. Tamrat's insult (l.115) travels: contempt (Ch.2), revision (Ch.3), joke (Ch.5), blessing (Ch.15), last thought (Ch.17), desecration (Ch.28).
7. **Counting against naming** [R1-lit-09]. The powerful count; the rememberers name. Book I's beats: Desta's count (Ch.1, 2, 13), the jeli's names (Ch.2), "seven, no more, no fewer" (Ch.21), the victory register against the captive's whispered names (Ch.23), and the uncounted Clerk (Ch.27, Epilogue).
8. **Restored-leaf cuts** [R1-lit-07]. Three Angeska scenes end on an exact, quotable last line, because Book VII will resume each from that line: Ch.9 (on Giovanni's "What do you want, woman?"), Ch.18 (the start of her missing night hours) and Ch.25 (needle and knife). Writers record the exact last line in the chapter's closing HTML comment, `<!-- RESTORED-LEAF CUT: "…" -->`.
9. **The Keeper's calendar** [R1-mystery-08]. The Keeper dates her entries in the Ethiopian reckoning, in Ge'ez numerals: *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* (Yekatit 1889 E.C., which is February 1897). Adwa fell in Yekatit 1888 E.C. A converting reader learns that the Keeper writes after the series ends, in an Ethiopian hand. No gloss is ever given in Book I.

## FRONT MATTER — The Keeper's entry (about 150 words)

Head it with the catalogue line *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* [R1-mystery-08]. Draft for the writer to polish (keep every plant):

> *Item the first. One sheet of vellum, unusually supple, scorched black at every edge and yet entire. The script is one I was taught to read by my mother, who died in the hour I was born, and whom I have been carrying since. The veins of the hide are visible and do not sit still under the lamp. Provenance: Avignon, by way of Lalibela, by way of a sea I will not name. I have catalogued worse things. I have not catalogued anything I loved.* — B.

**Plants.**
- "Died in the hour I was born, and whom I have been carrying since" [R1-cont-10]: Rosa consumed Angeska at birth (bible C18/C19) and holds her memories.
- The vellum is Zoser. Its words "supple", "veins" and "scorched … yet entire" are echoed by the three Book I clues in Ch.32–33 [R1-mystery-09].
- "B." is the Bellini mask.
- The Ge'ez-numeral date.

No proper names in the body of the entry.

## PROLOGUE — GEBRE
*Bet Maryam, Lalibela — c.1310*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Night before the matins bell; the rock-hewn church; frankincense, wet tuff, the hot feet of a dying woman. |
| Focal desire | Gebre, a young deacon, wants the forbidden burning done before the bell, and wants the woman to live. |
| Conflict | Abba Talka orders him to hold her feet ("When she stops, we burn her. Here. Before the bell."). Monks do not burn the dead, never inside a church. At the door stands her son, about seven, his fist closed. In Ge'ez she tells the boy: "Let no priest see it." Gebre is a priest. Under his hands Gebre feels **raised lines on her shins and the tops of her feet**, a deliberate sequence of short and long cuts like a tally [R1-myth-01]. He does not know what they are. She calls the boy "my lion" (l.37). |
| Micro-payoff | She dies; they burn her on the floor. The fire comes up **black**: cold, soundless. Candles lean toward it and gutter. It burns the lines on her legs first, so they show as darker strokes inside the dark flame, and the smoke that climbs and writes on the hewn ceiling repeats their rhythm as letters [R1-myth-01]. Talka hands Gebre a knife: "Scrape it off. Before the bell." The letters re-form under the blade. **Behind his knife a new line grows, letter by letter, under Amara's: his own name, *Gebre*.** The fire has entered him in its record as the witness [R1-dread-03]. He scrapes his name away and it returns, and he keeps scraping until the bell. Ash settles on his lip; he tastes **salt**, and thinks of the woman's tears, though she did not weep [R1-myth-02]. At the last he can read one more word: her name, *Amara*. |
| Breadcrumbs | The black flame records (series magic). The ceiling text survives (pays off in Ch.16, Ch.30 and VII). The boy's fist holds the pendant and strip (Fragment 0). The monks know the "curse" of fragment-bearers. Ash prints a line of letters on Gebre's palm that will not wash off; he cannot read it (the palm's first transcription: *[ — — — — — ]*). The witness line is the true terror under his lie. |
| Closing cliffhanger | The bell begins. Talka, at the doorway: "What did she give him?" Gebre looks at the boy's fist, then at his own palm. *"Nothing, Abba."* |
| Names | Gebre, Abba Talka, Bet Maryam, Amara (named only by the fire, at the end). The boy is unnamed. |
| Target length | 3,500 words |
| Source lines | 11–53 (cremation at 47; "my lion" 37; scarification 15; "Let no priest see it" per bible §4.1). The famine (13–17) is cut from here and held for Ch.12. |

## PART ONE — DIVISION

### CHAPTER 1 — ZOSER
*The* Santa Maria*, off Sicily — winter 1325*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Night. A plank already lashed to the rail; a squall building in the west; tar, wet rope, spray. The four of the embassy on deck. Captain Humbert, with a precise scholar's voice and manicured nails, walks the line with a **lit candle**, asking catechism questions. His men empty the bags. The line along the rail runs: the priest's wife (whom Humbert does not question), Desta, Zoser, and, crouched behind the water barrel at the end, "the priest". |
| Focal desire | Keep his mother's strip (sewn into his scrip, against his thigh) hidden and keep everyone alive. These are in conflict. |
| Conflict | Zoser's first spoken words, as the plank is run out, are "It's begun." (verbatim; the Entity will replay them in Ch.29 [R1-dread-01]). The search works down the line toward his scrip. At each man Humbert holds the candle close and watches it, not the answers. Past Desta the flame **leans** down the line, toward the space where Zoser kneels and the barrel beyond him. **Desta looks once at the candle and once at Zoser's scrip**, not at Zoser's face. Zoser registers the look and takes it as meant for him [R1-lit-01, R1-mystery-01]. Desta answers a question nobody put to him, in his church's true words: "One. United. Without mixture." "Wrong. Recant." Eyes on Zoser, Desta makes one dry joke, played straight and opaque: "I was never good at counting." He **walks off the plank of his own will** [R1-psych-01]. The searchers stop to watch, and the scrip goes unopened. After the splash Zoser asks, too loudly, "Was he wrong?" Humbert, eyes on the candle, which still leans past the empty place: **"He was early."** Zoser hears mockery [R1-lit-01]. Then the candle reaches the barrel. It **bends toward the priest's belly and goes blue**. The priest is frozen. As the men tip out his bag Zoser sees him shake, a tremor older than this deck (seed for Ch.3 and Ch.14 [R1-psych-03]). Zoser must choose: his church's answer, which kills; Rome's formula, which is apostasy; or silence. He answers **for the priest** in Rome's words. He learned the Latin missionaries' catechism and the Definition of Chalcedon by rote in 1324, telling himself it was to refute them. The truer reason, which he will not look at, is that he hoped to be chosen for exactly this. He recites more than he understands [R1-cont-03, R1-psych-07]. Part of him does it out of spite, to make the old man who calls him "wet clay" owe him his life. He watches the squall line and **talks until the wave comes**: the long answer is stalling. A wave puts out the candle. |
| Micro-payoff | The priest is saved. Humbert: "Good. You lie like a scholar." It lands on exactly Zoser's shame: he is good at apostasy. The plank is drawn in. Humbert keeps Desta's sword and gives them a map: "Ask for me in Genoa." Zoser hears the name spoken like a rank. Desta's leather pouch of *kolo* (roasted barley and chickpeas, unspiced) lies where he knelt; Zoser takes it [R1-myth-04]. |
| Breadcrumbs | Manicured finger and scholar's voice (Humbert is an office-holder). The key-in-circle seal on the captain's papers, the papal licence that lets him trade with Mamluk Egypt in the closed season [R1-cont-10]. The candle bending to the priest's belly (Tamrat's theft). The priest's wife watches the captain's hands, not the plank (Angeska). The candle **never stirs** at Zoser's scrip (Fragment 0 hides). Desta's glance at the candle and the scrip. "He was early." Desta's sword is kept. |
| Closing cliffhanger | As the lantern is relit, Humbert passes close and asks, almost tenderly: **"Your mother burned black, didn't she, boy?"** Then he spits over the rail, as a man spits out a name (the order's rite after naming a bearer [R1-cont-06]). End on the question or the spit, nothing after. |
| Names | Zoser, Desta, Humbert. The priest and his wife stay unnamed in this chapter. |
| Target length | 6,000 words |
| Source lines | 231–323 (plank 233–295; the answer for Tamrat 309–321); 201–229 (Humbert's description, "Moors", "prove themselves"); 339 (Genoa). Cut: 291 ("Oops, my bad!"), 233–235, 307 (E089). Desta's answer per E070 / C13. |
| Hidden truth (writers only; never stated) | Abba Talka privately charged Desta to keep the hunters from the boy's bag. Desta knows the highland lamp-blessing (see Ch.4) and so understands at once that Humbert is not testing faith but looking for a "claimed" body. He believes the candle leans toward Zoser's scrip. He steps off the plank to stop the search before it reaches the boy. He has counted wrong: the candle was leaning toward Tamrat, and it can never see the scrip. In Ch.15 Zoser concludes Desta died for Tamrat's theft; Book IV reveals the second layer [R1-mystery-01, R1-myth-03]. |

### CHAPTER 2 — ANGESKA
*Alexandria — autumn 1325 (three weeks earlier)*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Docks: fish, tar, gulls, Coptic bells and muezzins together, pilgrims, mercenaries. Memories of Cairo: gold that buys half what it did since the Malian king's passage. The Ethiopians' trade goods (incense, a few **salt bars** from the Afar caravans, the highland wealth) buy almost nothing here [R1-myth-02]. |
| Focal desire | Get the embassy onto a ship to Italy before their coin is worthless, on her terms. |
| Conflict | Tamrat insists on leading and on insulting everyone. He tells his Constantinople story ("I have debated with the finest minds in Constantinople", l.109) as a triumph, as he always does [R1-psych-03]. He calls Zoser "a mind like wet clay, requiring constant shaping by a master's hand" (l.115), and Angeska notes that the boy flinches every time [R1-psych-04]. The only master who will take "Moors" in the closed season is a Venetian-built ship's captain whose men laugh like wolves; he sails because of a licence no one else holds. In a flashback braid, at the red-bearded imam's table, Tamrat is asked "how does one distinguish between divine truth and human pride?" and Al-Hajj Mahmud speaks of "lights from the same divine source". The returning Malian party includes **a jeli, Fodé Kouyaté**, the hereditary keeper of genealogies and of the dead [R1-myth-08]. He does not "tell" history, he "carries" it, and he speaks of the strings of his *ngoni* (lute) as lines of kin (no kora: it is later). At the hardliner's "kneel or bleed", Desta's hand goes to his sword. Angeska defuses it in three languages, and the last move is hers alone: she asks the jeli, in Mande, to recite the young man's own lineage. The hardliner is the jeli's nephew, and he is shamed into sitting down by the weight of his ancestors' names. |
| Micro-payoff | Passage won, and the "work for passage" humiliation accepted. Night, seen from her bedroll: the book's first pocket of comfort. Desta shares a handful of kolo with Zoser under the Egyptian stars and teaches him to tell the hour by them, finger on each star. He tells the boy his count: since the Ifat wars he has counted the men who died beside him, and he stopped at thirty-nine. "Thirty-nine. I don't do forty. You're too young to be my forty." Angeska files it away; she notes how people can be used [R1-psych-01]. Later, she watches Desta check the strap of Zoser's scrip, never his own kit, "the way a soldier checks a sentry" [R1-mystery-01]. Another night, the jeli recites from memory the name of every pilgrim who died on the hajj road. Angeska's POV records, without comment, that the lamps around the fire stop guttering in the wind and burn upright while he speaks, and that she is afraid of him. No explanation. |
| Breadcrumbs (fair, and her POV never lies) | She notes the captain's signet ring, an iron key in a circle, and thinks only "*Good. Then it is him.*" She hands the captain's mate a small packet sealed in wax "for his master", as she had promised; the narration does not open it and she does not think about its contents [R1-mystery-02]. She knows the papacy sits at Avignon (she reads a Genoese factor's letter) and does not tell Tamrat. Once she thinks of the embassy as "the cargo", then corrects herself to "my husband". Once she thinks of her marriage as one "no bishop had blessed", and moves on [R1-cont-05]. A flashback names the novice **Giyorgis**, about twelve, who carried water for the embassy to Massawa and was sent home with its last message [R1-cont-09]. |
| Closing cliffhanger | As they board, she sees Tamrat touch his belly. She asks softly, "Is it heavy?" He thinks she means his pride and snaps at her. She smiles at the water. |
| Target length | 5,000 words |
| Source lines | 125–167 (Musa's gold, the imam 143–147, Mahmud 151–159, the hardliner 161–165); 169–183 (Desta's night talk); 187–229 (the docks and the hiring); 109, 115 (Tamrat's boast and insult). The march (117–137) is skipped by time-cut (E104). |

### CHAPTER 3 — TAMRAT
*The* Santa Maria *— the same night as Chapter 1*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Below deck: bilge stink, a guttering lamp, Desta's empty sleeping place, a stolen ship's biscuit. |
| Focal desire | Keep what is in his belly hidden until he can lay it at the Pope's feet and be made untouchable. |
| Conflict | Cramps. The boy he has bullied for a thousand miles saved his life with a lie about God, and Tamrat cannot bear the debt. He senses the spite in the gift and refuses to be grateful for it [R1-psych-07]. The sound of his bag being emptied on deck keeps coming back; his hands shake when a sailor drops a sack overhead. He tells himself the Constantinople story again, as triumph, to steady them [R1-psych-03]. Grief for Desta, who carried him "like a sack of barley" up the Afar escarpment. Once he thinks of "the first one, Ilfinesh, God rest her", and flinches from the arithmetic of his years [R1-cont-05]. He goes to the rail "to be sick"; the reader is not shown what he does there. |
| Micro-payoff | Alone, he finally weeps for Desta. Then he goes to Zoser and, instead of thanks, gives orders, and one sentence of true praise. It is for the boy's memory, not his Latin: "You held their words well. Clay that takes a shape is not nothing" [R1-psych-04, R1-cont-03]. The relationship moves one inch. |
| Breadcrumbs | "It is still with me" (ambiguous). He refuses the biscuit. He saw the candle bend toward him and tells himself it was the wind. A withheld memory surfaces and is pushed away: the stelae at dusk, the smell of a guardian's lamp [R1-cont-08]. Humbert's map carries a seal: an iron key in a circle, the same as the ring (Ch.2). |
| Closing cliffhanger | Lying awake, Tamrat hears his wife speaking softly on deck in Latin to the first mate, a language he does not know she speaks so well. |
| Target length | 4,000 words |

### CHAPTER 4 — ABBA TALKA
*Lalibela — Timkat, January 1326*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | The Epiphany feast: the tabots of every church in Lalibela carried down to the water under brocade and processional umbrellas; sistrums; kebero drums; tella and injera shared on cloth; frost at dawn on the red tuff. In Bet Maryam the painted ceiling, which Talka had painted over in 1311, shows a grey ghost of script through the pigment if one looks up; he does not [R1-cont-08]. |
| Focal desire | Keep the emperor's officers believing the embassy will bring Rome's alliance against Ifat, while hiding what he knows of the fragment-bearers' curse. |
| Conflict | Amda Seyon's officer, demanding news, brings word of Ifat raids on the eastern marches. In remembered dialogue, the Latin missionaries of 1324 ("Rome alone holds the keys"). Talka's choice of Zoser: "I did not choose lightly." He remembers, too, that the suggestion to send "the orphan with the good hand" came to him through Tamrat's young wife; he still chose freely [R1-mystery-02]. He remembers a private word with Desta at the gate: "Watch the boy's bag, not the boy." The memory cuts before he gives the reason [R1-mystery-01]. He has long suspected Tamrat's second marriage was blessed by no bishop, and said nothing [R1-cont-05]. Gebre, mute since 1310, serves him and will not meet his eye. The novice Giyorgis, home from Massawa, serves at the water [R1-cont-09]. |
| Micro-payoff | Talka wins the officer's patience with a well-timed lie: the embassy has reached Rome. Comfort: he blesses the children at the water. Nearby a grandmother holds an oil lamp to a newborn, the local lying-in custom: if the flame leans, "the child is claimed", and the family brings it to the priests for blessing. The flame stands straight. Talka relaxes visibly, and the reader stores the gesture [R1-myth-03]. Watching the tabots, he thinks the thought the book will test: Rome believes holiness is one object in one place, and his people made the Ark present on every altar by consecration [R1-myth-06]. |
| Breadcrumbs | Talka's guilt about the burning of 1310. Gebre's stained palm, hidden in his sleeve; Talka takes his wrist and checks it like a pulse. The same unreadable line, and beside it a new mark like a wave (the plank has already happened) [R1-mystery-07]. The missionaries of 1324 asked "about women who are burned", and watched the Timkat lamp-blessing with a great deal of interest. |
| Closing cliffhanger | A rider from Aksum: the guardian of the sanctum of St Mary of Zion is **blind**, the inner chest is disturbed, and he "has been hiding it since before the rains" [R1-cont-08]. |
| Target length | 5,000 words |
| Source lines | 55–95 (61 cut as a duplicate, E014; 69–93 the missionaries; 77 "Dawit" becomes Amda Seyon, E034; 87); 63–67 (the politics as dialogue). |

### CHAPTER 5 — ZOSER
*Naples, and the road north — January 1326*

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Setting | Landfall at sunset; then a time-cut road (Benevento, Capua, Cassino); frost, snow, stares, shut doors. |
| Focal desire | Get Tamrat to Rome alive, and become the man Desta thought he was. |
| Conflict | Tamrat's cramps slow them. He has begun the great fast (*Hudade*) and refuses most food; Angeska and Zoser read it as piety [R1-cont-02]. Angeska begins her nightly word. At a town gate she writes *clavis* in the road dust with one finger and says "key". Zoser feels a tug of memory he cannot place, and copies the word into his psalter that night [R1-psych-05]. ## BACK MATTER — The Keeper's closing entry (about 150 words) and ornament

Head it *Rome, Yekatit ፲፰፻፹፱* [R1-mystery-08]. Draft for the writer to polish:

> *Item: a dossier of the order called the Silence-Keepers, concerning "Angeska, a Nilotic, black-magic born." Addressed to the Holy Father at the Vatican. Seal of fresh beeswax. The hand is fine, with a looped* et*. The facts are not. I have read an older copy that called her what she was. One of these was written in 1326. Item: a consistory of the year ፲፫፻፳, of which the record keeps no minute. — B.*

Followed by the ornament **አ**, alone on the page.

**This entry presents Scar I.** Its tells: the Vatican in 1326 (the papacy was at Avignon); "Urban or Clement" if the dossier names a pope; fresh beeswax on an "old" document; the looped ligature that matches the Clerk's hand; the contradiction with the dossier the reader saw in Ch.24. The year ፲፫፻፳ (1320 in the Ethiopian reckoning, which is Lent 1328 in the Julian) restores the year Ch.33's date line hides, and reads to a careless eye as an eight-year "error": a deliberate false Scar that teaches the reader to audit dates.
